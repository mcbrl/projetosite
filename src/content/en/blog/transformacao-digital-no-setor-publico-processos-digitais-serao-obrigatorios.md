Investment in innovation and [digital transformation](/en/digital-transformation-in-the-public-sector/) within the public sector continues to be a growing trend and has now also reached the legal field and its courts.

Starting March 1, 2022, Brazilian courts will no longer be able to distribute cases in physical form. The new rule mandates that all cases be 100% digital. This initiative was spearheaded by the CNJ (National Council of Justice).

Over the years, a large part of the complaints from the population who needed to resort to the courts to resolve some issue focused on the slowness of processes and lack of adequate infrastructure. This context served as a guide for the staff to carry out their work with more agility and assertiveness.

## The pandemic accelerated the Digital Transformation in the public sector.

Due to the COVID-19 pandemic, many sectors had to rethink their work model, and the legal field was no exception. In this context, many cases began to be accepted only digitally, eliminating the need for gatherings of lawyers, judges, and parties involved.

The president of the National Council of Justice (CNJ) and the Supreme Federal Court, Luiz Fux, spoke about the impact of the pandemic and how it accelerated the implementation of 100% digital processes. Check it out:

*"Measures deemed necessary due to health restrictions should be adopted permanently, either because they have proven effective or because they have brought cost-effectiveness and speed to the processes* . "

[Digital transformation](/en/Digital-transformation-in-the-public-sector%3A-federal-government-announces-new-guidelines-for-public-procurement-management./) in the public sector emerges as a solution to a series of challenges faced by the Judiciary during the Covid-19 pandemic, delivering technological solutions as the only means to continue providing judicial services in the country.

## CNJ sets deadline for implementation of digital processes.

Seeking to ensure the progress of modernization and establish 100% digital processes as a standard, the presidents of each court have until December 19, 2021, to present to the CNJ (National Council of Justice) a work plan containing detailed information about each court's work in digitizing processes.

As part of the requirement, the document must state the total number of existing physical files and their percentage, along with the migration schedule to digital format, the estimated total cost, and the deadlines for contracting the digitization service.

In some cases, there are different dates for implementation:

- Courts with a caseload of less than 5% of the total cases pending as of September 30 of this year have until the end of 2022 to complete the migration.

- Courts with rates between 5% and 20% have until the end of 2023.

- In the case of those with physical collections with rates between 20% and 40%, they can migrate to the digital process until the end of 2024.

- Courts with fees above 40% have a maximum deadline of December 31, 2025 to conclude the process.

## Physical processes only when technically impossible.

Since not all courts have access to a digital infrastructure, the CNJ (National Council of Justice) has established minor exceptions to ensure that the migration occurs fairly for all employees.

Therefore, the processing of new cases through physical means will only be permitted in cases of technical impossibility or proven urgency that requires it.
