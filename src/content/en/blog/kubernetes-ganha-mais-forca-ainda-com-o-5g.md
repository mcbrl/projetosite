As companies increasingly seek strategic data infrastructure and work with applications in distributed environments, [Kubernetes is gaining traction](/en/Why-has-Kubernetes-become-so-popular/) and prominence among IT leaders. This container orchestrator handles application scaling and management, as the demand for orchestrated solutions grows in the global market.

With the spread of 5G technology, Kubernetes becomes the natural solution for handling the containerization of core network functions, as well as for transferring data from various applications and services to a specific location.

In this article you will see how the open source orchestration system is perfect for performing the stateless management that 5G requires.

## Understanding the complexities of 5G

5G, also known as fifth-generation mobile telephony, is the latest technology for transferring data over networks to mobile devices. It is the successor to the well-known 4G network and its predecessors.

To clearly demonstrate the evolution, when mobile browsing was still in its infancy, 1G technology had a maximum speed of 2 Kbit/s, while 4G, so common among major operators, provides traffic of 1 Gbit/s. When we make the leap to 5G, data transfer speeds reach up to 10 Gbit/s.

The need for using Kubernetes becomes clear when we see that the latency in 4G is 60-98 milliseconds, while in 5G it will be reduced to less than 1 millisecond. Let's understand this in detail.

It took several years for telecommunications companies to transition from large-scale physical network deployments to virtual network functions (VNFs). This operational flexibility significantly increased the speed of deployment and management for the now-familiar 4G environments.

However, the 5G network is even larger in scope, requiring more towers and greater levels of flexibility to provide dynamic network provisioning and management.

So, what's the best way to manage this technology to benefit applications with high speeds and low latency?

## Kubernetes orchestrator and 5G complement each other.

To properly utilize 5G technology, companies need to modernize their core capabilities in terms of mobility, low latency, and large-scale data volumes. The architecture, operations, and scaling of the 5G network are quite different from previous generations of mobile networks.

**The technologies needed for 5G include:**

- Orchestration and management to automate service fulfillment and assurance;
- Efficient low-latency radio networks;
- Virtual network functions – cloud-based networks that dynamically share resources regardless of geographic location;
- Security and authentication policies;
- Edge computing to bring services closer to the network edge.

5G brings with it the possibility of solutions through Edge Computing, which dedicates processing and storage capacity to an environment closer to applications and local data sources, such as devices. This results in low latency, real-time information processing, scalability, and reduced costs.

To perform these actions, the 5G network and the application need to be in a different execution environment than the virtual network functions (VNFs) of previous generations, which run in virtual machine (VM)-based clouds.

Therefore, we can say that cloud-native applications run on container technology, controlled by an orchestrator!

## Telecommunications companies are opting for Kubernetes; understand why.

Kubernetes is gaining increasing traction as a solution for container orchestration. Nokia, for example, adopted Kubernetes back in 2018 and attributes much of its success in the 5G market to it.

Kubernetes [is a system for](/en/Gitops-from-automation-to-continuous-integration/) container management orchestration, responsible for managing the containerized infrastructure that will be needed to support 5G networks and related services.

By allowing the separation of infrastructure and application layers, Kubernetes supports a system with fewer dependencies, which can easily support the implementation of features in application layers.
