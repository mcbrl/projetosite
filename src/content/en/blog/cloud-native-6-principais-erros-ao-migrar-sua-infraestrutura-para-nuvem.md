Cloud native is an application development model optimized for cloud computing. It functions as a set of independent [microservices](/en/What-are-microservices/) that are encapsulated, facilitating migration to different environments.

The advantages of migrating [infrastructure to the cloud](/en/Transform-your-corporate-IT-into-an-innovation-enabler-with-cloud-native-technology./) are numerous, as it makes the process of building and operating applications more dynamic. Cloud-native architecture generally focuses on task and provisioning automation, containers, and their orchestration.

According to a Gartner survey [,](https://www.gartner.com/en) more than 70% of companies have already migrated at least part of their infrastructure to the public cloud. This trend will continue to increase as organizations plan to increase cloud adoption after the COVID-19 pandemic.

According to the report, it is predicted that by 2024, 60% of infrastructure and operations leaders will have exceeded their budget limit due to public cloud costs negatively impacting their local budgets.

With this in mind, many companies have already adopted this model, but not all have done it correctly. Therefore, we will show the most common mistakes that technology leaders make by not taking the necessary precautions.

Learn how to avoid some basic mistakes when migrating your company's infrastructure to the cloud.

## 1 – Team with no experience in Cloud Native

Choosing the right partner to perform the migration is a key point in a cloud infrastructure migration strategy. However, many leaders fail to adhere to technical criteria and select a migration partner based on cost rather than experience.

There are also cases where companies cut out the migration implementation partner and hand the project over to an internal team without the necessary technical expertise. Both choices generally lead to disastrous results, increasing costs in the long run.

If you choose to implement the migration internally, it's always important to select the ideal partner to train your team.

## 2 – Lack of criteria when migrating projects to the cloud.

Without the necessary guidance and knowledge, many leaders are driven by the impulse to quickly move their applications and workloads to the cloud. Consequently, they end up prioritizing the " *lift and shift* " approach, performing the cloud migration process without modifying the existing applications. For many on-premises applications, however, the best change for the business may be no change at all.

A great alternative is to deliver the application natively in the cloud or even replace it entirely with a [SaaS](/en/What-you-need-to-know-about-SaaS/) -based alternative.

## 3 – Evaluation of applications made prematurely

Evaluating the application in the early stages of a cloud-native migration project is critical because it helps determine the best migration approach for each application.

Driven by haste or simply a lack of knowledge, some companies fail to fully assess the workloads to be migrated, which can result in incomplete specifications of migration requirements and an increase in downstream scope.

## 4 – Implementing Cloud Native without planning a “landing zone”

It is essential to properly architect and implement the underlying cloud "landing zone" environments to which workloads are migrated. Failure to take this crucial step can lead to increased security and compliance costs.

The configuration for this zone should include a list containing:

- The account structure project;
- Federation for identity directories;
- Virtual private cloud network;
- Access control functions based on rule sets and infrastructure for monitoring, security, and configuration management.

Before starting your migration, verify that these settings are mapped and included in the scope of work. It's important to think through every detail, as this can save valuable time for fixing future errors.

## 5 – Failure to assess potential bottlenecks

It is very important to evaluate the application to discover potential bottlenecks after cloud-native migration. Not having a specialized team to discover and consider the interdependencies between the on-premises systems being migrated can lead to incorrect grouping and ordering of application migrations, cascading delays, and network performance issues.

Failure to perform these checks can completely delay the cloud implementation process. It is the role of leaders to map dependencies as part of the application assessment process and thus ensure timelines for best practices in the migration process.

## 6 – Not evaluating the cost of implementing Cloud Native applications

Certainly, evaluating costs is something every leader should do, but more important than simply performing this step is doing it correctly. Many fail to consider the indirect costs of the project, such as the operational costs of effectively operating in the public cloud, or the residual costs of unoccupied capacity in the data center. These are details that are often overlooked, but discarding your old infrastructure also generates costs, so take them into account.
