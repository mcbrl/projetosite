Until recently, customer loyalty depended primarily on their experience entering physical stores, and while that contact remains very important, years have passed, and a large portion of consumers have migrated to another universe: the internet. This has significantly changed the relationship of loyalty with some brands.

Today, consumers are visiting physical stores, banks, insurance companies, telecommunications companies, etc., less and less. They are buying the products or services they want through websites and applications. And depending on the experience consumers have through these channels, they will be more or less likely to become loyal to the brand. Below, we will discuss the relationship between application performance and customer loyalty in more detail. See:

## Current consumer behavior

The digital experience is undoubtedly as complex as the in-person one; some even say it's more complicated due to constant technological changes and the novelty of the subject. This is because, with each passing day, the level of consumer demand increases and their tolerance decreases. A small performance flaw in an application is enough for consumers to become impatient, abandon the purchase, and not contact the company again. Here are some factors that have contributed to this change in behavior:

- The need for communication between people and brands;
- ease provided by new technologies, especially tablets, smartphones and smart devices (Internet of Things);
- Offering better digital experiences and new sales channels, through simple and fast websites and applications – which are, however, very complex for developers;
- Companies are shifting to cloud solutions, enabling greater elasticity during traffic peaks.

## What exactly happens when a website or application crashes?

When a digital channel fails, the whole business fails. This phrase best summarizes what happens when a website or application experiences any kind of malfunction, even for milliseconds. This negatively impacts not only the user experience but also the company's revenue. After all, when application performance is poor, impatient consumers don't think twice and abandon the purchase, especially if it's an impulse buy.

One of the aspects that most concerns IT leaders is the speed of applications, which are becoming increasingly 'heavy' and complex as they offer more features to users. To overcome the speed challenge, many retailers, [such as Amazon](http://apmblog.dynatrace.com/2016/07/08/winter-holiday-july-amazon-preps-performance-prime-day/?_ga=1.91305565.697270813.1474022136) , are reducing the amount of content, simplifying pages, and using fewer external domains so that their websites can support the enormous number of users.

There are also other techniques that companies are adopting to increase the digital performance of their channels. One of them is storing certain static page elements in the user's browser cache, which makes it load much faster. Another is not sending emails with offers to a large number of consumers at the same time, as this can cause the site to crash.

These and many other strategies are studied and implemented by companies that don't want to see the digital performance of their websites and applications negatively impacted and wish to maintain customer loyalty. When these strategies fail, consumers become frustrated, abandon their purchases, and even share their negative experience on social media. This reduces the company's conversion rate and revenue, and damages its market image.

## What can be done to improve digital performance?

To improve the performance of a website or application and deliver a great experience to demanding consumers, IT managers need to implement excellent management of digital platforms, and we know this is not simple! This includes monitoring users on these platforms, simulating visits to websites and mobile applications, comparing their performance with that of the company's competitors, among many other actions.

It's clear that managers can't waste time collecting all this data manually, or by sampling it over specific periods. The best approach is to use a platform that automatically monitors digital performance, such as [Dynatrace](/en/dynatrace) or other market leaders like IBM, Oracle, AppDynamics, and so on. It's important to remember that performance improvement doesn't happen overnight, but it's possible to identify application flaws from week to week and then develop an action plan. In reality, it's a continuous study of users, and when this happens automatically, with clear indicators, it becomes easier to create an action plan.

## How to improve user performance

**Step 1: Identify the user who is having problems!**

Identifying users through user tags, location, app usage, or other indicators needs to be simple and easy for those responsible, and this can be a shared task between IT and business areas. Having the results available quickly, especially if the complaining user is still interacting with your application, can be crucial in determining whether you'll be able to win back the customer or lose them, potentially temporarily or permanently, depending on the problem! It's not good to be flooded with complaints on Twitter, Facebook, or Reclame Aqui (Brazilian consumer complaint website)!

![](/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Identify-the-user-in-need-600x192-1.png)

**Step 2: Understand the steps of the customer journey within your application!**

It's not only critical to identify the user experiencing the problem, but it's also important to know each session they've opened, whether across different applications, devices, or locations. Determining the session duration and the number of interactions during that period are fundamental to developing a clear understanding of the user's frustration, in order to identify which session caused the error they're complaining about. Actions like these also help maintain customer loyalty.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Learn-about-the-user-journeys-600x272-1.png)

**Step 3: Map the path to understand why the conversion didn't happen!**

As seen in the example below, not being able to use the [date picker](http://www.devmedia.com.br/calendario-em-jquery-criando-calendarios-com-datepicker/37458) when trying to book a trip is a major problem. Try doing it manually, without the date picker (which formats the date for the user); this can be a huge challenge considering all the date formats used around the world. The example shows how a simple JavaScript error can prevent the customer from progressing through the conversion path, not generating the conversion/sale and even leading that customer to file a complaint on some social network.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/User-session-with-JavaScript-error-600x337-1.png)

**Step 4: Collaborate with the Infrastructure and Systems team to share this information and fix the problem!**

We often write about technical issues that bring down applications/channels/websites. The example below shows how a snippet of third-party JavaScript caused a major impact on the application, not only causing problems for a single user, but also affecting the entire website, causing more than 100 errors per minute! This can happen with the call to a specific third-party API.

## Conclusion

Today, people are increasingly using new channels to communicate with you or your applications – whether through APIs or human interaction – but the big question is:

> Are you prepared to react quickly and appropriately when an application malfunctions?

The digital changes of the past have helped, and continue to help, in the evolution of monitoring solutions. Now they have a view of the relationship between systems and not just the hardware. Not long ago, the big news in IT was Application Performance Management (APM), but today it's not just about actionable insights for IT; the issue needs to be about the application of data centered on user behavior. The goal is to help entire companies effectively expand their businesses.
