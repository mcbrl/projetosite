Women are a minority in the world of technology, and that's nothing new. However, more and more women are conquering their space in the market, which leads us to reflect on the **challenges and opportunities** faced by women IT professionals and how to overcome them.

Organizations around the world have been creating measures to overcome barriers that prevent, for example, equal job opportunities and pay between men and women.

In Europe, Women in Tech does interesting work encouraging the hiring and training of women in the IT market. In Brazil, there are similar initiatives like Programaria that also bridge this gap between women.

According to the IBGE's National Household Sample Survey (PNAD/IBGE), women represent only 20% of the more than 580 professionals working in the IT field in Brazil. This is still far from ideal and helps explain why we see so few women working in technology companies.

On this International Women's Day, we want to honor all women, but we must give special recognition to women in the world of technology, who face the challenges of conquering their space in a predominantly male market and help transform the world through technology.

## Women in the Technology Market

The fight for women's rights in the workplace is an old one, and much progress has been made in the last 100 years. In general, women have fought to gain respect, space, and opportunities in the job market, although many of the achievements of remarkable women still do not receive the credit they deserve.

An interesting film that addresses this theme is "Hidden Figures," which tells the story of three strong women who were essential to the success of the mission that took man to the moon. If you haven't seen this film yet, it's worth checking out.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/estrelas-alm-do-tempo.png)

Despite the progress, there is still a long way to go. Recently, a study released by the UN (United Nations) revealed that in Brazil, the percentage of women occupying places in technology courses does not reach 20% of the total places offered.

This statistic gives us a clue as to why women are an absolute minority in IT positions. And it leads us to another reflection: why is the percentage of women applying for technology courses so low compared to that of men?

From a sociological perspective, we can understand that culturally, the vast majority of women are encouraged to develop human and creative skills, to the detriment of fostering logical reasoning and discovering the mechanisms that make things work. On the other hand, technology tends to be part of the male universe from a very young age.

In the early days of information technology, the typical IT professional was a kind of programmer soldier, working in isolation for long hours with little human interaction. It was basically a programmer, a computer, and, of course, a mug of coffee!

However, the world has changed, and there is an increasing intersection between technology and business, requiring IT professionals to have social skills to enable them to understand problems, propose and discuss solutions.

The good news for us women is that digital transformation is driving all sectors of the company to be interconnected. Working in isolation no longer makes sense, since technology is present in all areas of the business.

In parallel, campaigns against harassment and discrimination have helped to create much more respectful professional environments for women to work, contribute, and grow within companies.

A very interesting series that helps us understand how much women had to fight to gain respect and space in the market is "Las chicas del cable" (in English, "The Cable Girls"), produced by Netflix. In this drama, set in the 20s, four women struggle and go through personal and professional dramas to conquer their place in a telephone company.

## Movements in support of women in IT.

The internet has given a voice to those who previously had no expression and has provided opportunities for communities to form around a common cause. This is the case with movements and groups advocating for women in IT.

In Brazil, the [PrograMaria](https://www.programaria.org/) Institution does inspiring work empowering women and encouraging their entry into the world of technology. With the motto "Learn to program and transform the world," the institution promotes online content, events, and courses, as well as highlighting the achievements and contributions of women to the technological evolution of the world. [It's worth checking out this article about women who have made history in technology.](https://www.programaria.org/mulheres-que-fizeram-historia-na-tecnologia/)

In Sweden, one of the global benchmark countries in terms of gender equality and pay equity, an organization called [Women in Tech](https://www.womenintech.se/) does interesting work to encourage the hiring of women IT professionals. Annually, WIT holds an event called [WIThired](https://www.womenintech.se/withired/) , which involves a series of lectures related to the technology field, and in addition to highlighting companies committed to gender equality in the technology sector, it also facilitates networking between professionals in the field and these companies.

## Challenges and opportunities for female IT professionals

Here at Vertigo Tecnologia, we have a large team of women working across various areas of the business. And, of course, IT is no exception. We interviewed some of our female employees and thought it would be interesting to share their experiences in the IT market.

We asked **Cristiane Portugal** , our project manager, if she had experienced any prejudice for being a woman.

Cristiane says she never noticed prejudice for being a woman in the companies where she worked. For her, the IT world is predominantly male, out of necessity. Men have always been the majority in activities that required more focus and attention and less interaction. In Brazil, in the 80s and 90s, when technological advances began to drive the emergence of academic fields and sectors focused on IT, it seemed very natural that male skills and market demand itself would create the perfect match.

In Cristiane's opinion, the biggest challenge for a woman to gain her place in the IT market is to present herself as a professional who can both be focused and interact with other people, in addition to developing her attributes and qualities, especially because she is a woman.

We also spoke with **Emilia Yamabe** , our project coordinator. She told us that she became interested in the technology field because it was new and sparked her curiosity. So she enrolled in the vocational IT course offered by the school in high school.

In his opinion, there are two main factors contributing to the male predominance in the IT market: the technology field requires a great deal of dedication due to the rapid pace of technological advancements, as well as long working hours and a significant amount of study time.

According to her, many women often work a **"double shift** ," taking care of the house and children, which means they have less time to dedicate to studies and professional development than men. Furthermore, many women end up abandoning their careers due to the pressure exerted by this double shift.

Emilia never perceived any kind of prejudice from the companies she worked for. For her, the main challenge for an IT professional is being **much more organized than the average man** , in order to stay updated, specialize further, and handle the other roles she needs to fulfill.

## A message from our IT professionals.

Finally, we would like to conclude with a message from our collaborators, who were interviewed specifically for this article, for those women who are thinking about entering the IT market.

> **"Believe that we women are just as capable as men, study hard and dedicate yourself to your profession."**

##### Emilia Yamabe, 57 years old. Project Coordinator at Vertigo Tecnologia.

> “ **Be yourself. Being a woman, you already have the advantage of seeing things differently than men. And they need us, always!”**

##### Cristiane Portugal, 40 years old. Project manager at Vertigo Tecnologia.
