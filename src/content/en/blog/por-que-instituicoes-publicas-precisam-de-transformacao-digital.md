Digital transformation is not just about information technology. It is, in fact, a change in institutional culture focused on agility, consistent deliveries, and customer experience. Just as in other sectors of the economy, it is essential that innovation and technology are combined to improve public policies. In public institutions, the [Transformation](/en/Traditional-IT-can-be-an-enemy-of-digital-transformation./) It makes services faster and more economical.
Many managers are investing in digital strategies to make government work more digitized and aligned with citizens' demands. This makes the daily lives of all professionals working in the area more practical and efficient. However, unfortunately, this is not yet the majority in public offices. Many still depend on outdated legacy systems, which are expensive to maintain and delay organizations when it comes to improving services. It is essential that public managers understand that digital culture is a change in the way the government operates, understands projects, and reports results.
As the population grows, so does the demand for government services. IT departments are under increasing pressure to find new ways to reduce costs, increase security, and at the same time provide higher quality services to citizens.
With budget cuts being made at all levels, the public sector cannot afford to continue with legacy infrastructures. It needs to become digitally enabled and enhanced.
With Digital Transformation came the offering of digital services on a large scale, where citizens can schedule appointments, verify documents, and request authorizations quickly, both through apps and in person at local offices. Requests are met with shorter turnaround times, allowing more people to be served using fewer resources.
Digital transformation is a central and strategic theme for the development of nations and has occupied a prominent position on the agendas of governments and international organizations. In this article, we present the main barriers and benefits of transformation for this sector. Enjoy your reading!

## Challenges

### Excessive bureaucracy in legacy systems

The attachment to legacy systems is the main barrier for public institutions on their journey to Digital Transformation. Maintenance is expensive, and the various hand-coded connections between systems become obsolete due to the current need to capture, store, access, and process data.
Modernizing these ecosystems requires commitment from IT departments, as it's a major structural change. However, it will still be less work than the time and effort needed just to continue working with legacy systems.

### Provision of financial resources

Ongoing budget cuts to IT departments are also a significant barrier. Creating integrated environments and adopting modern approaches – whether cloud, Artificial Intelligence, or IoT – requires a substantial investment, which, during times of budget reduction, is not seen as a priority. 
However, the gains from process optimization, the associated costs, and becoming more efficient and productive offset the initial costs.

### Absence of a culture of experimentation

Digital transformation is not something that happens overnight; it requires a strategic decision and demands planning, resources, and clear goals.
In this case, the fear of making mistakes prevents public institutions from more actively pursuing transformation. After all, this fear comes in many forms, including political pressure, public outcry, media shame, etc. 
One way to control this risk is to start with small, manageable projects before increasing investment. Ideally, digital modernization should happen first in new technology ventures, so that existing software is updated only afterwards.

### Lack of specific legislation

Legislative changes should be promoted. In this way, public officials will have more freedom to make investments that generate positive impacts and bring IT to various areas of the public sector. Furthermore, good communication is crucial.
The legal framework currently in place in Brazil for public procurement and technology contracts, in a way, makes the process relatively slow, and therefore out of sync with the speed of technological change that the world and society demand.

## Improvements

### Automation

By integrating their systems and applications, IT departments will be able to get rid of outdated and time-consuming processes, while automating and optimizing others.
Reducing the number of manual processes that have been built up over time frees up the team to focus on high-priority tasks: reducing the risk of errors in manual data processing, improving the Customer Experience and, as a result, obtaining a better evaluation from citizens regarding the services offered.

### Integrated service

[Omnichannel](/en/initiating-its-omnichannel-strategy/) This isn't a prerogative exclusive to the retail, financial, or insurance sectors. Increasingly, citizens expect to be served through various channels, both online and offline, with the same attention and availability they receive from their favorite brand.
The key characteristic for this strategy to be successful is the ability to offer the same Customer Experience across all channels. This can only be achieved by operating a fully integrated environment with a unified view of information. 
The introduction of Artificial Intelligence to automate communication with citizens is another technology-enabled step to improve services while reducing effort and cost.

### Intelligence

For any public institution, it is important to have a good understanding of the people, businesses, and communities within its area or region, in order to offer better and more targeted services.
Unfortunately, this is not easy, as the data is locked into a variety of legacy systems, often in a format that makes it difficult to extract, process, and analyze.
Depending on the data, this problem can be solved with a system integration and API management platform to connect the different systems, allowing users to obtain better, more accurate, and inclusive insights.
Another important reason is for [combat fraud](/en/What-are-the-cloud-security-challenges-for-2021/)With Artificial Intelligence, it is possible to detect anomalies such as similar handwriting, duplicate phone numbers, location, etc.

### Safety

As we have recently seen with the unauthorized withdrawals from FGTS (Brazilian severance pay fund) and Emergency Aid accounts, and the massive data leak from Serasa (a Brazilian credit bureau), the number of cyberattacks is increasing. With attackers becoming increasingly sophisticated and audacious, public institutions need to be prepared for potential security breaches.
A cyberattack can be extremely disruptive to day-to-day operations, while data breaches can be extremely dangerous, as the government holds confidential data with a high potential for disaster should a leak occur.
Currently, intelligent tools and methods are needed to prevent, detect, and terminate cyberattacks in real time. Machine Learning and algorithmic techniques can analyze logs, system reports, network packets, flows, and files to uncover deviations from established behavioral patterns, highlighting those that may pose a threat to the organization's security.
