Closely linked to DevOps practices, Kubernetes – or K8S, as it is also known – functions as an open-source container orchestration system for automating the deployment, scaling, and management of applications. In recent years, the platform has achieved a high level of popularity due to the growth of companies that have migrated their technologies to the cloud.

In this article, you will understand what is behind the rise of Kubernetes and how adopting it is an excellent way to start your project and avoid future rework. Here, we will see how to obtain information directly from the community of developers and enthusiasts of this popular orchestrator, which is only increasing its number of followers.

## The rise of Kubernetes

You've probably heard a lot about Kubernetes on tech websites and forums, but what does it actually mean and how did it come about? Before we delve into this topic, let's understand its origins.

In 2014, Google standardized its method of managing containers within its internal projects. It's no surprise that the giant search company has become a benchmark in the development of popular APIs and one of the most powerful brands in the world. Not bad, right? An orchestration system conceived by a leader in solid IT projects.

Isolating code in containers is a necessary practice for optimized application development. [Containers](/en/Containers-vs.-Virtual-Machines/) isolate your code in a modular and consistent way throughout its deployment across development, testing, and production environments.

In 2015, the system was handed over to the Cloud Native Computing Foundation (CNCF) and is still maintained by the Kubernetes community. Being open-source software, it receives many contributions from the community and also from major players such as: Amazon, Linux Foundation, Microsoft (today the creator of Kubernetes works at MS Azure), Google, Red Hat (which IBM acquired in 2018), and IBM itself.

## What is the best time to use it? 

If you started a new application project today, take the time to make it *cloud-native* and use Kubernetes from the very beginning of development.

This entire process ensures that your development pipelines gain more agility and robustness than if they were in the testing phase in traditional environments. 

Organizing your code within containers, and having a tool capable of organizing each of these modules, is also an important practice for the security of your project, as it isolates serious problems and flaws for bug fixes and code updates without necessarily interrupting the operation of your application.

Setting up Kubernetes is less difficult than you might think. Think of it this way: it's certainly less effort than rewriting parts of your application later to support containerization. Make this a native practice from the start!

## What are the benefits of adopting this orchestrator?

You will experience increased development speed, and your team will have a fast and secure framework to work with. 

Container orchestration with applications through PODs, which are essentially a collection of multiple containers. These typically consist of one container or several other containers linked together. This allows for automated control and management of the clusters.

By using Kubernetes, it's possible to separate the responsibilities of Development and Operations.

Flexibility to deploy applications anywhere, which is practically mandatory in today's world.

Automatically adjust the size and number of nodes in the cluster, allowing for scaling based on demand.

Freedom in choosing the programming language, which also provides flexibility when configuring it.

## Why is Kubernetes still growing?

We've already mentioned several benefits your development team will gain by adopting Kubernetes as an orchestrator for your applications, but did your success come out of nowhere? Not at all!

For years, since its launch, Kubernetes has had updates on average every four months, meaning that every year there are about three major releases with new features that shake up the world of application containerization. 

The number of new features being introduced continues at full speed, and contributions show no signs of slowing down, even during these challenging times, as indicated by the [Kubernetes project's Github](https://github.com/kubernetes) activity.

The new features allow cluster operators more flexibility when running a variety of different workloads, which ensures software engineers also love having more control to deploy their applications directly into production environments.

Another positive aspect that increases Kubernetes' popularity is its very active community. Kubernetes has been part of the Linux Foundation since 2015, when it reached version 1.0: the Cloud Native Computing Foundation.

There is also a large [SIG](https://github.com/kubernetes/community/blob/master/sig-list.md) (special interest groups) within the community that encompasses discussions and contributions from different areas of Kubernetes as the project receives updates. All of this happens collaboratively, as the groups add new features and make the environment user-friendly.

The Cloud Native Foundation has a Technical Oversight Committee that, along with its SIGs, reviews new and existing projects, as well as organizing important events to present new developments to the community.
