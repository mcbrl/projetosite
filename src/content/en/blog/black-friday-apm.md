Some holidays are so important to retail that they put the company's entire IT operation to the test. Among them, **Black Friday** represents one of the most important dates of the year, leaving many IT teams in "war mode" to ensure the availability and operation of all systems. This scenario is even more challenging and maddening when the company has a significant e-commerce presence.

Let's talk about the importance of application performance monitoring to ensure your systems can handle Black Friday at full capacity, without interruptions, with scalability and high availability.

Read this article to the end and see how to avoid stressful **War Rooms** during Black Friday.

## The importance of planning

Planning a campaign the size of a Black Friday usually involves many areas. In addition to the Marketing and Sales team, the company's IT department needs to be involved to design the necessary infrastructure so that the company's virtual environments can handle the high volume of traffic and ensure that each application functions as expected.

Many retailers derive a significant portion of their revenue from online sales. Therefore, bugs, outages, errors, and other setbacks can represent substantial losses for the company if they occur during periods of high traffic volume.

It turns out that, during Black Friday periods, many e-commerce sites receive a volume of traffic hundreds of times, and in some cases thousands of times, higher than usual. This exponentially increases the impact on the business caused by any downtime. Planning for the event can greatly help the company anticipate potential problems and mitigate operational risks.

It's not enough for a company's online environments to be prepared to handle the projected volume of traffic. They also need to be able to act quickly in the face of any potential failures and prevent the user from noticing them.

In this sense, companies that have already migrated their systems to the cloud and are already working with containers have a huge competitive advantage.

## The Cloud as a competitive advantage for online retail

One of the advantages of migrating your systems to the cloud is the ability to automatically scale the provisioning of machines to support peak access times.

Through automatic, pre-programmed load management, machine overload is avoided, reducing the likelihood of server crashes due to access volume.

Adopting ***Serverless*** enables dynamic management and balancing of resource allocation and machine consumption, with costs based on the actual amount of resources consumed by applications.

In this scenario, the company does not need to pay year-round for the infrastructure required to handle the one-off traffic from Black Friday-style campaigns.

Several best practices are recommended to ensure the performance of your systems during peak access days. We have listed some of them here.

## Best practices to avoid War Rooms during Black Friday.

### **1\. ALWAYS TEST EVERYTHING**

Anyone who is even slightly organized runs tests to ensure their websites can handle Black Friday traffic. However, they usually don't test everything. It's necessary to anticipate potential problems across all channels, from the mobile app to the website. And most importantly, to test a "Plan B" in case something goes wrong.

### **2- MAKE YOUR WEBSITE LIGHTWEIGHT**

A good practice for these peak access days is to adjust your portal to lighter versions. This involves minimizing heavy content, such as high-resolution videos and images, which slow down the user experience. This allows you to improve performance and minimize the cost of providing this content.

### **3- KNOW YOUR CUSTOMERS**

It's important for retailers to truly know their audience, understanding which channels they use most and for what purpose. What is the user's buying journey? How do they access information? Are apps used via Wi-Fi or mobile data? Are purchases made via credit card or bank slip? The depth of this knowledge can truly help optimize the user experience and maximize campaign results.

###  **4\. HAVE A TEST PLAN**

Many companies adopt a trial plan to test the performance of their environments, and also to distribute traffic over time. This is why large retailers are seen anticipating Black Friday, or extending it, to avoid handling all the traffic in a single day.

## Real-time monitoring

No matter how much planning and preparation goes into an event like Black Friday, many things can go wrong at the crucial moment. Hacker attacks, application command errors, system bugs, and other problems that can lead your campaign to complete failure are not uncommon.

In this sense, monitoring and managing the performance and availability of your applications in real time is essential to ensure that IT has the ability to anticipate problems, identify them accurately, and correct them quickly.

For this purpose, the type of market solution that aims to fulfill this role is known as Application Performance Monitoring (APM).

An APM (Application Performance Manager) has the function of providing accurate, complete diagnostics of all your applications, in real time, from the front-end layers to the last line of code.

With this, IT gains up to 3 times in productivity, making it easier to identify problems, attacks and resolve bugs, generate reports, etc.

This type of solution allows you to monitor application performance in terms of load and response times, conversion rates, and shopping cart abandonment, as well as essential technical and business indicators for an e-commerce business.

Customer loyalty when shopping online is often tied to price and experience. If a customer tries to complete a checkout process in their shopping cart and is unable to, they will likely look for the same product on a competitor's website. If that competitor offers a better experience, you are likely to lose the sale and, perhaps, the customer.
