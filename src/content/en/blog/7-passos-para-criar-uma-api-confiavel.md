The creation of **APIs (Application Programming Interfaces)** is becoming increasingly important for generating and scaling new businesses. It's important for both IT and business professionals. This is because, whether in your professional or personal life, you most likely consume several APIs daily. In this scenario, having problematic, confusing, or unintuitive interfaces (both from a programming and graphical perspective) can ultimately harm the user experience and, consequently, the usability and scalability of this type of service, not to mention reputation. In today's post, we'll share 7 tips on how to build a reliable API and do a good job for your client. Also read: [Public or private API, how to choose?](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/api-publica-ou-privada-como-saber/)

![](/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/Open-APIs-v5.png)

## **1\. Research your target audience.**

The first fundamental step in creating APIs is to think: "What experience do I want my target audience to have?". This is because the interface must be differentiated depending on the type of audience that will interact with your API; that is, developers, digital marketing professionals, and lawyers want and need different information. Each audience has its own demands, advantages, and disadvantages, and this must be taken into account during creation. I know, it seems obvious, but this doesn't always happen!

## **2\. Technology**

This is a technical aspect and can be considered the central point. It's necessary to think about how your service will be accessed by the user. This is extremely important because it defines how your audience will access the developed product. In this regard, it's necessary to define:

- Protocols: How your files will be accessed: REST, SOAP, Apache Thrift, etc.
- Formats: Your API should support the return standards most relevant to users according to their consumption patterns (for example, if they are more accustomed to JSON, XML, etc.). In some cases, for companies or government agencies with open API initiatives, this can be done using CSV or PDF formats.
- Hypermedia: In some cases, it is worth considering including controls that allow for intelligent navigation, such as pagination, the use of tags, etc.

## **3\. Design**

An API cannot have a poor design. Its aesthetics must also be considered, so that its use is attractive, otherwise it may drive the user away. Always think: what kind of software, website, or application made me never use it again? Was the design good or bad? Probably one of the main reasons for abandonment was a strange, aesthetically unappealing interface.
Another point to consider is innovation. More of the same usually becomes outdated, bores the public, and fails to attract attention. The developer must be creative and functional at the same time. Seeking help from a designer is a good idea to boost your project. Remember that an innovative interface doesn't mean cluttered. It's possible to create clean and impactful visuals that are very attractive to the user.

## **4\. External tests**

As mentioned earlier, it's necessary to research the target audience and understand their needs. Having done that, the development process requires testing your API before launching it, as it may contain errors that could compromise its image.
A good way to conduct these tests is to launch a beta version, fostering and monitoring feedback through forums. This allows for quick error correction and also builds trust with the public. Another way is to hire companies that perform A/B testing, where specialized professionals will thoroughly examine the product, looking for usability issues, bugs, and security flaws, in order to improve the experience before releasing it to the end user. Take the opportunity to read about... [5 benefits of API virtualization!](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/5-beneficios-da-virtualizacao-para-testar-sua-api/)

## **5\. Documentation/Changelogs**

To keep users happy with your API, it's important to maintain an up-to-date and functional changelog. If there are any problems, users will be notified immediately upon visiting your website. Another option is to create an email alert for registered users. This demonstrates transparency and concern, building credibility for your API. Remember that user experience always comes first, whether for the general public or employees of corporate groups.
Another way to improve API management is to create user-accessible homepages or databases containing FAQs, common errors, and their solutions. After all, users don't want to struggle when using any product; they want something that simplifies their experience.

## **6\. Speed**

Slow interfaces and/or interfaces prone to freezing or crashing severely impair the user experience. Regardless of whether the final product will be used for work or leisure, there can be no flaws in this experience. This is because, in an increasingly competitive market, another company can quickly create faster and more functional software/applications/websites, leaving yours behind. Therefore, prioritize the speed and functionality of the API. Often, this can be achieved simply with a simpler interface. In many cases, less is more.

## **7\. Maintenance and Support**

Consistent API documentation is essential, including its most relevant information and errors. Furthermore, constant maintenance and support of the API are crucial, always fostering close relationships with the user community to encourage interaction between users and the developer, as well as among users themselves, enabling mutual support. Addressing common errors and finding solutions is also important.

## **Conclusion**

By following the tips above, you'll have a good guide on how to create a secure and stable API.
Yes, there are factors that should always be monitored (speed, following good naming principles, etc.), but there are many others that will vary considerably, especially in relation to competitors. When you start to get a clear answer from the steps mentioned earlier, you will begin to have a picture of what your API should be like and what benefit it will bring to those who use it.
