The new IT operating model employs an approach whereby Central IT drives the production of reusable assets. And more importantly, it enables the consumption of these assets by teams within business segments to deliver digital initiatives. 

This model addresses the principles of production and consumption, but what is truly new is what is being produced and what is being consumed.

This new IT operating model is based on business principles that have existed for decades, but are only now being frequently applied in the delivery of corporate IT projects. An example of this is the *franchising model.*

Before discussing the topic, we would like to emphasize that this article is part of a series of articles. The focal point of this series is to present the main points and approaches for adopting an effective IT operational model, with the aim of eliminating the delivery gap.

Below we will discuss in more detail a proposed structural redesign of IT. To do this, we will shed light on the following question: how can we reassess the model used and how is this transformation possible?

## **THE FOUNDATIONS OF THE FRANCHISE MODEL**

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/1-768x512-1.jpg)
We'll draw a parallel with a world-renowned company – McDonald's. This business, in many respects, pioneered the franchise business model.

Your parent company has essential assets such as recipes, retail layouts, and marketing offerings. These assets are packaged for reuse by your franchisees, who in turn execute the plans autonomously.
This encourages innovation at the business's extremes, making reusable assets and intellectual property available for the individual consumption of each franchisee.

But how does this work? The company empowers its franchisees or suppliers by educating them on best practices and processes. Furthermore, it promotes quality both through established and demanding standards and through continuous customer feedback. 

Finally, the parent company retains control over the assets and has visibility into the entire operation, bringing innovation and autonomy to its franchisees.

This business model has two distinct advantages. First, the franchise model has allowed McDonald's to expand globally at a much faster pace than it ever could have otherwise achieved. 

Secondly, although all McDonald's restaurants worldwide are undeniably part of the same business, franchisees in local markets can innovate according to their own needs. 

A classic example of this is the permission to vary menu items according to the tastes and customs of a particular place. This increases the likelihood of the business succeeding in that region. 

In other words, in addition to standardizing the brand and its quality worldwide, this model also allows for business expansion based on knowledge of local aspects where the franchise is located.

At this point, you're probably wondering: what does this model have to do with IT? 

Well, for IT organizations to reach the level required by the era of digital disruption, rethinking their structure and adopting the principles used by franchises would be a competitive advantage.

### **The "secret weapon" of franchises that IT teams should be watching.**

 
Franchise companies have two main reusable assets. They are: **what"**– which refers to the principles of organization and the “**, the**"—which is how franchisors unlock and package ready-to-use models for their franchisees. This is all in order to boost the..." **innovation scale**, while maintaining quality and governance in accordance with required standards.

In the new operational model, central IT needs to transform its function into one that frees up assets and enables consumption by business segments and developers across the organization. This achieves scalability, fostering innovation and speed, without sacrificing control.

In this regard, some questions arise regarding the new IT operating model.
Stay with us to understand how all this works in practice.

### **How should the new IT operating model function within the organization?**

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/3a.jpg)
The idea is that, following the model of a franchise business, central IT should foster innovation throughout the organization. 
To do this, she must create **reusable assets** and to enable process autonomy, instead of working on and delivering entire projects independently.

By leveraging resources and capabilities outside of core IT, organizations can drive genuine change in the speed and ability to deliver.

### **How does IT scale a new operating model?**

Thinking about a new operational model implies that a significant change in the way IT operates is necessary beforehand. 

Instead of simply delivering projects, central IT should spend its time creating reusable assets and enabling the rest of the organization to use them.

But how does this actually happen in practice? 

There are three things that technology leaders need to consider when working on scaling the IT organization. 
At first glance, these changes may seem laborious, but the results are very profitable. In the future, the company will see an increase in productivity and innovation, with a consequent increase in profitability, as:  

- Change your delivery model.
- Change your approach to integration.
- It changes the way your IT team is organized.

With the answers to these questions and the priorities of the IT teams established, it is time to begin the transition from the existing delivery model, as we will see in more detail below.

### **Changing the delivery model from production to production + consumption.**

As we now know, core IT needs to move away from trying to deliver all IT projects and start building reusable assets. 

This will allow company departments to produce some of their own projects – giving them autonomy instead of waiting for IT delivery. The key to this strategy is to emphasize consumption as much as production.
Traditional IT approaches (such as SOA – Service-Oriented ArchitectureThey focus exclusively on production for project delivery. 

In the new operational model, IT will shift its mindset to focus on producing assets intended to be consumed by others across the enterprise. To achieve this, resources need to be discoverable.

Furthermore, developers need to be able to make them available in projects. This should be done through a visible and usable repository that includes documentation with best practices.
 

### **The production + consumption model**

One of the best aspects of this new operating model is this: the assets most useful to the rest of the company will rise to the top thanks to the active feedback from the consumption model. 
This will happen in conjunction with usage metrics to inform the production model. The most useful reusable assets become the basis for various project products, which can then be completed more quickly.
![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/2-1-768x514-1.jpg)
**mike hamilton**, **MuleSoft IT Director**, explains how this works in practice:

First, he showed us where his company started: *“We took the entire list of business process projects that needed automation and decided to break them down into parts, then group the pieces into common services.”*
As a result, they discovered that many of the projects had many needs in common: *"One key common microservice that emerged was related to sending notifications via email, Slack, or SMS."*

From there, Mike explains that "Since most integrations had this requirement, the 'notifications' microservice was quickly created." Hamilton further clarified the function of this microservice. *"It's about being able to post information about who to notify, how to notify them, and the content to send the notification and then deliver it to the intended recipients."*
It concludes, therefore, that by creating this as a service, each project can leverage the microservice's capabilities, instead of implementing notifications as part of each individual project. 
*"This saves development time on each project and provides us with a consistent way to implement notifications across all our work."*"That's all," concludes the IT director of MuleSoft.

As we saw in Mike Hamilton's presentation, discovering what all the delivery requests had in common allowed the MuleSoft IT team to develop a useful and reusable feature. 
This would save the MuleSoft team, and any other team that had access to it, from expending time and effort on delivering any project that required notifications. The model generated a reusable active consumer product and was then used to accelerate production.
