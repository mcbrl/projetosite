International Women's Day is celebrated on March 8th, and this commemorative date symbolizes women's struggle for equality in a structurally patriarchal society. Furthermore, it represents a milestone in the feminist fight for respect, equal working conditions to those of men, and the fight against violence against women.

**We asked some of our female employees what it's like to be a woman in the tech market, which is predominantly male, as well as the biggest challenges they faced in reaching their current career positions.**

**Finally, we also asked them to leave a message for all women who wish to enter the job market.**

Below are some of the stories that represent the women of Vertigo and so many others that fill us with pride.

## Testimonials from Vertigo collaborators

**![](/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/claudia-meire-235x300.jpg)Name: Claudia Mere** 

**Role: Project Coordinator** 

**Area: Delivery** 

"Being a leader today means being engaged with values ​​and goals that have a positive impact on the company's business and on society."

The B-Corps movement and B Certification are initiatives that meet the highest standards of social and environmental performance, transparency criteria, and legal accountability in balancing profit and purpose. Certification as a B Corp is a way to recognize the qualities of a product or service, and it's an assessment that measures and tracks companies' progress towards generating a positive impact in five dimensions: Governance, Workers, Customers, Community, and Environment.

This shows that, more than ever, the market is looking for solutions and services from companies that combat discrimination and deliver value with a humanistic vision, free from certain characteristics and prejudices. A company that cares about its people, customers, and the environment is more likely to outperform its peers than one that does not invest in and is not aligned with this business vision.

Despite the technology field being predominantly led by men, I feel that my role is to be a protagonist, to be aware of the potential and recognize the opportunities, and, with responsibility and humility, continue down the path I have chosen.

Here at Vertigo, we live the company's values ​​every day, working with excellence to generate a positive impact for our clients and society, with respect and empowerment. Vertigo promotes a safe and peaceful work environment and gives us the tools for our personal and professional growth.

Despite the obstacles, there is no shortage of examples of women who have faced the challenge and prove that women belong in IT. Examples include Marissa Mayer, a computer scientist who held strategic leadership positions at Google and Yahoo!; Ginni Rometty, CEO of IBM; Sheryl Sandberg, COO of Facebook; and Augusta Ada Byron, Countess of Lovelace, who was one of those responsible for the computer we have today.

We all help to create a positive scenario for the future. Technology is becoming increasingly interactive, more communicative, and more focused on interpersonal relationships, and women have a lot to contribute in these areas. This market is also a female market!

IT is still a predominantly male field, especially in senior positions. Women represent only 20% of technology professionals in Brazil, even though in the last five years the growth of women in the IT field has increased by 60%, going from about 28 to almost 45 in 2019 (Caged).

In different groups and organizations, friction and discomfort still exist, and situations such as gaslighting, mansplaining, manterrupting, and bropriating can be encountered daily. If we add to this the wage inequality (with offers well below the standard even with acquired experience) and ageism (which occurs earlier for women), we see that there is still a long way to go, but many companies are participating in initiatives and awareness forums for improving internal processes.

"My journey in IT began somewhat by chance. Exactly 20 years ago, I was hired as a telemarketing operator at a technology company. Initially, I used technology only as a work tool, but it wasn't long before I became fascinated by this area and sought more knowledge!"

I gradually rose through the ranks to leadership positions, and at one point I found myself leading over 1500 employees.

In a market where most professionals are men, I believe that the magic formula for standing out includes training, dedication, good communication, and empathy!

If you, the reader of this message, are a woman interested in entering this market, don't be afraid; technology is also our place.”

**Area: Delivery** 

"Being a woman is already a great challenge, but being a leader in the technology market makes it even greater."

My biggest challenge came when I was a recent graduate, when I had to take over the management of a project in its early stages. During that period, I faced many challenges such as conflicts within the project, with the team, and with the clients.

My greatest wish is that we women can reach high-level positions in the technology market, that we can break paradigms and prejudices more and more each day.”

**![](/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Lizandra-253x300.jpg)Name: Lizandra Duarte** 

**Role: Project Coordinator**

**Area: Delivery** 

"It doesn't surprise me that professions related to information technology are predominantly male. In my view, this relationship is entirely linked to the culture in which women have historically driven societal progress in politics, science, sports, the arts, and other areas. In general, women have fought to gain respect, space, and create opportunities in this broad job market focused on information technology."

The technology field requires a great deal of dedication due to the rapid pace of technological advancements, as well as long working hours and significant study time. This makes managing time a major challenge amidst all the responsibilities that we women undertake.

Briefly analyzing my journey in this challenge, the greatest difficulty I faced was insecurity, the fear of not succeeding, the fear of not meeting the team's expectations—yes, the team's expectations. I don't recall experiencing any prejudice for being a woman in the companies where I worked. My goal has always been to be recognized as a good professional; I only compete with myself, my fears, my insecurities, my frustrations, and my dreams.

I have always sought to have clear goals in my career, focusing on becoming a better, more capable professional. How? By recognizing and visualizing the mistakes in the projects I worked on, always mitigating each one of them, and most importantly, applying the learning to subsequent projects and challenges—in other words, learning more and more. I must also emphasize the importance of partnerships between teams, peers, suppliers, and project owners; this partnership was essential for my growth and evolution.

Perhaps not all of our female colleagues have had the same "luck," because we know the magnitude of the challenge and prejudices of being a woman and working in this field. We can also see situations from different perspectives and think that nothing is changing, or look ahead and to the sides and see how many steps have been taken and how many steps depend only on ourselves.

When we act outside our comfort zone, growth, satisfaction, and well-being certainly attract the possibility of new opportunities.

**![](/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Priscila-Madeira-300x220.jpg)Name: Priscila Madeira** 

**Job Title: Director of Operations**

**Area: Delivery** 

"Since the beginning of my career, I've always worked in predominantly male environments. I started working in the technology market more than 20 years ago and have focused from the start on demonstrating my competence. This has earned me confidence in my work and my place in the market. At the same time, I study hard to stay updated, as the technology market is very dynamic and demands constant updating for professional growth."

My relationships with men have always been based on partnership, focused on work and achieving goals, but at the same time maintaining the lightheartedness, joy, and relaxed nature that are part of my personality. I always strive to respect each other's space while never allowing boundaries to be crossed. Respect is the fundamental pillar of any relationship.

After this long journey of over 20 years in the corporate world and the technology market, I can affirm that a diverse context (different genders, age groups, cultures, professions, beliefs…) greatly contributes to achieving better results.

Early in my career, I had to adapt to male behavior to mitigate the stereotype of being a woman in technology, which wasn't difficult for me. Over time, there were a few occasions where I encountered sexist comments or situations, but I never let it affect me or give it any significant meaning; my focus and attention were always on performing my work with quality and contributing what I could.

Overall, the biggest difficulties were not related to gender differences; working with technology means being surrounded by a diversity of technologies and constant challenges, requiring determination, creativity, and knowledge to generate results.

It's a very promising market with many job opportunities.

Working with technology means being able to handle constant challenges and market volatility.

Make the most of learning opportunities; many companies are encouraging female professionals, and we see events dedicated to women giving space for the female universe to gain prominence in the world of technology. Women are characterized by being meticulous, careful, and creative in solving problems, which are essential qualities for anyone working in IT.”

**![](/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Daniella-Barros-271x300.jpg)Name: Daniela Barros**  

**Role: Project Manager**

**Area: Delivery**

“I started my internship very young, at a time when few women chose a career in IT. For several years, I was the only woman in my department. As a woman, young and an intern, I quickly learned to deal with “veiled harassment” and realized that I needed to position myself as an equal to be accepted and respected. Little by little, I proved my professional ability, developed my skills, and earned my place. I often say that a large part of the professional I am today was built during those years, because although the technology of the time is outdated, the learning I gained is timeless and has guided my career.”

For women, each new job opportunity is a new challenge, as we always need to prove how capable we are. Prejudice against us happens in many ways, because women are mothers, daughters, wives, and have many things to occupy themselves with. It seems old-fashioned, but I've seen friends hear complaints about the length of maternity leave or be labeled as "having problems with their children."

That's why I've always tried to surround myself with amazing people, good role models, and constructive relationships. Sometimes, it didn't happen that way, and I needed to know it was time to change and look for another place.

This is how we grow professionally: through competence, confidence, and recognition, but also through choices and positioning.

Over the years, I've discovered that I can make the most of the resources we women have, being sensitive when needed and strong when necessary. We don't need to transform ourselves into something we're not to gain space in a typically masculine universe. We already have our space guaranteed to maintain the balance of this scale; they need us. The secret is knowing how to find your place.”

**![](/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Priscila-Bacelar-300x297.jpg)Name: Priscila Bacelar**

**Job Title: Human Resources Specialist**

**Area: People and Management**

**“** As a woman and a leader within the People team, which is generally mostly women, I see my role in the technology market as encouraging and working harder to create more spaces for women. Here I feel respected and heard, and as part of the team I strive to create even more opportunities for women within this reality.”

I've always been a very sensitive person, and at certain points in my career I've noticed a prejudice linked to that, as if I needed to compromise too much or be too nice. In those moments, it was a challenge to show that I wasn't like that without losing this characteristic, which is important to me. I confess that at certain times this loss occurred, but maturity, interaction with other women, and working in companies that respect this have shown me a path for professional development.

Don't lose your unique qualities to fit in; use them to your advantage to achieve what you're looking for.
