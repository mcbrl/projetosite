Have you ever heard of the term DevSecOps? Nowadays, security has become a crucial and determining element for the success of any operation.

In summary, DevOps aims to align development and operations teams, while DevSecOps adds the element of security to this equation.

Therefore, when planning your Digital Transformation strategy, in addition to establishing a solid foundation in the DevOps methodology, it is essential to be concerned with protecting your company's data, that is, with security.

For many experts on the subject, DevSecOps is an evolution of DevOps, an improvement, insofar as it brings the key factor of Security to the method.

When we talk about DevOps, we are referring to the premise of collaboration between development and operations teams; when we refer to DevSecOps, we are referring to the synergy between development, security, and operations teams.

In this article, updated in June 2023, we will explore the DevSecOps concept, analyzing the role of security in development processes, and addressing how the shift to agile development methodologies and the adoption of DevOps forces companies to review their software quality and security management methods.

*Content of this publication updated in June 2023.*

## **The role of security in the development process.**

For some time, the role of security in companies was restricted to a specific team in the final stages of development. This didn't cause as many problems when development cycles lasted months or even years.

However, with the [DevOps methodology](/en/What-is-DevOps/) and the spread of Agile methods, those days are over.

The DevOps methodology is transforming the way applications are developed, effectively ensuring fast and continuous development cycles.

It's important to note that outdated security practices can lead to inefficiency, hindering even the most efficient DevOps solutions.

**Responsibility should be shared between operations departments and development teams.**

In this way, it is possible to take full advantage of the agility and responsiveness of a DevOps approach.

Based on this premise, IT security should also play an integrated role in the complete lifecycle of your applications.

## **But in practice, what is DevSecOps?**

Now that we've clarified the importance of a collaborative structure between development and operations, we've explained that security is an integrated responsibility, linking all points in the DevOps framework. Therefore, let's grasp the essence of the real need to build a solid and efficient security foundation.

Faster deliveries have resulted in traditional security teams being unable to protect these new, dynamic environments.

These teams began to realize the need to integrate into the DevOps culture, envisioning the possibility of being part of the development cycle – introducing new software versions even in the early stages of the *build process.*

As a result of this initiative, it was possible to reduce testing time, increase and streamline security, decrease errors, and achieve delivery that was not only fast but also efficient.

The term **DevSecOps** means thinking about application infrastructure and security from the very beginning of the build process. This is also directly linked to automating certain security measures, preventing the DevOps workflow from declining.

Using the right tools to seamlessly integrate security can help you achieve your security goals.

However, effective DevOps security requires more than just new tools. It relies on cultural changes to shape the work of security teams.

This new approach requires a new mindset, a cultural shift that must be embraced and integrated by all sectors of the company.

## **Integration and automation are key to the success of DevSecOps.**

The ideal approach is to include security as an integral part of the entire application lifecycle.

We must keep in mind that DevSecOps is about internal security, not about security that acts as a perimeter around data and applications.

To ensure we can benefit objectively, in an integrated and efficient way, we have outlined below some key points to help you understand the start of a DevSecOps culture strategy in a simple manner.

Below is a step-by-step guide to help you:

1.  Invite security teams at the start of the DevOps initiative;
2.  To encourage a change in attitude between DevOps and security teams, fostering dialogue and the creation of a cohesive team;
3.  To promote closer integration between traditionally isolated teams;
4.  Define a plan for security automation;
5.  To determine risk tolerance by conducting risk/benefit analyses;
6.  Encourage developers to think about security by sharing visibility, insights into known threats, and feedback;
7.  Promote security training for developers;
8.  Maintain short and frequent development cycles;
9.  Integrate security measures with minimal disruption to operations;
10.  Integrate security scanners into containers ([See Docker EE link](/en/Docker-EE-tutorial/));
11.  Keeping up-to-date with the evolution of DevOps culture, such as containers and microservices;
12.  Introduce secure API gateways ([see here](/en/API-gateway-or-API-management/));
13.  Automate input validation tests;
14.  Isolate containers running microservices from each other and from the network.

#### **Some of these initiatives begin at the human level, but the facilitating agent for these initiatives is automation.**

If you consider a simple mathematical operation, using DevOps + Automation as the factors in the addition, you will get the answer that DevSecOps delivers efficiently: **security.**

![](/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/1-1.png)

Organizations must step back and consider the entire development and operations environment.

In other words, this includes the entire source control repository, container registries, the continuous integration and continuous deployment [(CI/CD)](/en/What-is-a-CD-ROM-pipeline-Understand-it./) pipeline , [API management](/en/API-gateway-or-API-management/) , release orchestration and automation, and [operational management and monitoring](/en/6-reasons-to-start-monitoring-your-application-performance/).

New automation technologies have helped organizations adopt more agile development practices and have also played an important role in the progress of new security implementations.

But automation isn't the only thing about the IT landscape that has changed in recent years – technologies evolved from the cloud, such as containers and microservices, are now an important part of most DevOps initiatives, and DevSecOps security must adapt to meet them.

## **Enhance security with a focus on containers and microservices.**

Thanks to their extensive scalability and highly flexible infrastructure, the way many organizations and companies conduct their business activities has undergone significant transformations.

From this, DevOps security practices must adapt to the new scenario and align with the specific security guidelines of the container.

Therefore, security must be continuous and integrated into all stages of the application and infrastructure lifecycle.

DevSecOps means building security into end-to-end application development. This integration into the pipeline requires a new organizational mindset as well as new tools.

Based on this, DevOps teams should automate security to protect the overall environment and data, as well as the continuous integration/continuous delivery process, a goal that will likely include securing containerized microservices.

## **How can I use DevSecOps in my company?**

Many companies find it difficult to begin [implementing DevSecOps](/en/common-challenges/) , whether due to cultural issues, lack of manpower, resources, or tools.

**Empower your team –** Companies that invest in improving their team have a crucial advantage for success when establishing an internal development program. Therefore, promoting security workshops and keeping team members updated on the latest trends are essential measures to increase the chances of success in implementing a DevSecOps strategy, as well as stimulating professional growth.

**Encourage knowledge sharing –** IT managers who enable smart practices and encourage developers to share their knowledge can foster good practices in developing more secure code, making it a philosophy to be followed. Through this approach, security teams will naturally have information to share, feedback to provide, and everyone involved in the project will be constantly aware of threats. The culture of knowledge sharing should be incorporated into the company.

**Implement automated security testing** – Map out the security flaws and vulnerabilities in your project. After identifying and correcting them, dedicate efforts to developing automated security solutions, thus making the error correction process automated.

Code these solutions so that they can be included in the unit tests of the new features added. This way, security requirements will be incorporated from the beginning of the development process. Perform automations with specialized security teams, carefully and cautiously, implementing them first in test environments.

**Use security experts from the start** – The sooner security specialists get involved in the project, the better, as this way the project will be designed from the perspective of security best practices.

Ideally, the concept of DevSecOps should already be incorporated during the implementation phase of the DevOps methodology. The security aspect, as mentioned earlier, must be continuous throughout the entire development process. If security is considered late, the chances of a serious failure occurring will be much higher, resulting in additional work and delays in application delivery.

**Conduct regular audits** – Schedule security audits across the entire infrastructure that currently supports your software projects. Perform constant testing, simulate intrusions and scenarios that challenge your security team, in order to consider all possible failures. This will allow you to develop policies to prevent security breaches.
