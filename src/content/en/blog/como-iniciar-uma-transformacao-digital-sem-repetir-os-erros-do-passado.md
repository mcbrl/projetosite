When a company undertakes a *cloud-native* project , let alone a complete transformation, it already starts with many challenges.

Many professionals still lack experience with these new technologies and have little understanding of the changes that need to be made to people and processes to obtain maximum benefit for the business; however, there are some *red flags* that may appear, making the chances of success even smaller.

It is very common to realize during the digital transformation process that the company is falling victim to a common problem with these initiatives: repeating the mistakes of the past.

This is very common, so it's necessary to recognize the mistake and confront it so that it's not repeated.

## Clinging to a 'low-code' future

Consider this example: a certain company is working with outdated and inflexible systems used over the years, and is currently bolting on a variety of new systems onto a legacy AS/400-based *core* , created using proprietary development technologies, and is now beginning its digital transformation. It is still a profitable company, but it is starting to lose customers, and reports show that revenue is decreasing each month.

The project aims not only at changing the technology, but also the organizational culture. However, the company operates with little or no involvement from its business executives, and, moreover, has very aggressive goals to achieve.

All of these factors can be considered warning signs, but the biggest one involves the company's choice of platform. The organization is facing a ' *low-code* ' development platform product, which could become a challenge to adapt to in the future, mainly due to vendor lock-in.

Unlike open standards, major languages, and open-source products, this platform will find it difficult to find support for emerging problems, even with a larger community available.
The company chose this platform because it doesn't believe it can hire professionals, in sufficient quantity or quality, to build in any other way; in other words, it's repeating a past mistake: clinging to an inflexible and proprietary platform that will make it increasingly difficult to maintain and scale. There isn't much supporting documentation available on internet forums, and the developer ecosystems are small.

Choosing a technology like this contributes to a vicious cycle in attracting and hiring talent. It will become increasingly difficult for the company to hire high-quality people. The best professionals want to work with cutting-edge technology, not with rigid products.

What can be understood from the case above is that there are enough *red flags* for the transformation process to fail in the short to medium term. At best, it will offer what the company currently does more efficiently, but more likely, while it is busy building a system to maintain its *status quo* , its competitors will innovate and surpass it.

But it doesn’t have to be like this.

## A Case Study of Transformation

A decade ago, an organization was looking to migrate its platform away from the same niche proprietary development technology.

Over two years, the company replaced a multi-million line AS/400 codebase with a modern Java-based application through an automated conversion and targeted rewriting process. Simultaneously, it underwent agile development processes and organizational changes.
It was a high-risk and high-investment project, but it worked because:

- The project involved a high-caliber technical team with a solid understanding of the desired outcome.
- The people leading the project correctly identified the key problems that the transformation needed to solve, and understood the benefits of developing an open-source language, such as Java, instead of repeating the initial selection of a proprietary development technology;
- The company recognized that it needed to change people and processes, as well as technology, and acted on that understanding.

Today, this company has evolved from a simple consulting firm into a software engineering company. By executing its initiative with an open-minded team from the start, thinking strategically, and making careful changes and choices throughout the organization, its digital transformation has worked and yielded excellent results.

## Conclusion

*A cloud-native* transformation , when implemented thoughtfully and gradually, has reduced risk. Success also depends on the involvement of everyone in the organization and its partners.

No matter what your business is, the way forward means embracing this idea – and learning from past mistakes.
