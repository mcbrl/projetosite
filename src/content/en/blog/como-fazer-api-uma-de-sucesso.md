Building a **successful** [**API**](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/o-que-e-api-entenda-de-uma-maneira-simples/) has become the guiding principle for developers most aligned with our current times. We live in an era where data is one of the most valuable assets a company can possess.

As a result, many companies have focused on finding new ways to facilitate and improve data exchange between internal applications. Another measure adopted is opening up their data reserves to enter the platform economy.

Many organizations have already realized that platform-based business models tend to enhance their growth strategy.
We can say that APIs are at the heart of the new era unfolding before our eyes – the era of [**Transformation**](/en/real-digital-transformation/).

As **Successful APIs** These are solutions consumed by developers from various sectors, with different needs, and who are under constant pressure to grow and evolve their services as the company and the market mature.
Given the interplay between consumer needs and market dynamics, it's clear that APIs can no longer be treated merely as technical artifacts. Instead, they must be treated with the same attention and planning as a \[missing word - likely "product" or "product code"\]. **first-class product**.

That's why a product management approach is important to unlock the true potential of a successful API offering, providing an attractive service for both internal and external developers.

To produce a consumer-centric and product-centric approach, we can use a simple framework to think about the entire API development and delivery process.
Below are 7 key steps to help you on your journey to creating a successful API:

## **Step 1: Understand the purpose of your API.**

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/objetivo-da-API-de-sucesso-1-768x432-1.jpg)
What problem does your API offering promise to solve? Having a results-oriented approach to API development can help you define better success metrics, create a sustainable and scalable development strategy, and facilitate faster stakeholder adoption. Some well-known use cases for APIs include:

- Facilitate faster development and create an adaptable architecture by exposing all internal services via APIs – Amazon
- Create a successful platform to drive greater user engagement – ​​Facebook
- Increase traffic to the main website by encouraging users to refer the data through a partner API model – eBay

It's important to fully understand the purpose of your API to see how it maps into your business taxonomy. This taxonomy is composed of smaller building blocks of your business, based on a functional approach.
Identifying this and defining the problem space of your API helps provide a more focused approach to conceptualizing the exact purpose of your API and how it maps to your company.

## **Step 2: Understand your API audience.**

It's easy to throw the word "developer" around. What's important to understand is what result the end developer is trying to achieve and in what domain they are operating. There can be two types of consumers for your APIs:

- Consumers in their direct vertical: this type of API consumer creates services that directly benefit the primary consumer of their main product.

For example, Slack end users are people within a company trying to communicate better with each other. To further facilitate collaboration and communication, the Slack API can be easily integrated with many popular third-party tools that companies use and centralizes notifications received from those tools. In this way, the Slack API can directly benefit the very people who use its tools, increasing engagement and value.

- Consumers in tangential verticals: this type of API consumer creates services that may not necessarily help the direct consumer of the product, but solves the need of another vertical.

For example, ride-hailing companies like Uber could integrate with the Yelp API to suggest good restaurants based on their passengers' location and destination. In this example, the Yelp API connects to the Uber API and, through this, reaches users indirectly, adding more value to the customer experience. In general, this allows you to have a view of the complete API value chain, comprised of both direct and indirect consumers of the API.

## **Step 3: Align your team**

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/equipe-da-API-de-sucesso-1-768x432-1.jpg)
A scalable and successful API program first needs a solid team behind it that is fully aware of the API's objectives and purpose. A great team starts with alignment.
The belief that APIs are products, that they solve problems, and that there are customers who care about their quality, is a mindset that needs to be instilled by all teams. A culture of digital transformation involves full commitment to the power of APIs as the foundation of a successful platform.

Alignment can come when you clearly articulate the current and future state of your APIs and how they relate to your overall business strategy. After that, figure out how you bring together all your development, operations, and marketing teams.

The way you choose the right people and define processes that keep your workflow intact can be a determining factor in the success of your APIs. You should also implement tools that support a collaborative approach to developing these APIs.

## **Step 4: Develop your API incrementally.**

Having a product-oriented approach involves creating APIs that provide immense value to their consumers. This is possible by encouraging user participation as a way to measure the quality of the developed API.

In this sense, in addition to having a direct communication channel with API users, it is necessary to take their criticisms and suggestions into account. Another good practice is to keep an eye on technological innovations and, with this, keep your APIs constantly evolving.

The API lifecycle has several phases, ranging from design, creation, testing, and operation to monitoring and bug fixes. It's crucial to note that all these phases must be supported by a robust infrastructure of tools that align with your company's culture, methodologies, and practices.

There are several tools on the market, such as [Anypoint Platform](/en/mulesoft/) , that help development teams design and build APIs in a collaborative and secure environment.

## **Step 5: Define the KPIs**

A *Key Performance Indicator* (KPI) is a measurable value that demonstrates how efficiently your API is achieving its business objectives. While your API is being developed, it's important to understand exactly what some quantifiable measures of success for your API program are. It's easy to get lost in a sea of ​​potential KPIs you can compare yourself to, so linking your measurable results to the API's objective and audience, defined in steps 1 and 2, can help.

## **Step 6: Collecting and acting on feedback**

The metrics defined in the previous step can help you establish market comparisons by gathering feedback on the quality and commercial value of your API. After completing the above steps, make the APIs available on a basic portal to allow stakeholders to consume them and obtain their feedback. This step aims to obtain some valuable initial sample feedback from potential users, as follows:

1.    Identify early adopters who can provide feedback. You can leverage relationships with some of your existing customers or scout for early adopters on channels where they are frequently present.
2.    Collect and consolidate feedback, in order to iterate on it.

This feedback needs to be processed, evaluated, and, if necessary, implemented quickly, as part of the build-measure-learn philosophy. The new feedback is then incorporated back into the API design and development process, releasing new updates continuously.

## **Step 7: Successful API Marketing**

The marketing strategy for your successful API should focus on educating your customers about its benefits and advocating why it should be an essential part of the developer's toolchain.

At the end of the day, you want to make sure your APIs are being discovered and consumed, both internally and externally.
In general, marketing is a combination of content aimed at promoting the API's success in an integrated way.
Furthermore, it is necessary to generate knowledge about the product through usage tutorials, create channels for interaction, and bring developers closer to users.

Obviously, there's no single way to create a marketing strategy; what you shouldn't do is neglect this step.
