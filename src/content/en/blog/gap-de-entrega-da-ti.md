The IT delivery gap affects all sectors of a company and directly impacts short- and long-term results. Therefore, more and more companies understand that they need to redesign their internal processes and invest in best practices to reduce the technology gap quickly **and ensure information security.**

It is well known that in recent times the demand for technological innovations has increased the workload of IT teams, compounded by the pressure to meet deadlines.

In this article we will address an issue of essential importance for IT leaders: the **IT delivery gap**.

## Effects of the so-called Fourth Industrial Revolution

For many business leaders, the “**Fourth Industrial Revolution**"It's already underway. Our era is marked by a mix of technologies that are blurring the lines between the physical, the digital, and the biological."
This revolution is considered as disruptive as the agricultural, industrial, and electronic revolutions (major milestones of past generations). **Klaus Schwab, the founder of the World Economic Forum**, outlined the hallmarks of this revolution:

> “ *The possibilities of billions of people connected by mobile devices, with unprecedented processing power, storage capacity, and access to knowledge, are limitless. And these possibilities will be multiplied by emerging technological advances in fields such as artificial intelligence, robotics, the Internet of Things, autonomous vehicles, 3D printing, nanotechnology, biotechnology, materials science, energy storage, and quantum computing.”*

While these technological advantages have the potential to offer significant benefits to the world, they also present extraordinary challenges.

For the company, the challenge posed by the emergence of all these technologies is particularly profound. These forces are not merely a matter of structural change in the business model – as we saw in the late 90s, for example, when e-commerce entered into conflict with the retail sector.

The emergence of all these forces at once is transforming the way industries do business; it is demanding a radical shift in corporate culture. The qualities needed to survive in today's business environment are very different compared to previous years.

Today's organizations have to deal with demanding stakeholders who engage through new channels, competition coming from everywhere due to lower barriers to entry, and a truly global market.
Today the paradigm has changed, it's no longer the "*The big one eats the little one.*”; today, the “*The quick eats the slow.*".

The qualities that organizations need to succeed today are: **agility, inventiveness, the ability to test, fail fast, and the ability to manage security**.

How can companies ensure they have the necessary qualities to survive and thrive in today's competitive environment?

How can they ensure that the IT delivery gap will be as small as possible?

How will you enable your IT team to develop an innovative and truly agile infrastructure?

One of the solutions lies in a unified vision of enterprise architecture: the **network of applications**.

## So, what exactly is the IT delivery gap?

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/grafico-de-entrega-da-TI-768x689-1.jpeg)

The chart above shows a survey conducted in 2017 by MuleSoft with 900 IT leaders.
The surprising result reveals a clear divide among decision-makers. Only 50% of them managed to meet all project demands from the last 12 months!

For 60% of professionals who were unable to meet all the demands, the main reason was a lack of sufficient resources to meet the company's digital goals.

They also acknowledged that if the **Digital Transformation goals** were not met, revenues would be negatively impacted within six months or less. In this case, the biggest obstacle they identified in achieving these goals was the **limited time available**.

It's not hard to understand why. Companies need to adapt to the endless transformations in strategy. The interactions forced by technological trends – such as cloud computing, mobile devices, and [SaaS](/en/What-you-need-to-know-about-SaaS/) – compel IT to take responsibility for implementing these responses.

Because there are so many forces at play and IT has only a finite amount of resources, what usually happens is a gap between what IT needs to do and what it can do.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/entrega-da-ti-figura-1-768x425-1.png)
With each new digital pressure arriving and IT remaining unable to add more resources or specialized capacity, the gap is widening rapidly and exponentially.

Furthermore, under pressure, the IT department often takes shortcuts and creates technical debt, even as complexity increases, which exacerbates the problem.

And this only addresses the technological change that companies have to respond to today. IT decision-makers believe their businesses will change more in the next five years than in the last five years.
The question then remains: how will the IT team be able to accommodate changes that cannot be predicted?

Clearly, a new approach to project delivery and technology strategy is needed, one that can scale IT capacity.

It is clear that it would be impossible to continue adding resources to fill the gap – what is needed is a way to exponentially increase the delivery capacity of existing resources.

It seems like an impossible task, but it can be done through reflection on the role of IT in business. In short, **IT needs a new operating model.**

## What challenges will companies face?

Creating a new operational model to enable IT to deliver existing resources will require overcoming some important challenges, such as:

**Responding to technological changes and digital disruption**
– They overburden the company's IT teams, creating a gap between what the company needs and what the IT team can actually provide.

**Maintain or increase IT resources only slightly.**
IT resources are expected to remain the same or increase very little, meaning that the IT delivery gap cannot be resolved solely through additional resources.

**Being resilient and adapting to change quickly.**
If companies do not respond quickly to these changes, it is believed that negative repercussions could occur in six months or less.

## How can companies reposition themselves to promote these changes?

It is essential to rethink the role of IT in the company, from a sole project deliverer to a strategic partner and facilitator for the rest of the business, serving its own technology deliverables. With this in mind, some clues emerge about the changes the organization needs to make:

- Consider established business models – such as franchising – when determining how IT can scale its delivery capacity.
- Establish a strategic integration discipline, led by APIs.
- To encourage and make visible the reusable assets that the entire organization can collaborate on and use to deliver new products and services.
