iPaaS is a cloud-based systems integration solution considered the next-generation integration technology. Despite this, many people still don't know **what iPaaS is** , what it actually does, and why this technology is gaining increasing prominence in major IT projects.

In this content, we will delve deeper into this concept, explaining what iPaaS is, what these platforms deliver, how they emerged, and what needs to be considered when choosing an Integration Platform as a Service.

## What is iPaaS?

**iPaaS (Integration Platform as a Service) is a cloud-based solution where users can develop integration flows that connect applications in the cloud, SaaS, or on-premise. They can then deploy these flows without needing to install locally or manage any hardware or software.**

Gartner, [in](/en/mulesoft-leader-quadrant-api-lifecycle) its reference model for iPaaS, describes a series of important functions that provide a greater understanding of what an Integration Platform as a Service does. We can highlight the following topics:

- The solution should include tools and technologies that support the execution of integration flows;
- It must support the development and lifecycle management of integrations;
- Manage and monitor application flows;
- Ensure governance of integrations;
- Include essential cloud resources, such as *multitenancy*, elasticity and self-sufficiency;

Gartner also sees iPaaS as a potential platform for the purchase, sale, and exchange of integration flows between users, service providers, and integration providers.

## Why is iPaaS the future of integrations?

The demand for systems integration solutions is growing in tandem with the driving force behind the digital transformation of companies. Every day, new applications and systems emerge that need to connect securely, quickly, and reliably with companies' legacy systems. This is where many corporate IT departments face significant challenges.

With little to no barrier to entry in SaaS adoption, companies are deploying numerous SaaS applications without IT involvement, resulting in hundreds of applications and services in the ecosystem, all isolated and unable to seamlessly communicate with each other.

Alongside this , the trend is for systems to become increasingly fragmented. As companies move their applications to the cloud, they encounter difficulties in integrating different tools, platforms, technologies, and languages.

Although many companies have made great efforts to integrate their internal systems, combating "information silos," this new scenario renews the problem, requiring a new solution adapted to combat "cloud silos," the dark side of SaaS integration.

This new set of integration problems requires a new platform to solve them . iPaaS presents itself as a virtuous path for the company to reap the benefits of a cloud architecture in a secure, manageable, and future-proof way.

## How did iPaaS come about?

No trend in the IT world has had a greater impact on businesses than the shift to cloud computing. Enterprise solutions like [Salesforce](/en/Salesforce-purchase-of-MuleSoft) , infrastructure platforms (such as Amazon EC2), and social media services (like Facebook and YouTube) are creating new needs for businesses and demanding adaptation from IT departments.

Over time, technological innovations have demanded new software architectures. The 1970s saw the beginning of the online transaction processing model. The 1980s saw the rise of client/server models. In the 1990s, three-tier component computing gained traction, followed by SOA in the 2000s. In this context, the structure of business systems is once again undergoing a transformation.

With the expansion of cloud computing, three layers of cloud solution delivery have emerged.

1.  Software as a Service (SaaS)
2.  Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS)
3.  Platform as a Service (PaaS)

## SaaS (Software as a Service)

The concept of Software as a Service emerged in the early 2000s as a major innovation. Salesforce was perhaps the most successful of the early SaaS providers. The CRM market transitioned from large, complex on-premises deployments to cloud-based services at an unexpectedly rapid pace. By the end of the decade, emerging SaaS offerings existed for almost every type of application, with new competitors appearing every day.

## IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service)

Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS) emerged next. AWS (Amazon Web Services) is an example. The emergence of new technologies, such as virtualization, allowed shared data centers to achieve higher resource utilization rates while offering dynamic scalability and avoiding upfront capital expenditures. In recent years, the drive for Infrastructure as a Service has accelerated, and a number of new competitors have emerged.

As IaaS and SaaS emerged and quickly became cornerstones of many corporate IT environments, the only missing piece was the platform layer. Addressing this layer is crucial for the next step in the evolution of cloud services.

## PaaS (Platform as a Service)

Platform as a Service (PaS) is a category of cloud computing that offers a framework and environment for users to install and manage their own applications, whether developed or acquired from third parties. It also allows the use of tools and libraries offered by the provider, resulting in significant flexibility in software usage.

The missing piece of the puzzle is a way to **integrate applications** . The emergence of a series of application platform elements – without integration solutions – is the genesis of the “cloud silo” problem.

As more applications move to this new architecture, there is a growing need to easily connect, integrate, and orchestrate these different components. Developers require adapters for various applications, orchestration capabilities, flow management, and much more. A cloud integration platform as a service (iPaaS) addresses this vital need.

## How to choose an iPaaS?

To choose an iPaaS that truly meets a company's needs, many factors must be considered. Generally, companies have different needs and may therefore give more or less weight to certain features of the tool. However, there are essential elements in an iPaaS solution, which we will address in order to shed light on the matter.

When selecting the best platform, an important point to consider is that the solution has a robust set of connectors for SaaS and cloud services, ensuring rapid updates as new cloud technologies emerge. Furthermore, an iPaaS needs to be prepared to integrate both on-premises and cloud solutions, providing a secure integration option with the company's infrastructure and legacy systems.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/IpaaS-segundo-o-Gartner.png)

Furthermore, an iPaaS should give developers the autonomy to build customized integration solutions that meet the specific needs of the SaaS world. In addition, it should allow for the creation of user-friendly and manageable integration flows.

Other essential requirements concern solutions that are based on a robust "core integration engine" mechanism and are highly available, reliable, and secure. Powerful management tools are needed to understand application performance, monitor it, and provide auditing and alerts. In terms of capacity, the platform must allow for transparent expansion as demand grows.

Finally, an integration platform must be future-proof and ready for an [API](/en/What-is-API-Understand-it-in-a-simple-way.) architecture . It's crucial that the solution allows for easy adaptation to the changing ecosystem and growth with business needs.
