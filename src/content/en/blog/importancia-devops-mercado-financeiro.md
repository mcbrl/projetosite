Today the financial market experienced serious turbulence, and several *online brokerage platforms* are having difficulty operating. Infrastructure failures in some systems have generated significant losses and are causing various disruptions for investors.

While the IT departments of online retailers have already learned to handle recurring and well-known peaks (Christmas, Black Friday, and Mother's Day, for example), it's curious to note that the IT departments of financial institutions and traders are still "old-school." To avoid losses, these companies should create scalable systems supported by "auto-scaling" mechanisms, an infrastructure practice that is very common for those who have already adopted #DevOps. 

Periods of high volatility are a kind of "surprise Black Friday" for the financial market, where many investors trade simultaneously in panic or euphoria. Brokerages with statically provisioned infrastructures are sized for a predetermined number of accesses and transactions, and when faced with this surge of traffic, they simply stop responding.

Automation and continuous improvement practices in #DevOps, combined with a good technology stack such as containers and orchestrators, solve this issue. Unforeseen peaks can be handled by an infrastructure that "adapts" to the needs of the occasion. What few people know is that adopting this path does not mean making a large initial investment in tools or products – on the contrary, the path to adopting [DevOps](/en/devops/) is a self-funded innovation journey: each step taken generates measurable savings very quickly, justifying moving forward, project by project, product by product.
