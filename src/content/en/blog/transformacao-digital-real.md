As we know, technology has driven profound changes in society and business. In this sense, Digital Transformation comes as a great ally, implementing the necessary changes for companies to better adapt to the Digital Age.

For many experts, Digital Transformation is not an end in itself, but rather a means to ensure survival. Because of this, many companies around the world have assembled teams dedicated to the digital transformation of their businesses, aiming to improve their performance and secure better results.

Here we have one point of agreement: the proper use of technology is indispensable for the development of an organization. However, what is rarely discussed is the great challenge, the trade-off, faced by the technology executive. On the one hand, they must respond to the demands and provocations for innovation that the company, investors, and the market bring to them – often stemming from the competition. On the other hand, they must also be concerned with the operational and practical issues of the business.

"Insurance company X has figured out a way to process digital reimbursements; we need to do the same." "Bank Y's customers can open an account with three clicks, why can't we do the same?" These are some of the many questions a technology leader receives daily. At the same time, they have to worry about basic issues such as whether clicking the email button will send the message to its recipient, or whether clicking the print button will have ink in the printer's toner cartridge.

How to balance this equation? On one hand, this professional needs to run the business; on the other, they need to meet the demands of digital transformation. A lack of balance means that neither of their objectives reaches its full potential. The business will be limited without the help of technology, and digital transformation, without practical results, is nothing more than a mere staged transformation. Only by balancing these two aspects can real innovation be achieved.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/trade-off-imagem-768x432-1.jpg)

## Bimodal IT: How to find the balance?

The challenge for this professional is significant; they have a lot to do: they need to learn how to divide their efforts, their mental space, define where to dedicate their energy, where to place their best talents, where to invest. How to meet two demands: they cannot let the business stop and, at the same time, they cannot fail to react to the demands that the market is bringing and driving.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/sala-de-guerra-imagem-768x432-1.jpg)
A common scene in large companies, where minutes of downtime can generate enormous losses, is these war room meetings. This is when the company's best professionals are taken away from their respective work areas to meet and discuss innovation, new service channels, or how to improve the service application, whether it's internet banking or a customer service center. Meanwhile, in the company's basements, employees are scrambling to process an order or generate an invoice.

This is a very common problem: top executives in a room, not listening to those at the base. Even though complaints are frequent, nobody listens. Real innovation comes from the inside out; the main drivers of innovation are within the company. The people who truly know the processes are the people who make the business run, the people who serve the customers, and who best understand their needs. Often, a company launches an initiative like a chat board, a personal assistant, or a robot with the promise of solving all problems, but in reality, they are not listening to the customer or the employee, risking their investment falling flat due to strategic misalignment.

This happens because companies are mostly focused on what the end user sees and perceives. A new application is truly important. It's necessary for the user to feel happy and, in a way, pampered by the platform when using it, and to have an intuitive and complete digital experience with well-designed interfaces that communicate appropriately and in a personalized way. However, if the corporate events that happen behind the scenes are ignored, the whole thing will become hollow and meaningless.

So, even more important than digital experiences is that these experiences are based on a solid foundation of digital operations. Above any innovation, the business needs to be running smoothly, in a managed and integrated way. This presupposes new business models, new processes, properly integrated systems, reusable services, and a 360-degree view, taking into account what the customer really needs.

## 5 pillars of digital transformation to achieve real innovation

To achieve true innovation, these are some of the key points that companies should consider:
![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/cultura-de-inovacao-imagem-768x432-1.jpg)

### 1 – Culture of innovation

Peter Drucker famously said, "Culture eats strategy for breakfast." He could well have continued, because it also eats operational excellence for lunch and everything else for dinner. Without a vision of a culture of innovation, no digital transformation initiative can establish itself or change the status quo. The culture of innovation must be in the people, the processes, and the technologies, and not concentrated in an isolated area of ​​the company, in the famous innovation garages, which, despite being a cool company initiative, don't solve the problem. Innovation has to permeate all areas of the company, so it's necessary to allow employees to have a voice, to give their opinions, and to propose changes.

### 2 – Digital Experiences Platforms (DXP)

Easier to implement, these are tools through which it is possible to effectively bring practical results to the business.
![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/4ps-imagem-768x432-1.jpg)
To better understand them, it is first necessary to observe the change undergone by the traditional 4 Ps of marketing, now replaced by the 4 Es, which bring the new concepts of experience, exchange, everywhere, and evangelism to the market.

#### And what does it mean?

Previously, we were more concerned with buying the product; now we are more interested in buying the experience. Just look at today's advertisements, let's take car ads as an example: you don't see a mechanical engineer on television explaining the functionalities of cars, their technical characteristics, or even highlighting their power; no, you see people driving in the most idyllic settings, demonstrating enormous satisfaction and happiness in living that experience. This strategy is intelligent because it puts the customer right in the crosshairs, in focus, and at the center of it. And in the context of digital transformations, the use of digital platforms, marketing practices, customer journey mapping, and understanding behavioral analytics are the actions that must be taken to achieve real innovation.

### 3 – APIs

APIs are a set of routines and programming standards for accessing a software application or web-based platform. Explained another way, they are the glue or link between systems, business partners, companies, and nations. Having a well-defined, well-documented, and well-publicized API strategy is essential. The landscape we live in today is quite heterogeneous: it's multi-cloud, multi-vendor, multi-technology, multi-everything.

Therefore, to meet its needs, it's necessary to have connectivity strategies that allow for agility in responding to market demands. Consider, for example, Facebook's API strategy, which has a multitude of applications interconnected to it through APIs. Having a good API strategy can guarantee new revenue from unimaginable sources; however, in practice, these are some of the problems found in the current business environment, and which should be avoided:

### 4 – DevOps

It's no use having a platform with well-documented and designed digital strategies and APIs if I don't have the agility to deliver the software my company needs. DevOps is a term coined to describe a set of practices for integrating software development teams and adopting automated processes for the rapid and secure production of applications and services. In other words, this pillar is the main one involved in software delivery. Working together, without silos of separation, with the goal of making corporate IT more agile and efficient, responding faster to market demand and faster to the need for change, whether for innovation or problem correction.

Imagine this scenario: an e-commerce store, in the middle of Black Friday, identifies a problem. At checkout, if a customer buys four products in their cart instead of three, the product is not registered. The company waits two days for a modification window to be able to publish a change. That's it, they lost Black Friday due to the inability to react quickly. Meanwhile, just in the time it takes to read this article, Netflix may have already performed eight DevOps operations on its platform, without its customers being impacted at all by these new software releases.

### 5 – Application Performance Monitoring

In today's landscape, companies need a new, automated approach to monitoring to control their business and ensure everything is running smoothly and as planned. They need to know if operational hiccups are within expectations or indicate an operational problem. They need to know if their Black Friday sales are going according to plan or if there are any obstacles hindering the smooth running of processes. To respond quickly, efficiently, and accurately, monitoring needs to be automated, instrumentalized, automatically configurable, and with centralized control within a unified interface for complete real-time system monitoring. This can increase IT productivity by up to three times because it allows for faster responses, quicker resolutions, and rapid removal of bottlenecks.
