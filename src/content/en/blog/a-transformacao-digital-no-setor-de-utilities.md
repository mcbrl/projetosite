As a result of the crisis triggered by the Covid-19 pandemic, several markets accelerated their [Digital Transformation](/en/Digital-transformation-and-its-contribution-to-the-consumer-goods-market./) processes on a global scale. In Brazil, the utilities sector, comprised of essential services for society such as gas, energy, and water, was no exception.

Social isolation has led to a need for companies to reinvent themselves, requiring them to invest in technology to streamline customer service and resolve issues without the need for face-to-face contact.

In this article, we will explore the impacts of the pandemic on the sector and explore the alternatives available to companies to accelerate innovation, improving their processes through system modernization, and ultimately offering better services to society.

It's worth noting that we've already discussed digital transformation in the public sector here on the blog, as part of our special coverage on the impacts of the pandemic on different sectors. If you'd like to learn more about this topic, you can check out the article [here](/en/digital-transformation-in-the-public-sector/).

## The Covid-19 pandemic and its impacts on the Utilities sector.

The world has undergone major transformations, and the utilities sector is no exception. There has been a sharp shift in consumption patterns, impacted by new work models and business strategies.

Naturally, there was an increase in the demand for electricity in homes, which, on the other hand, represented a drop in the industrial sector. A negative highlight observed during this period was the increase in non-payment, which was around R$ 14,5 billion by November 2020.

The oil and gas sector is suffering from reduced demand and increased input prices, a situation that is expected to continue until the end of 2021. Meanwhile, fuels for transportation (gasoline, diesel, and ethanol) are experiencing a 27% drop in demand compared to 2019.

This year also saw important concessions for some basic sanitation and water companies, such as CEDAE in Rio de Janeiro. Auctions are still planned before the end of the year in Amapá (R$ 3 billion in investments), Porto Alegre (R$ 2,17 billion) and Rio Grande do Sul (R$ 3 billion).

## Digital transformation in the utilities sector is already showing positive results.

It's true that many sectors were caught off guard by the rapid need to adapt to the reality of a world in crisis due to the pandemic, but the Utilities sector is going through a period of transition and many companies are building their plans to reduce costs, optimize processes and improve the customer experience.

The consulting firm [McKinsey conducted a survey that affirms the effectiveness of Digital Transformation in the Utilities sector](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/electric-power-and-natural-gas/our-insights/the-power-and-gas-blog/digital-transformation-for-utilities#) . Some data indicate an approximate 25% reduction in operational expenses, in addition to a performance gain of around 40%. That's quite something, wouldn't you agree?

Benefits of innovation for the energy industry

- Intelligently leveraging technology when applying Digital Transformation yields assertive and positive results that emerge more easily;

- Energy companies may gain capacity and predictability regarding their financial health, leading to increased cash flow.

- Through administrative tools, it is possible to analyze revenues, as well as generation plants and transmission and distribution lines. In this way, it is possible to seek mechanisms to optimize resources and increase profitability.

- With the entire production chain optimized, the energy industry will reduce costs and thus be able to invest in equipment and process improvements, generating less risk of demand disruptions.

## Technologies driving the Digital Transformation of the Utilities sector

**API adoption**

Managing applications through the use of APIs facilitates integrations between systems, improving the performance of services provided, reducing the risk of equipment failures, and assisting in the management of expenses and contracts.

**Conversational Interfaces and Personal Assistants**

Conversational interfaces through chatbots and WhatsApp, and personal assistants managed via artificial intelligence, such as Siri, Alexa, and Amazon Echo, are increasingly present in people's daily lives. Within the Utilities universe, this allows customers to check their consumption in real time and have their monthly bill forecasted to organize their monthly expenses.

**Omnichannel Digital Platforms**

Integrating your company's different communication channels is an almost mandatory differentiator in the market.

When you need to contact an electricity company to obtain a duplicate copy of your electricity bill, in addition to mobile applications, it's also likely that by calling the phone, the system will identify your data through an API that reads your phone number and connects it to the registration.

Applications that put the average user at the forefront of the business also heat up the utilities market. In the electric power generation sector, this is already becoming a reality; we have examples of engineers who have developed smaller power plants capable of supplying energy to approximately five homes from a single water source.
