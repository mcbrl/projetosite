In today's competitive landscape, a company's value delivery is closely linked to its ability to create exceptional customer experiences. This almost always means that the ability to consistently deliver reliable software is an indispensable competitive advantage: *every company is a software company*.

In modern IT, teams responsible for delivering applications have taken on a leading role as technology has ceased to be seen merely as operational support and has become one of the strategic activities in companies. 

Therefore, it is impossible for an organization that intends to be and remain competitive to still be stuck with a [traditional IT approach](/en/Traditional-IT-can-be-an-enemy-of-digital-transformation./) whose practices and management model are not focused on quality and speed of delivery.

In this context, the work systems proposed by agile methods and [DevOps](/en/devops/) culture bring concepts and tools that, while not new, need to be approached in a new way. This is the case with continuous delivery.

##
Traditional IT and modern IT in relation to continuous delivery.

Traditional IT was designed with the goal of maintaining the status quo, that is, seeking stability and availability by suppressing change. It's no coincidence that 80% of companies' IT budgets are spent on maintenance. However, it's clear that this model no longer works, and organizations that haven't yet realized this are doomed to failure. 

In this scenario, a modern IT (or, as I prefer, a *Cloud-Native IT*It plays a fundamental role: to bring fluidity to development processes, so that organizations remain competitive. 
And, among the strategies of modern IT, the adoption of [microservices](/en/Microservices%3A-10-new-security-challenges/) e [DevOps](/en/What-is-DevOps/) It emerges as a solution for companies to increase the speed of launching new features with better scalability, quality, efficiency, and lower cost.

As a result of the DevOps mantra – “you build it, you execute” – development teams have gained a great deal of autonomy and control, in addition to handling quality assurance and release tasks. However, with the shift to DevOps, the development/test/acceptance/production (DTAP) processes, tools, and environments, typically designed for traditional codebases and applications, are no longer adequate and end up acting as a barrier to greater efficiency in software delivery.

Therefore, it was necessary to rethink delivery methodologies for the development of modern software, as well as restructure the process in order to obtain the advantages of continuous delivery for software and business.

## Continuous best practices in DevOps

Several practices have been developed to optimize the software development and delivery process with the goal of achieving code quality and faster deliveries.

### 1\. Continuous Integration – Continuous integration (CI)

Finding and analyzing errors more quickly, improving software quality, and reducing the time for validating and releasing new system updates are the main objectives of continuous integration (CI) in DevOps.
With it, it is possible to have Controlling versions and changes made to the code will allow for comparison of code in different branches, prevent developers from performing the same task, increase control over each professional's output, and, in case of conflicts between new and existing code, CI facilitates correction. bugs occur quickly and frequently.

### 2\. Continuous delivery (CD)

After the automation of builds and integration tests in CI, continuous delivery automates the release of this validated code to a repository. 

At this stage, in addition to conducting tests, a release version is generated each time a new integration is made. Thus, with this new version, the developer can verify the behavior of new features without having to worry about sending any unwanted features to the end user.

With this practice, deliveries happen efficiently and objectively. Small updates and corrections are made almost daily, increasing the delivery flow, unlike what happened with Traditional IT.

### 3\. Continuous implementation – Continuous deployment

Continuous delivery and continuous deployment are very similar concepts. However, while continuous delivery automates the release of production-ready builds to a code repository, continuous deployment automates the release of an application to production.

In this model, the application is delivered to the end user automatically, and the only thing that will prevent the software from being distributed is a failure in some unit test. 

This technique is not applicable to all companies: besides being primarily designed for web application development, some companies have strict governance rules that require manual testing, which may prevent the use of this methodology.

## New challenges in DevOps with continuous delivery.

The goal of continuous practices is to get the software up and running as quickly as possible, thus contributing to the achievement of business goals. However, things don't always go as planned. 
The tests focus on technical verifications of the developers' output (the code), rather than checking for improvements (conversion, revenue) or impact (market share, profitability, customer satisfaction) on business results. In other words, traditional DTPA doesn't work for DevOps.

Since we are no longer building applications using traditional methods and silos, we cannot use static pre-production test and acceptance environments to ensure they work as intended. This is due to the independent nature of microservices and their deployments. 
Furthermore, many companies, in their haste to implement the methodology [DevOps](/en/devops/)They end up falling into some traps, such as automating the wrong processes. 

Companies with existing processes that need to implement modern IT should migrate from manual to highly automated development gradually. This transition can take months to complete, so it's important to know how you will adopt continuous delivery and continuous integration.

In our experience, "big-bang" approaches never succeed; a gradual transformation approach – "lighthouse projects," time by time, project by project – has a much lower adoption cost and far better results.
Therefore, it is essential to follow certain steps, even if you have unlimited resources and it is tempting to opt for automating everything possible from the start, or even to acquire expensive "magical" platforms with grand promises. *The greatest challenge to transformation is cultural.* Lighthouse approaches are not only based on better incentives, but also... **They postpone purchase decisions until they make sense.**.

Another very common mistake is treating this issue as mere delivery pipeline automation – bad software with automated delivery is still bad software. A new distribution of roles and team composition is essential, as is the traditional way of dealing with monitoring, which must be superseded by the [observability](/en/Observability-in-the-process-of-new-business-evolution./) available to product teams.

Modern IT, with its continuous delivery, generates a better work experience for your team and is extremely advantageous for your business, since continuous evolution, quick fixes, and quality assurance help retain customers. However, the methodology must be implemented correctly. Otherwise, it can do more harm than good.
