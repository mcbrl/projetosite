Digital Marketing and IT are powerful allies and together, they can achieve incredible results. You know that saying that the journey itself can be more important than the destination? The famous customer experience (CX), in the digital environment, is becoming more crucial than conversion. Yes, you read that right! How so, you might be wondering, right? While conversion is the goal of any company, whether digital or not, it's fundamental to win over the visitor! The objective is to turn the visitor into a repeat customer. And to do that, you need to know them better, establish an automated interaction system, if possible, to capture their purchase intentions at the moment they are occurring. In the digital world, this task has become less complicated in terms of tools, but very complicated in terms of understanding the visitor's buying journey.

This is because what matters much more is customer loyalty, as customers are becoming increasingly demanding regarding their shopping experience (CX). A consumer who completes a purchase today must be willing to return for another experience with your application.

If the technology fails, the application loads slowly \[ [Discover what it means to be fast enough in the digital world](/en/fast-enough-digital-world) \], and the user leaves the site/app frustrated, there's a high probability they won't want to use your application again. Therefore, in today's article, we'll explain what marketing professionals can and should learn from IT professionals. Read on:

## The partnership between the Marketing, Sales and IT areas

Professionals facing this new reality of the digital market know that it's impossible for digital marketing professionals to plan campaigns without the technical support of IT personnel. This is absolutely true, and those who understand this will gain a competitive edge. There's a very strong relationship between application performance and customer loyalty; understand this relationship better [by clicking here](/en/The-relationship-between-application-performance-and-customer-loyalty.)!

In the journey through the sales funnel – where Marketing, Sales, and IT teams need to be increasingly aligned in discussions about [Application Performance and Customer Loyalty](/en/The-relationship-between-application-performance-and-customer-loyalty.) – the main objective is to transform visitors into fans and paying customers. This is no small challenge. Certainly, this task cannot be done individually or manually.

Organizations that are driven to offer a good experience through their applications are able to handle the wide range of emotions linked to a purchasing process, where the consumer demands everything but is not willing to be patient with any system failures.

## Customer Experience

That's why it's said that the customer is at the heart of digital transformation. This means that every point of interaction with the customer has to be positive from their point of view. We're talking about customer loyalty, and the customer doesn't care if it's a marketing or IT failure because, in their view, the company has failed.

Three points are important in this matter:

- It's not enough to just worry about the graphic beauty of the website, mobile interfaces, or other applications;
- It's no use having the fastest app of all if it doesn't generate user engagement due to the app's visual identity or complicated navigation;
- There's no point in generating a high volume of data if you don't know what you're going to do with it.

> The Customer Experience Journey must be monitored in real time.

It's also important to remember that this experience must be offered across all sales channels; in other words, no matter which channel the consumer uses, they need to be well received (physical stores or virtual platforms, via work computer or mobile phone on the go). We live in the [omnichannel](/en/tag/omnichannel) era.
