In recent years, DevSecOps has gained prominence as the corporate IT infrastructure model has evolved exponentially. The shift to cloud-native platforms, data sharing and storage, and dynamic applications brings significant benefits to businesses.

However, despite applications being developed within a DevOps culture and having scalability, they sometimes present problems related to security and compliance. Within this scenario, the DevSecOps approach emerged, uniting development, operations, and security within the same package.

However, despite the benefits that a [DevSecOps](/en/devsecops-5-steps-to-implement/) culture brings, many companies struggle to implement it.

We prepared this article to highlight some common challenges in implementing DevSecOps and show how to overcome them.

## Why is DevSecOps important?

Cybercriminals are always looking for new ways to spread malware and other forms of attack. Therefore, the impact of malware inserted during the compilation process of an application can go undetected until it has been distributed to thousands of clients.

The damage to the system and the company's reputation ends up becoming incalculable.

Therefore, concern for security measures is as important as development and other processes. By combining [DevSecOps](/en/DevSecCops-in-API-Development%3A-4-Digital-Security-Tips-for-Your-Application/) and DevOps approaches, all developers and network administrators become concerned with security at all stages of application production and deployment.

## What are the challenges in implementing DevSecOps?

IT companies sometimes face difficulties adopting DevSecOps; here are a few:

**Difficulty in absorbing the culture:** embracing a DevSecOps culture requires discipline to put new customs into practice, which value security from the initial stages of the development and integration process.

**Learning curve for teams:** To implement DevSecOps, companies need technical teams specialized in security and development, which can be a challenge, especially for small and medium-sized enterprises.

**Limited budget for security:** Some companies may not have the financial resources to invest in security tools and technologies, however, the losses from interrupted operations or shared data can generate a much higher cost, in addition to the headaches.

**Difficulty in team integration:** The success of DevSecOps requires the collaborative work of security, development, and operations teams. When there is a failure in this communication and collaboration channel, the implementation of DevSecOps may fail.

## What are the first steps to implementing DevSecOps?

To ensure the successful implementation of a [DevSecOps](/en/devsecops-ransomware-protection/) strategy , it is essential to invest in your team's training. Conducting security workshops and keeping the team up-to-date on trends is fundamental to encouraging professional growth and increasing the chances of successfully adopting this methodology.

Furthermore, encouraging knowledge sharing is a smart practice that should be incorporated into the company culture.

It's important that the development team creates code with security as a guiding philosophy. This will allow security teams to access relevant information, feedback, and visibility into threats. Implementing automated security testing is another crucial action.

This allows for mapping security flaws and vulnerabilities in the project, identifying and correcting problems, and developing automated security solutions to iterate and automate error correction. Furthermore, it is important to implement these solutions first in test environments, with considerable security and caution.

Allocating security professionals from the start of the project is essential. The sooner they get involved, the better. The security process needs to be continuous throughout the entire development process, and if security is considered late, the chances of serious failures are greater and the work may cause delays in the application's delivery.

Finally, it's important to conduct constant audits of all the infrastructure that currently supports your software projects. Constant testing, intrusion simulations, and scenarios that push the security team to its limits allow all possible failures to be considered.

In this way, it is possible to develop policies to prevent security breaches, ensuring the protection of the project and the company as a whole.
