Financial APIs have been central to the transformation of the financial sector, driving everything from new forms of payment to entirely innovative digital experiences. Today, these financial APIs are fundamental for banks, fintechs, and other banking institutions to offer efficient services and products, connecting systems and enabling access to real-time data. 

However, while these APIs provide innovation and integration, they also bring significant security challenges to the financial sector.

The increasing use of financial APIs raises the risk of exposure to vulnerabilities. The digital environment, which connects financial institutions, their partners, and users, requires robust protection to prevent fraud, safeguard confidential data, and ensure a secure and reliable user experience. 

In this content, we will explore what financial APIs are, how they work, the importance of security measures, and the best practices that companies in the banking sector should adopt to protect their programming interfaces.

## What are APIs in the financial sector and how do they work?

In the financial sector, **financial APIs** enable banks, [**fintechs**](/en/5-reasons-for-using-chatbots-in-fintech-companies/) , and other players to share data and functionalities in a standardized and secure way. In other words, they act as digital bridges, allowing financial services, such as digital accounts, instant payments, and access to balances and transactions, to be offered directly to the end user.

For example, by using a financial API, a fintech company can connect to a bank to offer services such as balance inquiries and transfers directly on its platform. This type of integration not only facilitates user access to financial services but also promotes a simplified experience without the need to switch between multiple platforms. 

Furthermore, financial APIs are the driving force behind initiatives such as [**Open Banking**](/en/How-digital-transformation-contributes-to-open-banking./) , where financial data is shared with the user's consent, expanding the range of services offered.

These characteristics make APIs in the financial sector one of the main drivers of the sector's digitalization. They help reduce costs, improve operational agility, and create opportunities for the development of innovative products that meet the demands of modern consumers. However, the use of financial APIs comes with challenges and security risks that need to be addressed with preventive measures.

## API Security Challenges in the Financial Sector

With the advancement of APIs in the financial sector, [**threats and vulnerabilities**](/en/devsecops-ransomware-protection/) that can compromise sensitive data and affect customer trust are also growing. In many cases, poorly protected APIs become entry points for cyberattacks, as they often contain banking and personal information.

The main security risks include data interception, denial-of-service attacks, exploitation of vulnerabilities in APIs, and unauthorized access to systems. 

These threats can result in fraud, data leaks, and serious financial losses, as well as significantly damaging the institution's reputation. 

Protection against these risks involves adopting security measures and best practices that guarantee the integrity of financial data and compliance with regulations such as the [**LGPD in Brazil**](https://www.gov.br/aeb/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/lei-geral-de-protecao-de-dados-pessoais-lgpd) and the GDPR in the European Union.

## 5 Essential Security Measures for APIs in the Financial Sector

Given the importance of financial APIs and the increasing sophistication of cyber threats, securing these interfaces should be a priority for all companies in the financial sector. 

Next, we explore key security practices that help mitigate risks and protect customer data in financial APIs.

### 1\. Strict Authentication and Authorization in APIs in the Financial Sector

Robust authentication is one of the first barriers against unauthorized access to financial APIs. Protocols such as OAuth 2.0, widely used in the financial sector, allow access to APIs to be securely controlled, ensuring that only authenticated users and applications can access sensitive data. 

Furthermore, multifactor authentication (MFA) is an additional layer that helps prevent credential compromise, increasing the security of financial APIs.

Implementing strict authentication and authorization allows each user or external system to have specific and controlled permissions, minimizing the chances of an intruder gaining access to confidential information. This type of control is especially relevant in the financial sector, where data confidentiality and integrity are paramount.

### 2\. Encryption of Data in Transit and at Rest

To ensure the security of financial data during traffic between systems, it is essential that APIs in the financial sector use encryption protocols. TLS (Transport Layer Security) is widely adopted to protect transmitted data, ensuring that information exchanged between systems is secure and inaccessible to interception.

In addition to encrypting data in transit, it is also recommended that stored information be protected. Encrypting data at rest prevents sensitive information from being interpreted by attackers if accessed. 

In this way, even if a breach occurs, financial data will be better protected, reducing the potential impact of the incident.

### 3\. Continuous Monitoring and Anomaly Detection in Financial APIs

Continuous monitoring of financial APIs is an essential practice to ensure that any suspicious activity is detected quickly. Monitoring tools can identify anomalous behavior, such as access spikes or unusual requests, which may indicate attempted attacks.

By implementing monitoring systems, companies are able to act preventively, preventing cyberattacks from compromising the integrity of financial data. 

Furthermore, continuous monitoring allows security teams to be prepared to respond to incidents quickly, limiting damage and minimizing system downtime.

### 4\. Implementing DevSecOps in API Development

[**DevSecOps culture**](/en/devsecops-5-steps-to-implement/) is a practice that integrates security into the API development process, ensuring that data protection is present from the initial stages of the project. With DevSecOps, development and security teams work collaboratively to identify and fix vulnerabilities before financial APIs are released.

This approach is especially advantageous in the financial sector, where the speed and frequency of updates are critical to keeping up with consumer demands and market evolution. By adopting DevSecOps practices, companies can perform secure and agile updates, maintaining the integrity and security of financial APIs at all stages of development and operation.

### 5\. Adopt the Zero Trust Access Policy

The [**Zero Trust strategy**](/en/zero-trust-security-in-a-devsecops-strategy/) is a security model that treats all access as a potential threat, requiring rigorous authentication and verification for each request. In the context of financial APIs, this means that no device or user will have access to financial data without going through a robust verification process.

The Zero Trust policy reduces the attack surface and ensures that only authorized users and devices can interact with APIs. In the financial sector, this approach is especially effective for protecting financial data and ensuring compliance with security and data protection regulations.
