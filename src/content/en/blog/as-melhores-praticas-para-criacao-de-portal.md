If you're already convinced that you'll adopt a **Portal** **Platform** to create or improve your customer's **Digital Experience , now is the time to learn about the most important points in your planning so you can execute the project in the best possible way. Here at** [Vertigo](/en/contact?utm_source=BlogVertigo&utm_medium=Post&utm_campaign=Melhoras_Praticas_Portal) , we heavily use [Liferay](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/liferay-como-ferramenta-de-portal/) as our **Digital Platform.**

Next, we will indicate some points that should be carefully observed so that you can successfully build your digital platform:

## 1\. Identify the business area that should sponsor the portal.

Portals often face the danger of promising too much and delivering too little. While technology sometimes faces challenges in delivering the promised benefits, more often the problem lies in the lack of clarity regarding which business objective (e.g., facilitating and integrating claims communication processes, expanding sales channels, increasing mobile sales participation, reducing call center costs, creating partnerships with other companies or suppliers, integrating company areas, etc.) the portal will serve. Sometimes, no department is willing to allocate budget to a portal because the benefits are seen as inter-organizational.

On the other hand, a portal that starts with the efforts of a single department can grow to encompass a wider reach, and thus show other business units within the company the advantages of adopting that portal, but it is quite true that the original owners will hardly find anyone else willing to take on the responsibility.

Both situations can be corrected by aligning the portal's strategies with the company's vision and business strategy, securing sponsorship from senior management. In most cases, it makes sense to define the portal as the foundation for the company's internet policy and strategy (including the website, extranet, and [intranet](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/o-que-nao-pode-faltar-na-sua-intranet/) ).

In summary: **What business objective will this portal serve?**

- It will reduce bureaucracy in a public service;
- Increase your sales performance;
- Relieve the pressure on your call center and generate new sales;
- Create partnerships between your company and your suppliers.
- Start a loyalty program with other companies participating and sales happening.

## 2\. Establish guidelines on portal governance.

Getting the right content, applications, and services to the right users is one of the primary goals of any portal platform. But without proper governance and processes, content will become outdated, applications redundant, or services irrelevant, and thus the portal will be compromised.

To avoid these situations, it is advisable to:

- Create guidelines on how to create, update, and when to expire content;
- To define which services are priorities in order to improve the digital experience for those who use the portal;
- Map and integrate the main applications that need to be integrated with this platform;
- Facilitate content creation and approval processes with a pre-established workflow;
- Create well-defined roles for resource access, application approval, and approval processes.
- Review the portal periodically to remove outdated content;
- Create a plan for the portal's evolution.

## 3\. Work with software you trust.

Here at Vertigo, when it comes to creating a Portal Platform, we opt for Liferay ( [a leader in the Magic Quadrant for the sixth year](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/liferay-lider-em-portais-horizontais-pelo-sexto-ano/) ), which is open-source software that offers benefits such as: low acquisition cost, access to the source code to customize the software, search tool, ERP integration, public and private pages, among others.

Clearly, finding a supplier that offers support, compensation, and ongoing development is key to achieving your Portal's objective.

## 4\. Perform a quick initial deployment, then evolve your portal horizontally and vertically.

Companies often make the mistake of taking a "big bang" approach to portal deployment. Lengthy phases of requirements definition, development, testing, and deployment tend to drive costs up excessively. Start slowly, prioritizing what is fundamental to achieving your portal's objective.

Ideally, you should produce parts of it and submit them for user evaluation. This will ensure that they are receiving what they want/need.

## 5\. Think beyond the portal

Think beyond a simple integration framework for aggregating content and applications. Think of a portal to serve as the foundation for almost any web-based application use case.

E-commerce sites, for example, typically display products relevant to or related to a user, who can then access the site and view a personalized version with their account information and order history. While this is an external application, the portal can serve as the foundation for the website, especially if there is back-end integration with payment systems, CRM systems, logistics, supply chain, billing, etc.

Integrating web initiatives with the company-wide portal strategy can yield benefits in maintenance, reduced infrastructure costs, ease of development, and a unified digital experience for all audiences interacting with your business.
