[DevSecOps culture](/en/devsecops/) has become a major ally in protecting against cyberattacks and data kidnapping, better known as ransomware. This criminal practice has affected large companies and has been drawing the attention of managers and information security professionals around the world.

The recent cases involving Lojas Renner and JBS, which had their online services and websites paralyzed due to ransomware attacks, reignite the debate about how good data protection practices can minimize the risks of attack and protect the integrity of your project.

In this article, we will show you the fundamental role of security in the application development process and in data protection amidst the wave of ransomware attacks.

## What is ransomware?

Preventing ransomware attacks is one of the main challenges for DevSecOps professionals, as they represent a growing and often unsolvable threat, devastating applications and consequently company data.

But what exactly is so dangerous about ransomware? Understanding the origin of the term will make it easier to comprehend; the prefix "ransom" means "ransom," and the suffix "ware" already gives an idea of ​​the type of malware we are dealing with.

Ransomware is software designed to carry out extortion by cryptographically locking your files, either on your local computer or on a server, followed by a ransom demand to undo the encryption.

Infection can occur in several ways; the most common is that malware infects your device, and depending on the type of ransomware, your files are encrypted or even the entire operating system is compromised.

Next, large sums of money are extorted from the victims, almost always with a message pointing to a cryptocurrency wallet. It is worth noting that security experts do not recommend payment, especially since there are no guarantees that the criminals will keep their word and return access to your files.

## How does DevSecOps protect your company?

**Implementing the Observability concept –** As your company's infrastructure evolves, it's important to have visibility into security events and understand your application's data flows.

By implementing a higher rate of change, microservices and *serverless* architectures require continuous monitoring to detect events in an immutable infrastructure.

Monitoring allows the development team to intervene quickly when errors are detected in their systems, since observability aims to collect data in real time to identify faults.

**Security power via containers –** Containers require an effort in orchestration systems. They have layers of isolation between applications and between the application and the host.

[Docker containers](/en/container-orchestration-tools/) create these layers that protect the host and the containers while reducing the host's surface area.

Containerizing your applications offers greater security compared to virtual machines, provided they are deployed correctly; otherwise, it can leave your system vulnerable.

**Programmatic and declarative control policies –** Defining your company's infrastructure and network via software creates the opportunity to establish programmatic and declarative control mechanisms, automating system security.

To increase the security and integrity of applications, it is recommended to establish codified management policies and apply security to the mobile layers of their distributed architecture.

**Password management –** ​​Password management within companies is a key factor in ensuring security and preserving applications, systems, and environments.

It is very common for employees within the company to be responsible for leaking confidential information, such as passwords, API tokens, application code snippets in code repositories, configuration files, or registry entries.

Vertigo uses a great solution in its projects to promote security via containers and microservices through Vault, a [Hashicorp](/en/partnerships/hashicorp/) tool . Through this solution, it's possible to protect, store, and rigorously control employee access to tokens, passwords, certificates, and encryption keys to safeguard confidential information.

[**CI/CD Pipeline**](/en/What-is-a-CD-ROM-pipeline-Understand-it./) **–** Having a chain of continuous integration and delivery steps until the final version of a software is released is a critical component for protecting your company. When considering security within the pipeline, we must focus on minimizing the attack surface for cyberattacks within the development infrastructure itself. This ensures that automated and manual checks occur as the various stages are executed.

**Protecting access to code –** Reducing access to code and, consequently, the number of vulnerabilities within packages that can be deployed. Furthermore, it is recommended to limit the likelihood of code disclosure, minimizing the risk of malicious code being introduced into the environment. A common practice adopted by companies is the hybrid solution, which combines on-premises and cloud-native infrastructure to host and manage their code and artifacts.

**Cloud-native solutions –** Migrating your architecture to the cloud requires careful planning, as it is very common for [leaders to make basic mistakes in cloud-native migration](/en/Cloud-Native%3A-6-main-mistakes-when-migrating-your-infrastructure-to-the-cloud./) when done without an experienced partner or without a correct vision of the project.

It is necessary to create Identity and Access Management (IAM) strategies, including separate accounts or subscriptions in business units. It is also important to have teams with well-defined roles within application development or in testing environments and network segmentation. These are undoubtedly important steps in building a security strategy that will be put to the test over time.

**Access Management –** ​​IAM is a necessary and one of the most important practices, as it is present in all aspects of the software delivery pipeline. It is present from the moment a developer checks the code until the end of the process, when it is deployed through CI/CD.

With the growing popularity of cloud-native solutions, IAM and role-based access controls are becoming more deeply embedded in the technology stack.

Ideally, developers should not interact directly with the cloud architecture; instead, they should deploy through declarative configurations that launch the infrastructure on demand. This way, teams should deploy via these workflows, rather than manually modifying the infrastructure or platform services.

## Security professionals estimate that more ransomware attacks will occur.

According to new research from the security firm [Deep Instinct](https://www.deepinstinct.com/) , 78% of DevSecOps professionals expressed concern about cyberattacks in the next 12 months.

Approximately 600 IT and cybersecurity professionals were surveyed, with more than half of respondents believing that ransomware attacks are a potential threat to their organizations.

The Voice of SecOps report from Deep Instinct shows that 86% of respondents believe that tools powered by artificial intelligence, machine learning, and deep learning have a significant impact on preventing unknown threats.

According to the same report, 64% of respondents believe that humans are unable to keep up with the exponential pace of cybersecurity threats, further highlighting the need for DevSecOps solutions when planning data protection for their company.
