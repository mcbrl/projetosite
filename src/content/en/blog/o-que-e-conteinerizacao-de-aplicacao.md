## **The concept of application containerization**

In a simplified way, it's the process of distributing a software application in a compartmentalized, portable, and self-contained manner. Containers are sometimes confused with lightweight VMs ( *Virtual Machines* ) due to these characteristics; however, containers are more compact and efficient.

This tool becomes essential for many companies due to its agile nature, predictable behavior, portability across diverse scenarios, and minimal *footprint* (compared to VMs).

Containers have spread to various platforms beyond Linux, with Docker now being the *de facto* standard for their implementation.

## **Advantages of Containerization**

Their functionalities, benefits, and advantages go far beyond those mentioned above. Containers are fast, compact, and efficient when used in orchestrated operations across multiple tasks. Furthermore, they also possess high resilience – they can be quickly restarted in response to failures or operated for extended periods.

Another advantage of containerization is its adaptability to the use of microservices. This methodology is common in the development of mobile applications, optimizing production and facilitating the maintenance of environments.

Containers not only make the entire process more practical, but also more qualitative, maximizing productivity and making everything faster. The ability to run multiple containers on a single server in isolation results in greater efficiency in the use of computing resources.

## **Containerization and security**

Another advantageous feature of containers lies in their ability to promote security. This feature exists because the attack surface of a container is smaller than that of a VM.

The widespread acceptance of using containers in the creation of automated testing pipelines allows for faster updates and customizations to security policies in deliverables.

[**Check out our article about myths and truths about containers!**](/en/Truths--myths--modernization-of-containerized-applications/)

However, not everything is entirely risk-free or safe. Containerization can also present new security challenges, but many threats can be detected through scanning tools and analysis of container images.

It is necessary to incorporate resources into the delivery pipeline that analyze development artifacts within the containerization platform. This eliminates problems that are often overlooked by IT professionals. The immutability characteristic of the container simplifies risk analysis and mitigation in its processes.

More critical environments even have the possibility of ensuring greater isolation between containers, such as in specific *tenants* , SDNs (software-defined networks), or even firewall solutions between containers.
