New technologies have changed the way people access information and initiate their omnichannel strategy.

- Google searches, anywhere, using a smartphone;
- Social networks that facilitate recommendations between friends;
- Different applications that communicate through partnerships.

These are examples of the possibilities that the modern world offers us; these innovations have also changed the way consumers shop, and the traditional concept of commerce is increasingly being altered.

While in past decades it was common to see television advertisements selling products over the phone and commercials encouraging customers to visit stores, today this reality is much more interconnected: customers search for products on e-commerce sites and make purchases in physical stores, discover new things on television and search for them online, or even find what they are looking for through recommendations on social media. This interaction across different channels is called **omnichannel.**

In this post you will discover how to start your omnichannel strategy, and how the correct use of **API management** technologies can help in this process. Follow along:

## Starting your omnichannel strategy

To begin your omnichannel strategy, you need to understand what is most important in this process:

> Create a consistent integration between your different channels to generate the best possible customer experience. 

For this to happen, your audience needs to be able to move seamlessly between your different platforms, always achieving the desired result: being able to buy any of your products at any time, from any of these channels, anywhere, at the lowest price (if possible, of course) and through a quality service. But how do you start defining this strategy? First of all, you need to know some information:

- What are the most used channels by your audience?
- Identify your main problems in digital channels and how to solve them;
- Study the forms of interaction that best fit your business;
- Collect data and analyze purchases made by your customers on your digital platform (e-commerce, loyalty program, data query channel, etc.).

With this information in hand, you can get a better idea of ​​the path to follow to begin your omnichannel strategy. Now it's necessary to put this process into action and create new integrations, which can be done through an API, for example, but we'll talk about that later.

## Creating the omnichannel experience

After understanding how to develop the initial strategy, it's necessary to create the perfect omnichannel experience for those who will consume your service. To make this possible, you need to take some very important steps:

- *Improving the customer experience in channels where it needs improvement serves to equalize the service offered.* (Have your IT department communicate with your Marketing department);
- *Maintain brand identity across all channels;*
- *Establish a direct link between your physical store and your e-commerce/mobile app.* (promote this interaction, show them that buying through digital channels is just as simple as buying through other channels);
- *To make the connection between the systems used* (Integrate this information and start building a unified view of your customer. If they buy through e-commerce, mobile app, or in-store, you'll know!)

These steps are important so that your brand can maintain identification with the consumer, and that regardless of their first point of contact with your brand, they can expect the same experience in future interactions and on different channels. Regarding the connection between systems and the link between the physical and virtual store, the use of an API can be the key to success: it will help to carry out all this integration and make your systems work together.

## How an API helps you build your omnichannel strategy.

An API acts as a bridge: it's designed to enable one system or channel to communicate with another. Thanks to APIs, we have the integration of social networks with *smartphone* apps , online stores within marketplaces, integrated inventory systems for different types of businesses, and even the Internet of Things and cloud solutions. Just as an API can connect all these systems, it can also help integrate the different channels of your business.

API management platforms, such as those from [Mulesoft](/en/partnerships/mulesoft) , [WSO2](/en/partnerships/wso2) , Oracle, and others, aim to simplify API management and streamline deployment. These tools help capture audience information from various databases and ERP systems, providing a more comprehensive view of who each person is through their business consumption. The goal is to create a faster and more efficient omnichannel experience and integration process, and API management platforms achieve this by working with mobile applications, cloud solutions, security controls, and leveraging Big Data to aid in strategy analysis.

Use this information when defining your omnichannel strategy and achieve better results. Consider different API management platforms for your business, looking for the one that best fits your needs and can help your business grow.

## Omnichannel

This initiative has 3 main objectives:

1.  You must establish a common customer experience across all channels. In order to achieve this, the team must ensure integration between back-end systems that interact with customer information.
2.  It should facilitate agile solutions that are responsive to changes in the business; that is, whether modifying current business processes or creating new businesses, these APIs should help with that.
3.  You must reduce the time it takes to make new business solutions available to your audience, whether internal or external. To achieve this goal, the team must ensure heavy reuse of this API-based solution.
