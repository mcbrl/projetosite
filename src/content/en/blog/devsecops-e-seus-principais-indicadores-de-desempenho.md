Whether your organization is medium-sized or large, you need the right metrics to determine the effectiveness of your security, performance, and overall operations. This is no less true when it comes to measuring the success of the **DevSecOps approach** in your company.

DevSecOps needs to be led by members of the security team because they are responsible for [**the**](/en/devsecops/) company's cybersecurity posture. Those tasked with implementing it should expect a learning curve as they connect with [**DevOps**](/en/devops/) teams , familiarize themselves with concepts like continuous delivery, tools like [Docker](/en/docker/) , and determine the best way to implement DevSecOps in their organizations.

## Integration and automation are key to the success of DevSecOps.

The most appropriate approach is to include security as an integral part of the entire application lifecycle. It should be kept in mind that... **DevSecOps is about internal security, not perimeter security.**, which operates around data and applications.
To benefit objectively, in an integrated and efficient way, read below some key points so that the start of the DevSecOps strategy can be understood more easily.

## Step-by-step guide to help you evangelize and implement a DevSecOps culture:

1.  Invite security teams right at the start of the DevOps initiative;
2.  To encourage a change in attitude between DevOps and security teams, promoting dialogue and the creation of a cohesive team;
3.  To promote closer integration between traditionally isolated teams;
4.  Define a plan for security automation;
5.  To determine risk tolerance by conducting risk/benefit analyses;
6.  Encourage developers to think about security by sharing visibility. *insights* about known threats and *Feedback*;
7.  Promote security training for developers;
8.  Maintain short and frequent development cycles;
9.  Integrate security measures with minimal disruption to operations;
10.  Integrate security scanners for [containers](/en/Docker-EE-tutorial/);
11.  Keeping up-to-date with the evolution of DevOps culture, such as cloud, containers, and... [microservices](/en/the-advantages-of-microservices-architecture/);
12.  Introduce [API Gateways](/en/API-gateway-or-API-management/) Secure in its reference architecture;
13.  Automate the tests;
14.  Isolate containers running microservices from each other and from the network;

## Here are some KPIs to take your DevSecOps initiatives to the next level:

In theory, the benefits of adopting a DevSecOps approach are easy to explain. However, in practice, due to the pain involved in changing how your organization traditionally performs tasks, it's important to use effective metrics to show how the changes are improving business operations. 
Here are some of the key performance indicators that companies should consider implementing to measure success:

- **Risk point per device –** This metric monitors the number of unpatched vulnerabilities per server. They should be prioritized according to their criticality. Over time, the number of vulnerabilities should decrease – especially the critical ones and those most exposed to internet attacks.

- **Number of continuous delivery cycles per month** This is an important metric to help you understand how quickly you can deploy code changes to your production environment. Adopting a DevSecOps approach should increase the number of delivery cycles your company can manage. Ideally, organizations should be able to handle a delivery cycle every two weeks if they follow the agile development cycle, but the more the better.

- **Number of software defects per line of code –** It's important to measure the number of vulnerabilities discovered through static and dynamic security code analysis to verify the effectiveness of your security practices. Remember that any short-term increase may be linked to more effective detection processes – in other words, the fact that you are improving at detecting flaws before your custom code is promoted to production is generating this high number of vulnerabilities discovered.

- **Reducing the hours spent resolving security issues:** This KPI measures the hours spent on security in *pipeline*These include how many people handle security in the DevOps environment and how much time they spend on manual testing or validation. The more automated security becomes, the smaller these numbers will be. At a minimum, they should confirm and align with the KPIs that measure return and delay – that is, the time spent when things go wrong. Otherwise, it means you're not automating enough.
