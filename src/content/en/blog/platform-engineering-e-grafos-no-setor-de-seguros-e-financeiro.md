The insurance and financial sector is under intense pressure to innovate rapidly, serve customers with agile digital services, and meet increasingly stringent regulatory requirements. This combination creates an environment of high operational complexity, in which efficiency becomes as important a requirement as security.

At the same time, sophisticated fraud is increasing in volume and sophistication, exploiting vulnerabilities in systems and gaps in digital integrations. Fraud prevention in insurance and financial services has ceased to be a reactive task and now requires intelligent, integrated solutions capable of operating in real time.

In this scenario, Vertigo Tecnologia offers a clear path: uniting Platform Engineering through the VeeCode Platform and graph databases, [**such as Neo4j**](/en/Public-sector-data-governance-with-neo4j/) , to transform complexity into efficiency and effectively prevent fraud.

## **Challenges in the sector regarding efficiency and combating fraud.**

### **Organizational silos that limit innovation**

Many institutions in the insurance and financial sectors still face internal silos that hinder innovation. Development, security, and business teams operate in a fragmented way, with little integration and manual processes. This disconnect leads to rework, increased costs, and delays strategic deliverables.

Without integration between areas, the digital product development cycle becomes slower and more prone to errors. In a competitive environment like FSI, this limitation represents a loss of market share and difficulty in meeting the needs of increasingly demanding customers.

### **Manual processes that reduce productivity and increase risks.**

Many processes are still conducted manually, whether in setting up environments, managing access, or responding to incidents. This approach consumes team time, generates inconsistencies, and increases exposure to security risks.

Automation is essential for reducing costs, increasing predictability, and ensuring that operations are always aligned with safety and compliance policies. In the insurance and financial sectors, automation is no longer a differentiator but a minimum requirement.

### **Sophisticated frauds that defy traditional models.**

Fraud has evolved beyond simple manipulation attempts. Today, entire networks of fraudsters exploit digital integrations, legacy systems, and API vulnerabilities to create complex schemes to divert resources.

Traditional database models, structured in relational tables, have difficulty identifying hidden connections and suspicious patterns distributed across multiple sources. This scenario demands a graph-based approach, capable of analyzing complex relationships in real time and identifying risks before they cause significant impact.

## **The power of Platform Engineering in the insurance and financial sector.**

### **Concept of Internal Developer Platforms (IDPs)**

Platform Engineering emerges as a response to increasing operational complexity. It is based on the creation of [**Internal Developer Platforms (IDPs)**](/en/Veecode-platform/) , which provide teams with a centralized environment to develop, integrate, and manage solutions more efficiently.

With an IDP (Individual Development Plan), developers gain autonomy, governance becomes clearer, and delivery flows are standardized. The result is greater agility, reduced risk, and improved collaboration between departments.

### **Benefits: automation, governance, and self-service.**

A well-structured IDP offers automation of repetitive tasks, integrated governance, and self-service resources for developers. This reduces the cognitive load on teams, allowing them to focus on innovation and generating value for customers.

For institutions in the insurance and financial sectors, these benefits translate into a greater ability to respond quickly to regulatory demands, create new digital products, and maintain secure and auditable environments.

## **Graphs as a differentiating factor in fraud prevention.**

### **Limitations of relational databases in detecting complex patterns.**

Relational databases are efficient for storing tabular information, but they face difficulties when the goal is to identify complex connections between multiple elements. In sophisticated fraud, these connections often go unnoticed in traditional structures.

This limitation creates high risks in the insurance and financial sectors, where fraud can involve multiple policies, chained transactions, and indirectly connected false identities.

### **Neo4j application for real-time network and anomaly analysis.**

Neo4j allows you to map relationship networks in real time, analyzing connections between accounts, devices, policies, and transactions. This relational view makes it possible to identify anomalous behaviors, such as circular transfers, the creation of multiple linked accounts, or suspicious connections between clients and service providers.

In the insurance sector, for example, Neo4j helps identify claims fraud by correlating patterns in seemingly unrelated requests. In financial institutions, it allows for tracking money laundering networks or the misuse of credit.

This integration ensures that insights gained in real time are applied directly to critical processes such as credit granting, claims analysis, and monitoring of financial transactions.

The result is greater protection, reduced losses, and increased confidence from customers and partners.
