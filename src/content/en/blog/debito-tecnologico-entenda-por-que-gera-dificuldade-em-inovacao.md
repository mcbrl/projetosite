Technological debt is still very much felt today in companies of all sizes, such as banks, start-ups, and even so-called "born cloud" companies, that is, those that were born in an environment conducive to digital innovation.

This technological debt brings several disadvantages to companies, leaving them with a significant deficit when it comes to digital transformation. Therefore, identifying and resolving this problem is very important and needs to be done as soon as possible.

## What is Technology Debt and what difficulties does it bring to companies?

Technical Debt, in Portuguese, débito tecnológico, represents the debt a company has in the area of ​​technological development and innovation. It is important to understand that combating technological debt should permeate the IT management of companies, but often it is not clearly included in the strategic context of organizations due to difficulties in convincing the executive board about the risks and costs involved in accepting technological debt.

According to a study conducted in June 2021 by OutSystems – a consulting and research firm – more than 60% of IT leaders identify technology debt as a major threat to a company's ability to innovate.

Furthermore, McKinsey – an American business consulting firm that advises companies, governments, and other organizations on strategic consulting – conducted a study in 2020 with 50 CIOs from companies in the financial and technology sectors with revenues exceeding one billion dollars and found that almost half of these companies that completed modernization programs were unsuccessful in reducing their technology debt.

This outcome is linked to product delays, hidden risks, increased costs, and even talented engineers leaving in frustration.

These studies open our eyes to the Tech Debt Score (TDS), a metric that offers companies a quick way to quantify their technical debt and, at the same time, compare it to their direct competitors. 

**There is a significant correlation between technology debt and business performance.** According to these studies, it has been concluded that reducing technology debt can help redirect resources to initiatives that will increase company revenue.

Last year, banking institutions were (and many still are) racing against time to adapt to the new demand for online platforms to create Open Banking. Another example is the famous Pix system, which became super popular worldwide and also presented a major challenge for the financial sector. This is all because most of these institutions had a significant technological debt, even without realizing it.

This problem is often only noticed when there is a delay in releasing new technology features, when there is a high turnover rate, or even when architects and technology specialists belatedly realize the operational and security risks the company is facing.

## But why does technological debt create difficulties in innovation and the creation of new products?

Companies with technology debt will face difficulties when creating new technology features for their business, experiencing significant delays in their completion.

Other very common factors in companies that have this type of debt are:

- **high turnover rate:** When companies lack technology and innovation, employees and technicians end up leaving for other opportunities that will add more value to them professionally, generating high turnover.

- **Perception of operational risk through the lens of architects and technology specialists:** Outdated systems are more susceptible to hacker threats, which can lead to data leaks and have a significant impact on the organization.

- **Operational and security risks, such as difficulty managing and controlling access to systems, and compliance issues.** If you don't have a good security tool like Zero Trust Security (which we will present below), you are vulnerable to data loss and intrusions, since there is no control over your employees and who accesses company data.

- **difficulty in innovating, updating, or maintaining their systems:** Outdated and outdated systems end up losing ground in a world that updates so rapidly. This means that organizations that fail to update end up falling behind.

- **Difficulty in migrating to the Cloud environment:** It often happens that the systems used are legacy (i.e., outdated, old) and, therefore, do not have good integration with the cloud environment, making migration difficult.

- **Low demand for professionals with expertise:** This point is closely related to high turnover. Just as professionals in your company tend to look for other places, those who are already working elsewhere will not be interested in joining your company. Consequently, your team ends up becoming less and less specialized.

- **Complications when creating new products:** Innovation, technology, and digital transformation as a whole are completely linked to the creation of new products. If your organization cannot keep up-to-date, it will not be able to create a competitive product that attracts the public's attention and is marketable.

***Technology debt is a major obstacle to digital transformation.***

### Digital transformation and technology debt

Technology debt prevents companies from completing their digital transformation. This happens because of difficulties in identifying problems and, sometimes, due to potential fraud in the workplace, and because they are already obsolete or legacy systems that lack effective security processes.

## Zero Trust Security: the security everyone should have.

Zero [Trust Security](/en/projects/The-importance-of-zero-trust-security-when-migrating-to-microservices./) is a network security model. Using it, no person or device inside or outside an organization's network will receive access to connect to IT systems or services until they are authenticated and continuously verified.

The Zero Trust model is based on five basic principles:

- All users on a network are always considered hostile.
- External and internal threats exist on the network at all times.
- Network location alone is not sufficient to determine network reliability.
- Each device, user, and network flow is authenticated and authorized.
- Policies should be dynamic and calculated from as many data sources as possible.

With it, it would be much easier to maintain security, even if minimal. However, companies with technological debt often haven't even heard of this security model.
