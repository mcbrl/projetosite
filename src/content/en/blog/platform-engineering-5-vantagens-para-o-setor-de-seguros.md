## What is Platform Engineering and how can it be useful?

[**Platform Engineering**](/en/Platform-engineering-and-its-relationship-with-DevOps%3A-learn-how-to-implement-it./) is a strategic approach that seeks to create and manage standardized and highly scalable digital platforms to meet the needs of development and operations teams.

Essentially, it's about designing a "platform as a product," built to simplify the work of technical teams by providing a robust and automated environment for creating, testing, and implementing solutions.

Therefore, the central idea is to reduce the complexity faced by IT teams by integrating various tools, technologies, and workflows, allowing professionals to focus on developing business solutions instead of worrying about the underlying infrastructure.

In the insurance sector, where technology needs to handle large volumes of data, complex integrations, and strict regulatory compliance, Platform Engineering becomes an indispensable ally. It combines practices such as [**DevOps**](/en/Process-automation-in-the-public-sector%3A-the-impact-of-DevOps-on-government-modernization./) , infrastructure automation, and software engineering best practices to provide a technological foundation that supports efficient and agile operations.

This approach not only improves the productivity of IT teams, but also accelerates the time-to-market of new solutions, ensuring that insurers can respond quickly to the demands of a competitive market.

## Platform Engineering: 5 Reasons Why the Healthcare Insurance Sector Should Benefit

The health insurance sector faces specific challenges that demand robust and scalable technological solutions. From integrating large volumes of data to implementing technological innovations, insurers need an approach that simplifies operations, improves the customer experience, and ensures regulatory compliance.

Next, we explore five technical arguments about how and why the health insurance sector can benefit from adopting Platform Engineering.

### 1 – Integration of Complex and Multi-sectoral Data

In the health insurance sector, managing data from diverse sources, such as hospitals, clinics, laboratories, and the policyholders themselves, is a critical and challenging task. This data, often sensitive and fragmented, needs to be reliably integrated in compliance with regulations such as the LGPD (Brazilian General Data Protection Law), ensuring security and privacy.

Adopting Platform Engineering enables the creation of automated data pipelines that not only process large volumes of information in real time, but also ensure the consistency and integrity of that information. This significantly reduces manual errors and improves data-driven decision-making, which is essential for customizing services and optimizing internal processes.

### 2 – Process Automation to Speed ​​Up Authorizations and Reimbursements

Speed ​​is one of the main factors in customer satisfaction in health insurance, especially with regard to the approval of medical procedures and the reimbursement of expenses.

However, many insurance companies still rely on manual and bureaucratic processes, resulting in delays and dissatisfaction. The use of an IDP (Insurance Product Development) allows these processes to be automated, orchestrating integrations between legacy systems and modern solutions.

With this, steps such as validations, authorizations, and payments can be carried out more quickly and reliably, improving the efficiency and experience of development teams. This automation reduces the workload of technical and administrative teams, allowing them to focus on more strategic and innovative activities. As a result, companies gain more operational agility, which can positively impact the end customer experience.

### 3 – Scalability to Manage High Transaction Volumes

Periods of high demand, such as pandemics or seasonal outbreaks, place enormous pressure on health insurance systems. The exponential increase in the volume of requests, such as authorizations, consultations, and reimbursements, can cause instability, delays, and even service interruptions.

A solution based on Platform Engineering solves this problem by providing automated horizontal scalability, using modern technologies such as [**Kubernetes**](/en/Kubernetes-Ingress%3A-Check-out-the-best-usage-practices./) and cloud computing. This means that the infrastructure can dynamically adjust to handle the extra load, ensuring that systems remain operational and that customers are not affected, regardless of transaction volume.

### 4 – Ease of Implementing Technological Innovations

Technological advancements in healthcare, such as the popularization of telemedicine and remote monitoring devices via IoT, require insurance companies to rapidly adapt their operations. However, integrating these innovations into existing systems can be complex and time-consuming.

The use of Platform Engineering provides a flexible and modular foundation that facilitates the implementation of new technologies, allowing innovative services to be launched more quickly and at a lower cost.

Therefore, this approach reduces development and integration time, ensuring that insurance companies are always ahead of market demands and can serve their customers with modern and effective solutions.

### 5 – Reduction of Operational Costs and Technical Overload

Maintaining legacy systems and [**managing**](/en/DevSecOps%3A-How-can-public-agencies-protect-their-cloud-infrastructure/) customized infrastructures requires significant investments, both financial and in terms of time and effort from IT teams. This situation is compounded by the need to hire highly qualified specialists to handle the technical complexity of outdated systems.

With Platform Engineering, it's possible to automate routine tasks, standardize workflows, and implement practices like Infrastructure as Code (IaC), drastically reducing the need for manual intervention.

The result is a leaner operation, which reduces operational costs and frees up technical teams to focus on strategic projects, such as innovation and improving the customer experience.
