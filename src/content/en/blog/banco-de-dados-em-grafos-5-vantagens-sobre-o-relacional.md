Graph databases are an alternative to traditional relational databases, as they are designed to store and manage data with complex relationships, such as social networks, recommendation systems, navigation routes, and more. Their main difference lies in the use of nodes and edges to represent data and their relationships, rather than tables and rows.

Therefore, graph databases are highly scalable and can handle large amounts of data and complex queries. They are also flexible and can be easily adapted to meet the specific needs of a project.

However, graph databases can be more complex to manage than traditional relational databases. They require a deeper understanding of graph theory and may demand more hardware resources to execute complex queries. Furthermore, selecting a suitable graph database can be challenging, as there are several options available on the market.

## Graph databases vs. relational databases

Check out some differences between the two types of databases and see which one best suits your company's needs.

**5 characteristics of a relational database**

1 – Based on a tabular structure, where data is organized into tables with rows and columns, each with a defined schema.

2 – Prioritizes data entities, focusing on assigning specific importance and attention to certain data entities, optimizing management and efficient use of system resources.

3 – It has difficulties with horizontal scaling adjustment, due to limitations in efficiently distributing data on a large scale, impacting scalability.

4 - It is not very flexible for representing and storing data with complex relationships.

5 – It follows the strict ACID model, which is important in scenarios where data integrity and operational accuracy are paramount, such as in financial systems, reservation systems, and other critical applications.

**5 characteristics of graph databases**

1 – Based on nodes, edges, and properties, highlighting the relationships between various data entities.

2 – It prioritizes the relationships between entities, highlighting the ability to represent and store data effectively through the connections and interactions between different nodes, providing a flexible approach to modeling complex relationships.

3 – It excels at horizontal scaling and can be easily scaled to handle large volumes of data.

4 – It enables the efficient creation and querying of complex interconnections, making them ideal for representing and storing data in ways that relational databases cannot.

5 – It allows the application of specialized algorithms for paths, communities, and other relationships, providing valuable insights in cases of social network analysis, community detection, and traffic flow analysis.

## What are the main uses of a graph database?

**1 – Recommendation systems:** efficient in delivering accurate recommendations, graph databases become a favorable choice for recommendation systems for products, services, and content. Graphical modeling facilitates the identification of affinities and preferences.

**2 – Fraud detection:** the usefulness of graph databases stands out in the detection of financial crimes, offering an effective approach to analyzing transactions to identify suspicious patterns. This application contributes significantly to financial security.

**3 – Traffic flow analysis:** By enabling the analysis of traffic flow, graph databases provide valuable insights for managers and CEOs. This ability to visualize and understand movement patterns is crucial for informed decision-making.

**4 – Discovering hidden patterns:** Especially adapted for discovering hidden patterns and complex relationships in data, graph databases are essential tools for revealing insights that might otherwise go unnoticed. This capability enhances strategic decision-making.

**5 – Data integration:** In integrating data from diverse sources, graph databases prove useful by enabling more efficient analysis. This application favors the creation of a comprehensive and cohesive view of the data.
