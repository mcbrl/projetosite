As companies adopt new cloud technologies, they begin to face several challenges. This is mainly because there are disagreements between leaders and developers about what the priorities are – or should be – for cloud-native migrations.
There are several practices that developers want IT leaders to understand and apply to address the challenges faced by companies undergoing this transition. [cloud-native technologies](/en/Discover-the-6-pillars-for-building-cloud-native-applications./).
In this article, we've compiled some best practices recommended by IT professionals to prepare your organization for success. Enjoy your reading!

## Understand what technology cannot achieve.

A common discussion among developers is that leaders need to understand that cloud computing isn't magic. In other words, solving business problems doesn't depend solely on it. 
Migrate applications to [containers](/en/Learn-about-serverless-BAAS-FAAS-and-container./) One example is serverless architecture, but it doesn't necessarily save your organization money or free up work time for your developers. Spend some time with your team and understand what cannot be solved purely with technical solutions.
Put your business goals first and think about how technology can best serve each of them, but without believing that its implementation alone will solve everything.

## Finance your projects correctly.

Migrating legacy applications from your data center to the public cloud or Kubernetes cluster will be a wasted effort if you can't take full advantage of that infrastructure. 
This means dedicating the necessary resources to redesigning or replacing applications. Otherwise, infrastructure spending may end up being wasted.

## Create new applications in microservices.

Os [microservices](/en/How-to-ensure-your-microservices-are-functioning-correctly./) They allow their developers to move forward more quickly, working autonomously on different features without worrying about breaking other teams' code. 
It's also possible to prevent your teams from replicating each other's work, because any application can utilize a well-designed microservice, regardless of where it was created.
This means software. **more reliable**built **more quickly** by a team of developers **more satisfied** with your work. 
Start by creating new applications with a microservices-oriented architecture. As your teams reorient themselves toward this new way of building, it will be easier to reorganize existing applications to microservices.

## Stop feeding your infrastructure.

Does letting your developers build and maintain a custom authentication system add value to your business? Is it really necessary to maintain all those databases? 
Too often, IT teams get bogged down in maintaining applications that could easily be outsourced to cloud providers, allowing them to redirect their efforts to more valuable work.
It's necessary to make a clear assessment of your IT landscape and think about what should be done internally and what shouldn't. In some cases, outsourcing a service may be more expensive, but it's worthwhile precisely to free up personnel for more innovative operations.

## Reorganize your teams whenever necessary.

As development teams refocus on building microservices and outsource more commodity tasks, it may be necessary to change the composition of your team.
Some developers, for example, may become responsible for microservices used by several different departments. Others, however, may find that managing cloud services is a complete task in itself.
Similarly, the shift from the classic development process to more agile processes using CI/CD will likely also require organizational changes. Don't keep your organizational chart static while your build process becomes dynamic.

## Combine configuration with automation.

One of the biggest differences between developers and IT leaders is that the former often prefer the control that comes from highly configurable frameworks, while the latter frequently find it more desirable for their organizations to migrate as much as possible to total automation. 
It is important that everyone understands the trade-offs involved in both cases and makes firm decisions according to each situation. There may be good technical reasons to maintain more control and customization of a particular application. In other cases, management will need to make it clear to its developers that some features are simply not a high priority and can be outsourced.

## Conclusion

We can summarize these best practices in a single word: **communication**The shift towards cloud-native environments is one of the biggest in the history of software development. 
IT leaders need to work closely with technical teams to ensure everyone understands the business objectives and how the technology strategy fits into each situation or moment.
Cloud-native technology requires investment in a [A new way of thinking about IT.](/en/Transform-your-corporate-IT-into-an-innovation-enabler-with-cloud-native-technology./)with new construction paradigms. However, many conventional concepts remain necessary, such as good design and automated testing.
