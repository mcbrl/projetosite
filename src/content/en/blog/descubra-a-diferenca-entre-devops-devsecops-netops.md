Each area of ​​IT has its own terms and acronyms. Often, their meanings tend to vary depending on the professional you're talking to, due to their corporate background. Despite this, DevOps, DevSecOps, and NetOps have their particularities that allow for differentiation.
Sometimes, acronyms can even overlap, causing even more confusion. Therefore, when it comes to definitions, it's always helpful to ask a variety of people from different areas of technology how they would define these terms.
In this article, we will explore the basic differences between each of the approaches and their use cases. Enjoy your reading!

## What is NetOps?

Although not a very common term, as infrastructure shifts its focus from hardware to software, it is becoming more prominent. **NetOps is the team that works with similar principles and standardized practices in resilient infrastructure models.**
Since infrastructure is the silent backbone of all business operations, the scope is somewhat broader to include not only the infrastructure, operations, and technology teams, but also departments with different access needs and requirements.
NetOps includes security, compliance and management, change management, and many other teams. The idea is that, as a company, everyone is working together to create a more secure and privacy-focused solution.
The goal of everyone involved is for the structure to function efficiently, meeting all the requirements to enable consumers to enjoy cutting-edge technology in the best possible way.

## What is DevOps?

[**DevOps**](/en/Why-the-world%27s-most-innovative-companies-adopt-DevOps./) **It is a combination of practices and tools that help an organization increase its ability to continuously and efficiently evolve and improve products.**as well as providing high-speed IT services.
It is a **crop** Essentially, it has a team of developers who design, build, implement, and maintain all types of software. The goal is to perform this work efficiently and in a way that aligns with the standards defined by the organization.
Because it is a **holistic approach**Speed ​​and security are part of it, and developers work based on these two pillars.

## What is DevSecOps?

[**DevSecOps**](/en/devsecops/) **This means incorporating security into the agility of DevOps, right from the beginning of the software development lifecycle.**so that the value of the deal is delivered with the least possible risk.
In this approach, development teams carry out the [best security practices](/en/Devsecops-and-its-main-performance-indicators./) with the goal of creating resilient software for consumers. All solutions must include security and privacy from the outset to be considered holistic and inclusive.
Creating something different from this does not adequately meet the requirements and needs of the digital age. After all, it is necessary to respect the confidential information that teams have access to through the software.
