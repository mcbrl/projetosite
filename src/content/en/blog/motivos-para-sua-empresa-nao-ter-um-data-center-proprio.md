**Data centers** have historically established themselves as the best locations for storing information and running systems. The world's largest banks – a market that highly values ​​security – have their own dedicated spaces to house the machines that store their information.

Nowadays, companies have the possibility of using adaptable structures in public clouds (such as AWS or Google), but even so, many companies still follow the more traditional way of doing technology.

When you look at your PC tower or your laptop, you might get the impression that computers are sturdy, low-cost structures. However, when it comes to a data center, we're talking about a large amount of equipment that is much more sensitive, complex, and expensive.

A data center has operational requirements such as:

### **Organization** **and Security**

As we said, a data center is a very sensitive structure. Because of this, this type of structure needs certain prerequisites to operate.

Investments are needed in a good machine cooling system, humidity control, sound insulation, and a technical team for maintenance.

### **Dining Room**

Some data centers are truly monumental projects. One example is the one being built in Fortaleza/CE by Angola Cables. The construction will occupy an area equivalent to a football field, with 9 square meters. And this is just one example that can be considered average compared to other data centers around the world.

Properly arranging the systems requires a large amount of space, since computers and all the infrastructure they need demand room.

The items mentioned above give us an idea of ​​the complexity of these spaces. Computers require a great deal of care, and accidents often occur in these types of structures for various reasons.

Let's mention some problems that can occur and have already occurred. Some are quite curious.

### **Sound vibration**

Computers are sensitive to sound vibrations. Don't believe it? A curious incident occurred in Romania in 2016 during a fire drill at the ING Bank data center – one of the country's leading banks. The problem was caused by the high pressure at which a refrigerant gas was released in the server room. The loud bang generated during the gas release damaged the hard drives in the area.

Several customers have reported problems making payments and using internet banking.

### **Fire**

A 13-story building, which houses the data center for several companies, caught fire this year. Several companies, including Unimed, experienced disruptions to their services.  

The server storing the Internacional club's database was also located there . The fire caused so much disruption that it temporarily prevented the check-in and ticket sales services for the Gre-Nal derby. Imagine the "happiness" of the fans unable to access one of the most hotly contested football matches in the country?

### **Human Error**

Information systems, however automated they may be, still eventually depend on human intervention. Sometimes failures occur due to poor system configuration or mishandling of the infrastructure, due to technical issues.

However, often, trivial things like unintentionally bumping into a cable can cause problems in the system. This type of problem occurs even more frequently in environments with makeshift repairs, such as wire splices, making the structures fragile.

## **Successful companies that outsource their data centers.**

Gartner – **a** consulting firm – predicts that global public cloud revenue will grow **21,4%** in 2018. This reflects the world waking up to new ways of managing its businesses, running its systems in the cloud, for example.

A bank needs to manage banking operations, Spotify needs to play your music, and Netflix makes your audiovisual content available. All these companies have their specific market niches and manage their systems in the cloud.

The advantage of this, besides the cost, is that you only need to worry about your core business. There's no need to create an IT department within your company to manage a data center. The focus is entirely on your business.

In conclusion, we would like to draw attention to the risks and obstacles of having your own infrastructure, as well as the techniques for migrating to the cloud.
