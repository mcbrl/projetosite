There are internet users who are robots (bots), some search engines (like Google) and others not; they are digital tools that perform tasks without human intervention! Furthermore, there are different types of bots. And most surprisingly, they already make up the majority of online traffic. How does this happen? Why does this happen? How does this affect visits to your company's website? Is it possible to separate human visits from those made by robots? These are some of the questions we will clarify in today's article. Keep reading!

## What are the online traffic percentages?

First of all, it's good to know that there are several types of bots: search engine robots (which organize information on the internet), spyware programs, scrapers (content thieves), hacker attack programs, spam comments, and impersonators (which slow down competing websites). All of them help increase internet traffic when they interact with web pages, downloading and uploading files.
[According to Incapsula](http://g1.globo.com/tecnologia/noticia/2013/12/mais-de-60-do-trafego-da-internet-nao-e-produzido-por-seres-humanos.html)According to a study that analyzed 1,45 billion accesses over 90 days, there are approximately 2 billion people connected to the internet. However, 61,5% of that traffic is generated by bots. You can imagine the share that robots account for! But this share also has other subdivisions: 20% for search engine robots, 19% for spyware, 5% for scrapers, 5% for attack programs used by hackers, and 2% for spam comments.

## Why are bots the majority?

Several simple facts explain why bots dominate the traffic. The first is the significant increase in the use of search engines by human users. This is quite predictable in an era where... [Information is vital.](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/o-governo-os-cidadaos-e-os-dados/)The importance of information also leads hackers to program more malicious bots to try and steal and use more data that generates real-world benefits. And this also increases the number of robots browsing around.
The second fact is that robots don't have human biological needs, such as eating and sleeping. Therefore, they can browse the internet twenty-four hours a day without interruption. So, during that time, they jump from computer to computer. Some do good deeds. Others, not so much.

## How does this affect website visits?

In the previous post, we talked about "Why should I monitor my business's online performance?" and the big answer is knowing exactly what's happening with your application in real time. It's not just malicious bots that harm a company's website. More robots than humans visiting a virtual address can have much more serious consequences. For example, a company's marketing department that wants to measure indicators such as the number of visitors, or that wants to calculate profitability or conversion (from leads to customers), will be completely at the mercy of false figures, and this is a problem for those who need to prove results.
These actions generate a high referral rate, but they are usually spam, and Google Analytics shows you this. It can hinder analysis and decision-making by planning departments because the indicators are unreliable, so be careful and filter your results!
