Accounts Payable/Accounts Receivable management, which encompasses both accounts payable and accounts receivable operations, is central to the financial operations of banks, fintechs, credit unions, and other institutions in the sector. It is the flow responsible for controlling inflows and outflows, honoring commitments to suppliers and partners, processing receivables, and ensuring the integrity of the financial cycle.

Despite its strategic importance, this area still accumulates structural inefficiencies that compromise operational performance, increase compliance risk, and hinder business scalability. This article maps the 7 most critical bottlenecks in AP/AR (Advanced Planning/Assessment of Financial Transactions) in the Brazilian financial sector and points out technological paths to overcome them.

## **Why AP/AR is still a critical point in the financial sector.**

The complexity of AP/AR in the financial sector goes beyond the volume of transactions. It is a direct result of the combination of legacy systems with low integration capacity, processes still dependent on human intervention, and an increasingly demanding regulatory environment. While other sectors have advanced in automating these routines, financial institutions face an additional challenge: modernizing without interrupting critical operations and without compromising compliance with Central Bank regulations, the LGPD (Brazilian General Data Protection Law), and CMN (National Monetary Council) resolutions.

It is in this context that bottlenecks persist, generating rework, hidden costs, and risks that often only become visible when the impact is already significant.

## **The 7 bottlenecks that compromise the efficiency of AP/AR.**

The bottlenecks in AP/AR (Accounting and Financial Management) in the financial sector rarely appear in isolation. In practice, they accumulate throughout the operation, often invisible until the cost is too high to ignore. Some originate from architectural choices made years ago. Others are the result of processes that grew without governance. What they all have in common is the direct impact on operational efficiency, regulatory compliance, and the institution's ability to scale with control. Below are the 7 most recurring bottlenecks.

### **1\. Manual financial reconciliation processes**

In many environments with legacy systems, reconciliation between internal records, bank statements, and partner data still relies on manual steps to close the loop. Even in institutions that have advanced in the digitization of channels, the AP/AR back office often operates with spreadsheets, exports, and point-to-point reconciliations. This model does not keep pace with the volume and speed of current digital operations, and each discrepancy identified outside the deadline represents a concrete financial and operational risk.

### **2\. Lack of integration between ERP, banking systems, and payment APIs.**

When financial management systems don't communicate with banking platforms and payment APIs in an automated way, the AP/AR flow becomes fragmented. Data needs to be exported, manually processed, and re-imported, which increases the risk of inconsistencies and lengthens the cycle time of each operation. The absence of a robust integration layer, such as a well-governed **[API Gateway](/en/APIS-insurance-and-finance-sector-innovation-with-Vertigo-technology/)** , is one of the main causes of this disconnect.

### **3\. Limited visibility of real-time cash flow.**

Without a consolidated and up-to-date view of AP/AR (Accounts Payable/Receivables Management), managers operate with outdated information. This compromises decision-making regarding advances, negotiations with suppliers, fundraising, and liquidity management. In the **[financial sector](/en/Top-10-pain-points-in-the-financial-sector-and-how-technology-can-solve-them./)** , where timing is an asset, the lack of real-time visibility is a bottleneck with a direct impact on competitiveness.

### **4\. Inefficient management of exceptions and disputes.**

Discrepancies in billing, payment disputes, and inconsistencies in invoices generate queues of exceptions that overwhelm back-office teams. Without automated handling and escalation workflows, these issues accumulate and can result in payment delays, contractual penalties, or conflicts with partners.

### **5\. Regulatory compliance as an operational bottleneck**

The requirements of the LGPD (Brazilian General Data Protection Law), the regulations of the Central Bank, and the resolutions of the National Monetary Council impose rigorous controls on the processing of financial data, the traceability of operations, and the documentation of processes. When the AP/AR (Accounting and Financial Management) is not structured with adequate governance, the compliance effort becomes a manual, repetitive process that is highly susceptible to errors. Compliance cannot be a side project; it needs to be embedded in the operational architecture.

### **6\. Rework due to failures in the approval chain.**

Poorly defined approval workflows, inconsistent approval levels, and a lack of automation in authorization routines lead to systematic rework. Documents are sent via email or spreadsheets, approvals are lost, and deadlines are missed. This scenario is especially critical in Public Administration, where delays in the approval chain directly affect supplier relationships and the cost of capital.

### **7\. Difficulty in scaling the operation without increasing risk.**

Customer base growth, increased transaction volume, or expansion into new financial products require that the AP/AR scale proportionally. When operations rely on manual processes or inflexible systems, scaling effectively means hiring more people, increasing exposure to errors, and raising operational costs. Sustainable scalability is only possible with automation, integration, and data-driven architecture.

## **The cumulative impact of these bottlenecks on the business.**

Individually, each of these bottlenecks already represents a significant problem. When combined, they form a cycle of inefficiency that raises operating costs, increases regulatory exposure, and reduces the institution's ability to respond quickly to market demands.

The impact manifests itself in various ways, including increased Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) and Days Sales Outstanding (DSO), higher transaction costs, difficulty in closing the financial cycle within expected timeframes, and greater reliance on manual intervention to correct system-preventable errors. In regulated environments, these problems also increase the risk of non-compliance, with potential consequences ranging from administrative penalties to loss of credibility with regulators and partners.

## **How technology breaks down these bottlenecks.**

Overcoming AP/AR bottlenecks requires a combination of process automation, API integration, data governance, and real-time monitoring. This approach eliminates the most high-risk manual steps, connects heterogeneous systems with traceability, and creates approval and reconciliation workflows that operate autonomously and are auditable.
