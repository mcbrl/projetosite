*By: Anne Durey and Vertigo*
What are the practical challenges of digitally transforming an existing company that, unlike a startup, has an IT department following traditional models? André Fernandes, CTIO of \[Company Name\] [Vertigo Technology](/en/), spoke about the subject during LDXC Online Week, promoted by [Liferay](https://www.liferay.com/pt/home) and held in May 2020. 

Fernandes pointed out that the process of identifying the challenges for digital transformation begins with what can be summarized in a phrase by Peter Drucker in the essay *Managing For Business Effectiveness* : "There is nothing more useless than doing with great efficiency that which should not be done at all." In other words, there is no point in insisting on traditional IT management practices when they prevent the delivery of value to the business.

Focusing on this, [digital transformation](/en/How-to-start-a-digital-transformation-without-repeating-the-mistakes-of-the-past./) should be the innovative use of digital technologies in the creation of new products and business models that are potentially disruptive and experience exponential growth.

## **
The role of corporate IT in digital transformation.**

“In organizations that already have an active corporate IT department, it is very common to find that this area is originally designed to maintain the status quo. One of the main characteristics of traditional IT is its aversion to change, as it is focused on stability and availability to the detriment of everything else,” stated Fernandes.
Other characteristics commonly found in traditional IT are:

- Viewing change as a source of operational risk: changing something is an act surrounded by lengthy rituals and mechanisms for shifting blame;
- Assuming that speed and quality cannot coexist, creating an aversion to fast deliveries;
- Making changes in large batches requires mobilizing enormous amounts of resources within inconvenient time windows.

From this perspective, three main vices commonly arise:

1.  **Operational silos:** These structures are formed when there is a team for each function (network, storage, security, etc.). Routine tasks such as provisioning an environment for a new application require opening multiple tickets for each of these teams, each with its own specific SLAs, which disregard the value chain.
2.  **Recurring friction:** Faced with operational silos, there will be friction between those who aim to deliver change and those who wish to maintain stability at any cost. A recurring situation is operations teams using availability as the primary metric, versus development teams, monitored only by the quantity of features delivered. "These two teams are held accountable for conflicting goals and will not be able to work as a team with the existing processes, and this becomes a problem of constant attrition," Fernandes pointed out.
3.  **Illusion of control:** By surrounding the change process with bureaucracies and SLAs that don't make sense for the value chain, traditional IT creates a false sense of control that discourages process improvements.

According to André, the consequences of these three vices are numerous: lack of prioritization of system patching, high average time to resolve problems, unpredictable deliveries, among others. “One of the prerequisites for digital transformation is to abolish these problems. Traditional IT not only hinders transformation, it is an enemy to be defeated,” stated André Fernandes.

## **3 main premises of the new IT management models** 

In contrast to this perspective, the modern IT world already assumes constant changes, even occurring several times a day and during peak hours. "This should be routine so that IT truly delivers value and can respond quickly to challenges," added the CTIO of Vertigo.
To do this, new IT management models must seek three basic premises:

1.  **Operational risk exists in the barriers against change:** The company needs to work on mitigating risk by reducing the average time to resolve problems. It needs to be quick both in detecting a vulnerability and delivering its fix.
2.  **Quality is synonymous with speed.** You will only deliver quality if you do so quickly and with visibility into the system's behavior. It's necessary to shorten feedback cycles to see the results of a change more quickly.
3.  **Changes should be frequent and minor:** The scope of the changes is smaller, and the company must implement them on a daily basis.

## **
How to do this?**

One of the main tools for modernizing traditional IT are self-service platforms that allow for the integrated provisioning, building, consumption, and monitoring of services. These platforms encourage experimentation and achieve a balance between innovation and maturity. Container orchestrators are examples of this type of platform, as are [DevOps](/en/benefits-of-having-a-continuous-delivery-conveyor-belt/) automation pipelines and Observability practices for delivering visibility to the team.

Changing team structures is also important. Operational silos should be transformed into knowledge areas, prioritizing the creation of autonomous and multidisciplinary product teams, even for the longest-running internal systems.

Another step towards process modernization is to use cloud-native architectures that allow for the redesign of infrastructure, systems architecture, and organizational culture to maximize operational gains.

[DevOps](/en/What-is-DevOps/) practices are extremely important for making teams more agile. Supported by the three pillars of agile culture, tools, and processes, this practice leads to continuous improvement models and great results from low-cost actions. Furthermore, it helps to share responsibilities by giving teams autonomy and automating repetitive tasks.
