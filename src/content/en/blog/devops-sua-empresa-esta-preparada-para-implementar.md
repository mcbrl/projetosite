DevOps aims for collaboration and integration between employees working in development and those responsible for infrastructure and IT.

But the big question is: **is your company ready to implement DevOps?**

Many organizations are implementing, or taking their first steps in, the Digital Transformation of their businesses, which represents a major impact on their processes and especially on their corporate culture.

All of this structural change can directly impact team management and productivity, which becomes a major challenge that DevOps has come to solve.

We prepared this article to address the main points of attention in implementing a DevOps culture and to analyze the maturity level of your company in adopting this methodology.

## Is the concept clear to your team?

Making sure the team understands what the DevOps methodology is is one of the most delicate factors during the implementation process.

When we talk about DevOps culture, we need to understand that it's still a very recent concept, which generates divergent opinions on the subject. Some believe that DevOps is a job title, or just a methodology, or even a new culture.

DevOps emerged from the need to improve IT deliverables through the evolution of the Agile methodology. The main focus is on collaboration and optimized integration between programmers and infrastructure professionals.

When implemented correctly, the delivery of software and services occurs quickly, reducing costs and increasing productivity.

We know that each person has their own way of understanding and absorbing information, so the understanding and level of learning of this new methodology will not be the same for all members within its structure.

Investing in cultural change and having the [right partner](/en/us/) during this transition process is the best way to ensure everyone is "on the same page."

DevOps creates the ideal environment for skills development, constructive feedback, and encouraging collaboration, thereby integrating teams, solving problems, and delivering quality results.

## What are the benefits of implementing DevOps?

Throughout this article, we've already discussed several advantages that implementing DevOps can provide, but we can summarize a few points to make it even clearer:

– Promotes integration between development and operations teams;

– Increased frequency and quality of deployments;

– Reduces the risk of failures;

– It promotes greater agility in deliveries, due to its agile methodology;

– Reduced project timelines, allowing deadlines to be met more efficiently;

– Correcting errors or flaws with greater accuracy and speed.

Yes, DevOps is the agent of change in companies, and implementing it is a challenging process because it promotes the integration of operations and development teams.
