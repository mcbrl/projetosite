Where is [DevOps](/en/devops/) going this year? Let's examine the key trends around topics such as training, metrics, and tools.

In 2019, we saw numerous successful [DevOps](/en/devops/) initiatives in companies of all sizes. If you were involved in any of them, congratulations! **But what can we expect for the future of DevOps in 2020?**

Expressing the impressions of leading thought leaders in the DevOps community worldwide, here are the top ten trends that will shape the current year:

## **1\. Agile and DevOps will increase collaboration between technology and business functions.**

Agile and [DevOps](/en/What-is-DevOps/) are two popular movements that started within the technology field, in many cases failing to detach themselves from it. The Agile methodology, however, has managed to be adopted in other functions, including finance, human resources, purchasing, and marketing, and some senior leaders are increasingly inviting their entire organizations to become agile.

This movement, on the other hand, so far doesn't seem to have helped the tech community much to join forces with their colleagues in these other roles. Due to the competitive pressure that the digital world is exerting on organizations, in 2020, the prediction is that we will begin to see greater collaboration between roles – with agility being the starting point for conversations.

Therefore, if this is your case, start by encouraging your teams to talk to people in different roles about their experiences using agile methods. Some helpful questions include: How are you using these methods? What are you doing? What is changing for you? What problems are you having? How can we work together to help solve some of these problems? These questions will help people in different roles get to know each other as individuals, and collaboration will certainly improve.

## **2\. Training and improving DevOps skills will become an organizational priority.**

DevOps requires experimentation with new technologies. A recent survey conducted with companies by the DevOps Institute revealed that 55% of participants prefer to hire their DevOps teams [from](/en/devops/) [within](/en/devops/) the organization.

Unfortunately, many companies lack the necessary skills to do this, and hiring new people may not be possible due to budget constraints. In this scenario, one possible approach is to create corporate universities for internal training. This measure was taken by the delivery service company "FedEx".

The company knew it lacked adequate skills within its engineering talent pool, and this led its CIO to initiate the "FedEx Cloud Dojo," which teaches its own engineers modern software development and technologies and functions as a university for FedEx. The university has trained over 2.500 software programmers.

Organizations that want to use DevOps to help drive their [digital transformation](/en/Digital-transformation%3A-are-you-ready/) must make drastic improvements in training, learning, and upskilling essential DevOps skills. And a more proactive pursuit of this is predicted for 2020.

## **3\. Upskilling and cross-skilling will lead to the T-shaped career path.**

Recognizing the talent market, organizations and individuals tend to invest heavily in upskilling and cross-skilling in order to meet the accelerating demands for new skills. While all IT professionals need to become more proficient in various domains, developers, in particular, will need to add a new breadth to their skill portfolio in areas such as testing, [containerization](/en/What-is-application-containerization/) , infrastructure, AI, and security.

There will also be a stronger emphasis on core (flexible) skills such as empathy, customer experience, and collaboration. Silos are beginning to diminish in many areas, and the need is for everyone to become T-shaped professionals – that is, with both depth and breadth of knowledge – to enable and support innovation.

All this training and collaboration between the parties will lead more workers to develop new technical and professional skills and personal qualities, adding new depth and capabilities to the individuals on their teams.

## **4\. More teams will shift their mindset from "job done" to "value delivered."**

Value stream mapping can help change the way your teams think about defining completed work, shifting from "I did my job" to "value is delivered." This is one of the most effective ways to change behaviors and get your teams thinking about the end-to-end lifecycle of the projects they are working on. This is why adopting Value Stream Management is critical in 2020.

With it, it will be possible to automate the results of Value Stream Mapping for continuous progress monitoring. This will allow a team to connect all parts of the [DevOps](/en/devops/) complex , uniting a set of tools with system-derived data based on cycle time. Teams that adopt Value Stream Management in 2020 will be able to base their next improvement experiments on data-driven decisions and priorities.

## **5\. The "war" of tools will get worse before it gets better.**

The sheer number of tools and frameworks in technology is staggering. The challenges that IT teams face in understanding, interconnecting, and applying them will continue to exist, and in 2020, there is no real solution in sight.

[Competition in the DevOps](/en/devops/) toolchain is also fierce and flourishing. Events and conferences are filled with technology sessions and best practices. Books, blogs, and videos are flooding email inboxes, and leaders are eager to share their knowledge. Furthermore, more open-source tools are emerging to integrate new technologies.

To survive the challenges of complexity, having an automation strategy is becoming increasingly important. As you work to develop this, don't lose sight of the real problems you're trying to solve and how to get there by leveraging your own teams.

## **6\. DevOps will become more measurable and metrics will be better defined.**

"What gets measured gets managed." This quote still holds true, more than 60 years after Peter Drucker referenced it in his book "The Practice of Management." What we all want to avoid, however, is measuring by metrics.

In the coming years of DevOps, the continuous improvement metrics we know today will continue to be the key ones that matter. In 2020, we expect more organizations to agree on exactly what should be measured, adopting these metrics more widely. Those seeking support can rely on the performance metrics described in the DevOps Research and Assessment (DORA) study, which cites five Software Delivery and Operational Performance (SDO) measures that can be used as key success indicators for high-performing DevOps teams.

The report's key benchmarks provide guidance on areas where teams should improve in 2020. Selecting these key metrics and filling in the data will provide insights into the value and journey of [DevOps](/en/devops/).

## **7\. DevOps teams will earn more and experience greater job satisfaction.**

Employees who pursue the DevOps path in 2020 will see direct benefits in terms of compensation and job satisfaction. Automation will allow employees to work on activities that add more value, instead of common manual tasks, resulting in greater job satisfaction and reduced stress levels.

[In particular, DevOps](/en/devops/) engineers who focus their work on automation and collaboration to improve software delivery can expect their salaries to be significantly higher than those of colleagues in traditional roles, such as systems administrators.

Investments in training and certifications also positively impact the quality of work and, therefore, can improve business outcomes. In turn, this can ultimately change the value equation in more organizations and give IT more strategic seats at the table. This will certainly lead to improvements in how other functional areas work with IT, both now and in the future.

## **8\. There may be service management and DevOps reunification.**

With the recent release of ITILv4, 2020 will be an interesting year for organizations adopting [DevOps and service management frameworks](/en/devops/) . Software product development and management require agile techniques, focusing on co-creating value in order to reduce waste. DevOps, service management, and other best practices such as SRE can coexist to align teams, meet stakeholder demands, and improve the value delivered.

Since digital transformation is not achieved instantly in an organization, established companies should start with best practices and methodologies suited to their needs, beginning small, and then learning and developing expertise that increases scalability.

## **9\. A new generation of IT takes over.**

The number of people who remember the days before DevOps is starting to dwindle. The younger generation of employees on today's IT and DevOps teams doesn't remember the strict silos, with clear lines around areas of responsibility such as infrastructure, operations, application design, development, testing, and security.

They don't remember how much transition work this caused between teams and groups. They don't know that product owners, business analysts, architects, developers, testers, release managers, system administrators, and infrastructure owners had to agree on and coordinate the planning, development, testing, deployment, operation, and management of a program. And if just reading that sentence is exhausting, imagine living through it.

When the 10th anniversary of DevOps was celebrated in 2019, we saw the wall between Dev and Ops come down. That's a reason for all of us to celebrate, and in 2020 we expect to see this happen as more and more organizations adopt DevOps.

## **10\. AI adoption will increase significantly, and DevOps teams should monitor this closely.**

AI and machine learning (ML) have recently been ranked as the most important enterprise technologies of the next decade, according to a recent ISACA report. Both will play a critical role in next-generation IT operations and [DevOps](/en/devops/) teams . AIOps will give DevOps teams the ability to analyze more data faster, allowing them to improve important processes, tasks, and decision-making. In 2020, expect to see more DevOps teams adopting these tools, which automate the processing of fast-moving volumes of data, use ML to analyze the data, and have the ability to leverage knowledge for automation or decision-making.

The AIOps market has already gained momentum, with 22% of IT companies using ML and data science as part of their work. The vendor landscape is broad, with a variety of leaders. This year, the AIOps market will continue its shift from a scientific project to the pilot and experimental stage.

There is still much work to be done in 2020 to help shape the future adoption of DevOps. This will require more than just a focus on tools and techniques. DevOps initiatives must be registered as change programs, demanding time, resources, and priority commitment from all business leaders. We expect some of these trends to materialize this year and for DevOps to be recognized as a new way of working.
