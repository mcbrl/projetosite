The use of **APIs** is revolutionizing the global market. Adoption of the technology is increasing, and companies are finding new ways to diversify and increase their business profits. The possibility of expanding market share and scaling systems are just some of the reasons why companies have their own communication interfaces. Strategies focused on gaining *customer* share can also be based on **APIs** , improving the user and customer experience. Let's embark on the world of **Public APIs** and **Private APIs**.

Defining the strategy for using **Application Programming Interfaces** ( **APIs** ) can be fundamental to shaping a company's future success. For this reason, knowing how to choose between a **Public API** and **a Private API** is essential.

First and foremost, it's important to highlight that each model is better suited to a particular type of business. There isn't one model that's inherently better than another. The purpose of this content is to guide your business, helping you understand which **API** is ideal for your industry. Let's take a look below!

## What is a public API?

\[alert style=”warning”\]Subscribe to our blog to be notified when we publish content like this!\[/alert\]

![](/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Open-APIs-v5.png)

A **public API** is an interface designed to be easily accessible to the wider web population and mobile developers. This means it can be used both by developers within the organization that published the API and by any external developer who wants access to the interface.

**Public APIs** improve companies' relationships with developers and create [new business models, increasing sales through APIs](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/aumente-suas-vendas-usando-api/) . This is because this technology generally aims to reach the growing community of free-agent application developers. This allows the organization to encourage the development of innovative applications or even innovative solutions that generate value for the core business, without directly investing in development efforts.

The success of a **public API** depends on its ability to attract external developers and help them create new functionalities and connections. Therefore, for business managers and interface designers, the fundamental objective should be to increase the quantity and quality of API usage. In this regard, there are some points worth considering, such as:

- **Having a specific target audience;**
- **To deliver an interface and documentation designed to meet the preferences of this audience;**
- **Conducting awareness-raising activities;**
- **Targeted materials.**

It's important to know that opening an interface to external developers can significantly increase the management and security challenges associated with that API. The increased security risks represent another major challenge for those starting to work with public APIs. This, theoretically, means that any developer can access exposed *back-end* systems.

We have some very famous examples of products that expose **a public API** . Check them out below:

### **Trello**

Trello is a process management tool that is very useful for countless companies around the world. It allows developers to amplify the application's capabilities themselves. It's possible to integrate with the **Trello API** or create custom features.

### **Facebook**

The world's largest social network has a wide variety of APIs. The functionalities revolve around providing data to generate *marketing* automation and charts. This data can be crucial for acquiring interesting metrics for a company's *marketing and sales teams.*

### **Salesforce**

Some point to the **Salesforce API** as the first. You're probably already familiar with its features. The company's CRM is one of the most widely used in the world as a sales force automation service.

Salesforce provides a series of APIs on its developer page that allow IT professionals to help their companies achieve better results. Salesforce's API starter kits include code samples illustrating the most common tasks developers perform in conjunction with the **Marketing** **Cloud API**.

### **Google Maps**

Google is a leading company in various sectors of the global market, and **APIs** are no exception. Companies from diverse market niches use **Google Maps** as a tool for executing their services.

Maps offers a maximum quota of free calls. Once that number is exceeded, you need to pay for packages to maintain access. Individuals can use the service as much as they want, but businesses usually have to pay for it due to the high number of calls.

## **What is a Private API?**

![](/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Private-APIs-v5.png)

A **Private API** is an interface that opens up parts of an organization's *backend* data, applications, and functionality so that developers can use the information and data. In this model, new applications created by development staff can be distributed publicly. However, in this type, the administrative **interface** is not available to anyone who does not work directly for the API publisher.

The goal of a **private API program** is to allow internal developers, who are building new applications, to leverage existing systems. Therefore, the needs and preferences of these developers must be taken into account by business managers. The interface teams implementing the program also need to be considered.

**Private APIs** can significantly reduce the time and resources required for various procedures such as:

- **Integrate internal systems;**
- **Building new systems;**
- **Increase internal connectivity and even productivity;**
- **Create mobile applications and more.**

These benefits extend the company's market reach and add value to the products it offers. Instead of creating applications in silos from scratch, developers can design from a common set of **internal software** assets , which is faster and cheaper.

### **Examples of private APIs.**

There are other considerations that need to be asked:

**How can we ensure that the program meets both of the organization's project goals?**

**Are there any future connectivity requirements?**

The issues above are vital for handling the ongoing management of any API program. This management is what will guarantee the security and performance of *back-end* systems.

Managing a **private API program** might seem easy, since the *interfaces* are only exposed to internal developers. This reduces security risks, giving API *designers* direct access to these developers and making it easier to create developer-friendly interfaces. However, it's important to remember that exposing **software interfaces** creates a number of security and management challenges.

For example, in many cases, these applications will communicate with APIs over the public internet or mobile networks. This will occur even if the applications are only for internal employee use. There are also challenges associated with systems that use different integration protocols and standards.

### Credit card rewards plans

There are programs like Dotz, Km de Vantagens, Smiles, and Multiplus that offer benefits based on the user's credit card spending, with the possibility of integrating this functionality with various commercial establishments.

The company that owns the "reward points" program offers companies the possibility to take advantage of these benefits through registration and evaluation of the API applicant.

### E-commerce companies

Many people who receive a product at home, after a few mouse clicks, don't imagine the number of steps involved in completing the transaction. **Private APIs** are undoubtedly key players in this process. This is because, through them, different departments of the company connect.

Different departments often use different control mechanisms. APIs are suitable for connecting the sales department with the warehouse, acting as an *interface* between different sectors.

### Internet Banking

Banks are still considered somewhat conservative companies regarding the "openness" of data. But, despite this stance, *internet banking* applications represent a major advancement in terms of service provision. Facing queues has become optional in many cases.

Bank APIs allow specific data to be accessed through applications that connect via **private APIs** , which are only available to users.

##  **Public APIs and Society**

It has become clear that the private sector can use APIs to explore new markets and products. But even regulatory bodies can mandate the availability of **APIs** in strategic sectors of the economy. The goal is to combat monopolies and cartels, stimulate competition, or even foster an entire ecosystem of innovative companies that create solutions for society. The most notable example today is *fintech* [and its potential for Open Banking](https://conteudo.startse.com.br/mercado/mariana-rodrigues/open-banking-europeu-pode-virar-exemplo-para-o-brasil/) initiatives.
