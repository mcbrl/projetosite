Regardless of the sector you work in, you've already learned this or will soon: the era of monolithic service architectures is over. In other words, the time of a complex system where all business functions were implemented in a single process is gone.

Therefore, as the need for greater agility takes priority, organizations of all types are migrating to microservices. A microservices architecture can offer a more scalable, resilient, and secure enterprise – while simplifying the process and time required to create and deploy working improvements to any application. Keep reading this article to understand how!

### **What are microservices?**

With the goal of having individual services capable of being deployed independently of one another, microservices architecture is a flexible and scalable approach to software programming. It is, therefore, the counterpoint to the traditional monolithic architecture, in which the entire program is developed and deployed as a single application. In contrast, microservices allow each service to be programmed and deployed on its own – a type of modular approach to building the finished program.

Similarly, obsolete microservices can be removed from the stack more easily, whereas for a monolithic application, it is more difficult and uneconomical to rewrite, as well as redesign or remove obsolete components.

### **What is the purpose of microservices?**

In the race to remain competitive, companies are increasingly adopting new IT methodologies: [Agile](/en/agile-software-development/) , [DevOps](/en/devops/) , continuous testing models, or some combination of these three. However, their monolithic IT structures simply cannot support these updates.

Thus, monolithic structures are more difficult to scale, update, and maintain, especially as a company grows and evolves. Furthermore, today's end users expect dynamic and consistent experiences across multiple devices. And to make this happen, organizations of all sizes are adopting a microservices architecture.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Vertigo-Post-Blog-Microservicos_Blog-Lk-1.png)

### **More opportunities for exclusive experiences.**

Microservices break down each application into smaller, interconnected sets of services, reducing the time it takes for an IT department to build, maintain, and update each one. Therefore, this gives any development team more opportunities to customize these unique end-user experiences while still adhering to the tighter schedules required by release schedules. [Agile](/en/agile-software-development/).

### **What are the benefits of microservices architecture?**

 In summary, the benefits of microservices architecture stem from its modular pattern and the flexibility that this creates:

- It's easier to add new microservices in order to expand functionality.
- Easier to deploy new versions of specific microservices without downtime.
- It's easier to work with individual microservices because the programming code is more contained.

**More advantages**
Furthermore, for very large applications and those with continuous deployment, microservices architecture helps keep the programming code digestible, making it easier for new team members to work on modules, and allowing existing team members to work independently on different services.

**Other advantages of microservices architecture**

- Faster deployment with reduced delivery time.
- Faster recovery from failed deployments.
- Reduced risk of downtime/interruptions due to deployment failures.

Microservices architecture, however, is not the only solution – and should be seen as a means to an end, not as the end itself.

### **Are microservices and SOA the same thing?**

SOA stands for Service-Oriented Architecture – and microservices are also clearly service-oriented in the broader sense. However, there are some important differences between SOA and microservices, see below:

- SOA uses independent software components, but it is not inherently modular in the same way that microservices are.
- Modifying the SOA may still require monolithic modifications, as well as downtime across the entire application.
- Microservices are generally more cloud-based than on-premises.
- Microservices can have independent storage; SOA services typically share data storage.
- Microservices are less restrictive in terms of standards and governance, with the purpose of giving development teams more options and control.

Microservices have been called "low-granularity SOA," which is therefore indicative of the similarities between the two architectures, as well as the lightweight and simplified nature of individual microservices.

### **What are the advantages of microservices?**

The ability of microservices to decompose a monolithic application into a set of independent services offers faster production, and therefore a more efficient workflow and sharp increases in resilience and productivity.

- **Faster deployment and scaling**

A microservices architecture accelerates each production cycle, thus allowing teams to develop, deploy, and scale services in parallel. Furthermore, it also helps them reduce the time needed to put changes or improvements into production.

- **Reduced downtime**

Parallel development reduces system downtime and increases performance, thus helping an organization get more from its investment. Similarly, greater autonomy gives teams more agility to simplify and maintain code for any given service.

- **High Availability**

Microservices are able to disperse functionality across multiple services in order to prevent any single point of failure from disabling the application. If a component dies, teams can simply create another as the application continues to function. Furthermore, these shorter cycles and reduced downtime improve productivity, making it easier to update and ship within the tighter Agile production sprints. In other words, this gives companies more opportunities to drive improvements that can increase user engagement.

**Microservices as an API gateway**
A microservices API gateway is a proxy that mediates traffic from one service to another. Therefore, the goal is to make your microservices respond as a customized API to handle a client's unique requests. Think of it like an air traffic controller, directing client information to the underlying microservice.

An API gateway can serve as a single point of entry for the client into the system, as it performs services such as:

- Routing or proxy requests to the appropriate service/services/service groups.
- Responding to specific requests, spreading them across a customized range of services, and aggregating their results.
- Translating between different databases or protocols

All of this is conducted in a way that makes it seem like a universal point of contact with the application.

### **Are web services microservices?**

Microservices and web services are broadly similar, but their definitions differ subtly. Web services are simply services accessed via the web, that is, usually via the HTTP protocol, and increasingly many of them are deployed as standalone or modular microservices.

On the other hand, microservice architecture is more about how the application is built and deployed – its form, not its function. Web services can be microservices and microservices can be web services, although this is not necessarily the case 100% of the time.

### **What problems can arise when using microservices?**

While microservices offer [DevOps](/en/devops/) greater autonomy and accountability, they also increase the complexity between systems and their practices. Therefore, teams accustomed to legacy systems and monolithic architectures can expect a certain amount of "culture shock."

Teams should expect greater complexity in discovery, as well as monitoring, testing, and service networking. Therefore, these teams are tasked with maintaining performance parity across each service and managing the flow of information throughout the enterprise. For example, these new or expanded duties include:

- Addressing network latency
- Reduce fault tolerance.
- load balancing
- Engage with various message formats.
- Orchestration across multiple teams and services.

Consistent communication with (and updates from) other teams, as well as internal stakeholders.
Therefore, the sooner teams become accustomed to the requirements of a microservices architecture, the lower the risk of service duplication and the sooner... The benefits can be enjoyed.

### **Why is open source important for microservices?**

Microservices applications feature dozens, even hundreds, of separate components. They typically run under rapid production cycles and tight budgets. Therefore, rigid, centralized, and "black box" development methods simply don't offer the flexibility and adaptability that open source provides.

Furthermore, because open-source solutions allow your applications to be modular from the start, they free up your [DevOps](/en/devops/) team to customize and extend your applications. Therefore, any team can develop the service, modifying the open-source code to fix or improve it. The result: greater opportunity for innovation and speed of resolution.
