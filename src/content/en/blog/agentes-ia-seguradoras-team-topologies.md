The insurance sector is undergoing a period of accelerated transformation. The pressure for digitalization, coupled with growing customer expectations for instant answers and simplified processes, is forcing insurers to rethink their operations. In this scenario, AI agents emerge as a promising solution to automate analyses, accelerate decisions, and personalize experiences. However, simply adopting technology does not guarantee results. Poorly structured implementations generate bottlenecks, rework, and frustration.

The real challenge lies not in the technology itself, which is already mature and accessible, but in how organizations structure their teams to deliver it with speed, quality, and governance. It is in this context that the Team Topologies framework gains relevance. Created by Matthew Skelton and Manuel Pais, this model proposes a clear reorganization of teams, focused on results, autonomy, and reduced dependencies. 

When applied to the development and operation of AI agents, Team Topologies offers a concrete path to scaling innovation without compromising security or compliance.

## **The organizational challenge behind the adoption of AI agents.**

AI technology is mature. Models capable of analyzing documents, predicting risks, and conversing with clients have already demonstrated their effectiveness in various sectors. However, the traditional organizational structure of insurance companies, often characterized by isolated departments and rigid processes, has not kept pace with this evolution. The way teams are organized directly impacts the speed and quality of the delivery of intelligent solutions.

Traditional insurance companies operate with isolated departments: IT manages technology, actuarial science handles risk calculations, claims processing complaints, and customer service dealing with clients. This functional separation makes sense in stable operations, but becomes an obstacle when the goal is to deliver AI agents that work across multiple areas.

An agent who automates claims analysis, for example, needs to access data from various sources, connect to existing systems, apply artificial intelligence models, and present results in an auditable way. This requires close collaboration between data specialists, developers, compliance professionals, and business people. In structures with isolated departments, each step depends on approvals and alignments between areas, which increases delivery time and generates rework.

It's not uncommon for **[insurance companies](/en/APIS-insurance-and-finance-sector-innovation-with-Vertigo-technology/)** to invest in advanced AI platforms, hire specialized talent, and implement sophisticated models, but fail to scale these solutions. The reason wasn't technical, but organizational. Without a shared technological foundation that offers standardized ways to access data and create new solutions, each new AI agent becomes a customized project built from scratch.

## **Team Topologies applied to the insurance sector with AI.**

Team Topologies offers a clear model for structuring teams around specific deliverables, reducing dependencies and accelerating results. In the context of AI agents, this framework translates into specialized teams that collaborate efficiently, each with well-defined responsibilities.

### **The 4 fundamental types of teams and their role in delivering AI agents.**

The Team Topologies framework proposes four types of teams, each with a specific role in the organization.

Teams aligned with specific deliverables are those focused on generating direct value for the business. In the context of **[AI-powered insurance](/en/The-future-of-insurance%3A-AI-and-APIs-to-accelerate-claims-and-reduce-risks./) ,** each team can be responsible for a specific deliverable, such as claims automation, intelligent risk analysis, or customer service via conversational agents. These teams have the autonomy to make decisions about how to deliver their solutions.

Platform teams build and maintain the shared technological foundation that serves product teams. In the case of AI agents, this includes standardized ways to access data, environments for training and deploying models to production, and monitoring tools. The goal is to make technical complexity invisible, offering ready-to-use services.

Enabler teams are specialists who help other teams adopt new practices or technologies. In the context of AI, they may be responsible for teaching teams about automation best practices, ensuring compliance with regulations such as LGPD (Brazilian General Data Protection Law), or disseminating ethical principles in AI.

Complex subsystem teams are made up of technical specialists who handle parts of the solution that require in-depth knowledge. In insurance companies, this might include sophisticated risk calculation systems or models specialized in understanding legal or medical terminology.

### **How team interaction determines delivery speed.**

In addition to defining team types, Team Topologies proposes three forms of interaction that determine how these teams collaborate.

Direct collaboration occurs when two teams need to work together temporarily to solve a complex problem or create a new capability. This collaboration is temporary, and once the problem is solved, the teams return to operating independently.

The service model is the standard way for platform teams and product teams to interact. The platform offers ready-to-use services, and product teams use them autonomously, without needing to understand all the complexity behind them. This reduces dependencies and accelerates deliveries.

Facilitation is used by enablement teams to help other teams adopt new practices. After the enablement, the enablement team steps aside and the product team operates autonomously.

## **Use cases: AI agents transforming critical processes**

The practical application of AI agents in the insurance sector is already demonstrating measurable gains in efficiency, cost reduction, and customer experience.

### **Automated claims analysis**

Claims analysis traditionally requires manual review of documents, damage photos, and medical reports. With AI agents, this process can be automated, reducing analysis time from days to minutes. A specialized agent can receive photos of a damaged vehicle, automatically identify the type and extent of the damage, cross-reference this information with the policy coverage, and suggest a compensation amount.

To deliver this capability with quality, an insurance company can structure a team focused exclusively on the claims process. This team utilizes services from a platform team, which offers standardized ways to access policy data and environments to put AI models into production.

### **Smart subscription and dynamic pricing**

AI agents can analyze multiple variables to offer personalized pricing in real time. This type of solution requires complex risk models and integration with multiple data sources. A team specializing in risk models can develop and maintain sophisticated algorithms, exposing their solutions so that other teams can use them for different products.

### **Customer service with conversational AI.**

Intelligent conversational agents go beyond simple chatbots. They understand context, access customer history, and resolve complex queries. A team responsible for digital customer service develops and evolves the conversational agent, integrating it with internal systems and different communication channels. Data governance and ethics in AI are especially critical in this case, ensuring that the agent respects privacy boundaries and does not make discriminatory decisions.

## **Technical architecture for orchestrating AI agents.**

The efficient delivery of AI agents requires a solid technological foundation that enables secure integration, visibility, and scalability. The way teams are structured directly impacts architectural choices.

### **Standardized integrations in agent connection**

AI agents need to access data from multiple sources and connect to existing systems. Standardized integration methods are essential for this connection. An integration management layer acts as a secure gateway, centralizing access control and providing complete visibility into interactions.

For teams focused on specific deliverables, using established integration patterns means less time spent on custom connections and more focus on delivering business value.

### **Data infrastructure and visibility**

AI agents rely on high-quality data, available at the right time. A platform team can be responsible for maintaining this infrastructure, offering data capture, processing, and access services.

Visibility is key. Autonomous agents make decisions that impact customers and businesses, and these decisions need to be traceable. Detailed logs, performance metrics, and automated alerts allow teams to quickly identify problems and ensure regulatory compliance.

## **Strategic benefits of structuring teams with Team Topologies**

Adopting Team Topologies to structure the delivery of AI agents generates impacts that go beyond operational efficiency. The benefits extend to innovation capacity, regulatory compliance, and competitive advantage.

### **Accelerating the launch of solutions**

When teams have the autonomy to make decisions about their deliverables, the development cycle is drastically reduced. The reduction in transfers between departments also decreases rework. When a team is responsible for a complete deliverable, it has clarity about priorities and can adjust decisions quickly.

### **Organizational and technological scalability**

Adding new AI agents or expanding existing capabilities is no longer a complex project, but a natural evolution. Platform teams ensure that security and governance standards are maintained, while product teams have the freedom to innovate.

This scalability is especially important for insurance companies that operate in multiple markets or offer diverse products. Each product can have its own dedicated team, utilizing common services offered by the platform.

### **Integrated governance and compliance**

In highly regulated sectors like insurance, compliance needs to be part of the entire process. Enablement teams ensure that ethical AI practices, data protection, and auditability are disseminated across all teams. Traceability of decisions made by AI agents becomes simpler when the architecture is designed from the outset to support detailed logging and access control.

Organizational structure, clarity of responsibilities, and the ability to collaborate between teams are just as important as algorithms and infrastructure.
