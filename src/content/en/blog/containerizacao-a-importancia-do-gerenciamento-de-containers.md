Containerizing your applications means adopting a set of practices that isolate sections of code into containers. Container management tools automate the entire process of creating, deploying, destroying, and scaling application containers.

The main [difference between Virtual Machines (VMs) and containers](/en/Containers-vs.-Virtual-Machines/) is that VMs require their own operating system and link their application libraries and dependencies into a deployable unit, while the container management process isolates processes that share an operating system kernel.

As a result, containers naturally become lighter to run. They only require application configuration information and host operating system code, and it's also possible to scale application functions autonomously, regardless of demand.

Check out our article to understand the advantages of managing your applications' containers, through solutions and methods that pave the way for satisfactory results in the development process.

## Containerization became popular with the support of market giants.

Docker was responsible for kickstarting the popularization of Linux container technology in 2013. The container management process soon expanded to more complex functions such as efficient deployment and containerization[.](/en/partnerships/docker/)

In 2015, Google also contributed to the improvement of containerization-based solutions through the launch of the Kubernetes container orchestration platform, based on its data center management software. At its most basic level, Kubernetes automates the process of running, scheduling, scaling, and managing a group of Linux containers.

Starting in 2017, [Kubernetes gained popularity](/en/Why-has-Kubernetes-become-so-popular/) and became one of the most widely used containerization tools on the market.

Development teams have adopted containers to run cloud-native, distributed, and often [microservices-based](/en/The-importance-of-container-security-and-microservices-for-insurance-companies./) applications , packaging legacy applications for greater portability and efficient deployment.

The adoption of the DevOps methodology has helped popularize containerization, as it aligns with the rapid deployment of applications. Organizations can store application code in containers, from development to testing and deployment.

## Advantages of containerization

The main benefit of containerization is the simplified management of container host clusters. Administrators and the development team can start, stop, and restart containers, as well as release updates or check their status, among other actions. Container management includes orchestration and schedulers, security tools, storage and systems, and virtual network management monitoring.

It is possible to define policies that ensure containers share a host based on the application's structure and resource requirements. For example, administrators should place containers that communicate intensively. Container instances can be activated to meet demand and then terminated frequently.

## Challenges of containerization

Using containerization offers many advantages, but there are some challenges that need to be carefully considered to ensure everything runs efficiently and safely. So, before we move on to the container management strategy, let's reinforce some important points.

**Security –** Implementing security procedures for containerized environments ensures the integrity of the containers. Vulnerabilities in the management system reflect standard operating system vulnerabilities, such as access and authorization, and network traffic between containers.

**Training –** To take advantage of application containerization, teams need to have the appropriate qualifications and training. For example, it's important to understand the relationship between host server clusters and how the container network interacts with applications.

## What is an efficient strategy for container management?

This is a question that all technology professionals would like to have at their fingertips. There are many variables and contexts that can make a strategy work or not. In any case, there are some points we can always adopt when talking about containerization, see:

**Monitoring –** It's important to track your application's indicators and metrics using integrated tools. Container monitoring can be performed at different levels, but monitoring at the service level is particularly beneficial.

**Automating the network –** Providing flexibility and portability for the team regarding information developed in the container is paramount. To achieve this, automating the network is essential. Consider this point when managing containers in your project.

**Storage –** Invest in external storage for the information developed in this environment and ensure that nothing is lost, as the containers only exist while they are being used; after this process, they are deleted.

**Container lifespan –** The container has a short life cycle, but it has a large capacity for expansion, according to need.

Planning the container lifecycle is a key aspect of efficient management. This management can be automated through continuous integration and other automation tools.

**Container orchestration –** It seems obvious, but using Kubernetes solutions, such as [Docker](/en/partnerships/docker/) (present in Vertigo solutions), is necessary for your container management strategy. Orchestrating containers is like defining a brain for the system, as it can integrate information, provide the necessary infrastructure for development, and distribute workloads in a balanced way.
