**DevOps** has emerged to combine tradition with innovation, contributing to the technological evolution of businesses and providing them with greater agility.

For **DevOps** to be adopted in a company, a change in organizational culture is necessary. This is because the practice aims to simplify and automate development processes as much as possible.

[If you don't know much about the subject, check out our article "What is DevOps?"](/en/What-is-DevOps/)

Keep reading this article and discover the five changes needed to adopt **DevOps** in your company. Happy reading!

## **Change of culture and design**

A change in the cultural model is necessary. With **DevOps** , developers gain more autonomy to manage their applications and processes. Tools like containers and automated testing provide this greater freedom, while also ensuring the quality and predictability of software behavior.

The change in design is necessary because adopting **DevOps** implies greater responsibility and autonomy for the project, bringing issues that previously did not belong to it into the scope of its decisions. In addition to the agile development method, there is a constant search for task automation in continuous integration and delivery pipelines.

## **Standardization of environments, heterogeneity of technologies**

**DevOps** practices demand a constant search for automation and more efficient metrics than methods based on formal documentation and reports. The intention is for developers and infrastructure professionals to replace formal and time-consuming processes, focused on blame shifting *,* with a new set of tools and processes that reduce friction in deliveries.

With this alignment of procedures, IT environments tend to function more harmoniously, with process monitoring and greater control over everything. As a result, departments gain greater autonomy and are organized to respond more quickly to problems, changes, and incidents.

As a rule, in DevOps, there is a search for a combination of environments and tools that standardizes a self-sufficiency model for projects, but at the same time accepting an inherent heterogeneity of technologies and programming languages.

## **Self-sufficient service model**

One of the main characteristics of **DevOps** is the greater autonomy given to development teams and less friction with operations/infrastructure teams. Several tasks that were once the responsibility of operations become the trivial responsibility of the project teams themselves, simply ceasing to exist (e.g., formal resource provisioning). This "harmonization" between these two exponents of the company is only possible through the simultaneous application of new tools, processes, and culture.

Therefore, companies that operate in silos, with specialized and isolated teams, must adapt to this new reality. This old model is ceasing to exist.

The idea is that the DevOps model should be agile and promote frequent deliveries, allowing users to experience new features and provide feedback to the team. Any team need should be met promptly, in an automated environment that provisions what is requested immediately.

## **Multidisciplinary teams**

In a context where operational silos are no longer viable, what really matters is focusing on delivering IT value.

And for this to happen, multidisciplinary teams need to be involved. This reality transforms organizational structures, alters hierarchies, and offers a much more collaborative way of working.

Furthermore, the work of an integrated team is quite different when compared to that of a team working in isolation. This is because multidisciplinary teams are responsible for the development, maintenance, and operation of the software for each service line. Remember that two of the characteristics of **DevOps** are agility and automation. A multidisciplinary team is capable of creating an excellent build and testing pipeline, for example.

## **Management framework and metrics**

Last but not least, your company will also need a management framework. It needs to be integrated and flexible to effectively manage the **DevOps** methodology.

This framework should be created using automated tools, software-driven processes, and a consumption-based model.

This framework will allow us to monitor whether professionals are meeting the needs of business users with consistent deliveries. In this way, while it previously took 18 months to implement and produce a system, it will now take four to six weeks. This framework allows managers to track this progress and establish new benchmarks for projects.

Therefore, managing **DevOps** will lead to greater measurement of results, since there will be a clearer definition of indicators, such as:

- Delivery quality metrics
- Application availability metrics
- Application performance metrics
- Error detection and resolution metrics
