API in Digital Marketing

Anyone familiar with the world of software development and new technological solutions probably knows [what an API is](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/o-que-e-api-entenda-de-uma-maneira-simples/) . This type of code allows different systems to exchange information, enabling one to use the other as a basis for its operation. With this simple possibility, an **API** opens up new business opportunities. You don't need to be a developer to take advantage of this market: **digital marketing** specialists already know that it's possible to leverage this solution in **advertising.**

With the shift in advertising focus and the increasingly common use of **ads** within social networks and apps, knowing how to use an API to benefit your business and get ahead is essential. In this post, we'll show how this technology and advertising are related and why this is important. Keep reading!

More important than defining the difference between the models is understanding that there are ways to monetize some of your information. Objectively speaking, both models move in the same direction, that is, they want to turn a potential customer into a client. The fundamental difference lies in how each one will achieve this conversion.

## Ways Marketing Uses APIs

**Online Advertising:**  is strictly concerned with the immediate monetization of content. APIs such as those of [Google AdSense](https://www.google.com/intl/pt-BR/adsense/start/), [Amazon Mobile Ads](https://developer.amazon.com/public/apis/earn/mobile-ads), [Bing Ads](http://advertise.bingads.microsoft.com/pt-br/cl/13592/anuncios-gratis-mais-clientes?&s_cid=BR-SMB-PPC_mk_BR_src_GGL_cat_Brand_mt_e_mkwid_sNZhwdV41|dc_pcrid_75341425964_pkw_bing%20ads_pmt_e_slid_) Others are concerned about generating ads on social networks and then showing them to a customer.
We can see this basic concept applied on YouTube through video monetization – a content creator creates a video and chooses to monetize it. This choice calls an API that, using a database, allows the creation of ads in general categories such as "sports" or "games," respecting specified sizes and defined positions. The API then retrieves these ads from a separate streaming server and integrates them into the video. The revenue is split between the partnership system with Google and the YouTube content creator.
**Online Marketing:** It does this in a more specific and sometimes even more effective way. Marketing APIs, such as the Instagram Ad API, the Twitter API, and the Facebook Ad API, use customer data to create a profile of potential interest in products and services. This can be done implicitly, such as delivering a partner's product at the top of search results when a user searches for an item like "software company." Similarly, it can be done transparently, through lists of "relevant businesses" with links to similar partner products and products recently viewed in a store.
Another initiative is if a company manages to integrate its ordering system, retrieving information directly from airline websites, can you imagine the number of new customers that could be generated through this initiative? ## More targeted advertising

Learn more about how APIs in Digital Marketing can help. If you've been paying attention to the latest developments in Big Data over the last 10 years, you know that companies now have valuable information about their users, readers, and viewers online. This allows them to target ads better than any traditional format and offer a much higher ROI for advertisers. 

In 2013, [Twitter began working with an API for advertising](http://idgnow.com.br/internet/2013/02/20/twitter-lanca-aguardada-api-para-publicidade/) , and since then has continued developing tools to better integrate it with the main platform, enabling it to target ads to the most relevant end users. The company has access to billions of *tweets* that shape the preferences and tastes of its users, and it can leverage this information when working with other solutions through its API.

Another social media giant, Facebook, allows for personalized targeting alongside solutions that work with Big Data. The Facebook Ads API allows advertisers to target ads to people already known to the brand, such as specific groups or users who already follow and like their company's page, and also take advantage of the opportunities offered by Big Data to expand ad reach.

## More control and automation

It's not just targeting that benefits: working with an API for advertising also allows for more control over your ads and facilitates the purchase and distribution of new ads. On Facebook, for example, the API also allows integration with a platform where the advertiser can buy, schedule, and distribute new ads according to their preferences.

Another advantage of working with an API in advertising is the ability to better analyze engagement and campaign results: some internal analytics tools – such as the one included in Facebook – do not allow the creation of graphs and intuitive visual ways to display the collected data. Through an API, it is possible to integrate this information with other platforms and analyze the results and ROI of the digital marketing agency more effectively, for example.

## Offering space for advertisers

In addition to opportunities to advertise on different websites, social networks, and apps, working with an API in advertising is also an idea explored by digital marketing teams seeking to generate revenue for *blogs* and other pages. While social networks provide space and profit from campaign planning, a website can do the same by allowing the integration of APIs from companies like Google, or even different types of e-commerce like Amazon.

In this way, it's possible to identify the niche of a blog's visitors, and integrated companies can advertise products or services that are relevant to their readers or viewers. In many services, such as YouTube, this is already included and only requires activation, but for independent developments, it's necessary to use an API that allows this communication.

Experiment with tools that can combine advertising and APIs, test different ad-focused solutions, and continue analyzing the results to achieve campaign success.
