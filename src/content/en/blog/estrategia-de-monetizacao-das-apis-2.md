In some programs and systems we use, there is a set of routines and standards that allows connection to other systems without direct user intervention. This happens because of [APIs](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/o-que-e-api-entenda-de-uma-maneira-simples/) , which are used by companies that want their software to be associated with other types of services.

Many organizations are generating money and expanding their market power in this way. Giants like UOL, [Google](https://developers.google.com/apis-explorer/#p/), Philips, Samsung, BMW, Mailchimp and Serasa are making significant gains with **API monetization strategies**.

But, in practical terms, how do these companies develop strategies? That's what we'll discuss in the next section.

## **Large companies and API monetization.**

Imagine a company that sells airline tickets online. When creating a product sales strategy, the Marketing department will consider SEO and social media. This is very valuable, but it can be enhanced with the use of APIs.

If the same company manages to integrate its ordering system, retrieving information directly from airline websites, it will obtain valuable insights. Can you imagine the number of new customers that could be generated through this initiative? That's exactly what [Decolar.com](http://dev.despegar.com/) does.

APIs have already been recognized by the market as powerful revenue streams. That's why Marketing and IT departments need to communicate more. If IT offers this type of solution to the Marketing department, it's quite possible that it will emerge as a business model.

Another great example of using APIs to boost business is Netflix. The streaming company initially only offered subscriptions. Far beyond developing a responsive website, Netflix integrated its program with other technologies. One of these was enabling access using a Smart TV. Did you notice that there are no limits to the use of this technology?

However, as you can see from the examples cited above, the business logic in those examples was different. Let's look at these types of revenue generation below.

## **Types of revenue generation through APIs**

API monetization can have distinct business logics. While some may generate revenue directly from their use, others can be monetized in other ways, such as generating free advertising for the consumption of a particular product or service.

### Direct Revenue

This format is one of the most commonly used for charging for API access. It is widely used by companies that offer their services via [SaaS (Software as a Service)](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/o-que-voce-precisa-saber-sobre-saas/).

In this model, a value equivalent to the number of calls that can be made to your API is usually created. It's a direct relationship between volume and the amount to be paid, and this value can be fixed or variable. Some companies reduce the cost of the call or offer free access for a period of time. This way, the developer can test the **API**.

One example of this is the [Google Maps API.](https://www.google.com/work/mapsearth/products/mapsapi.html) We all know the service is free, but there's a limit to this free service, which is around 2500 calls per day. For a large company, this number is very low, so Google sells call packages.

### Indirect Revenue

Unlike Direct Revenue, in this model API access is not charged. However, it generates revenue through other avenues. One of them is through Marketplaces, expanding or building channels that enhance business generation.

It is through them that an e-commerce website can integrate with the base platform where the sale will take place. The marketplace offers an ocean of products from diverse sources without altering the user experience. This means increased revenue for both partners! Companies like Amazon, Buscapé, Mercado Livre, and Cnova operate closely linked to the power of APIs.

Another example is affiliate programs, such as **KM de Vantagens** , which encourages its customers to make purchases both at the gas station and at stores affiliated with the program.

## **Are your company's APIs monetized?**

We've reached the end of our content hoping you're convinced that an API strategy can be the extra boost your business needs. If you're still interested, we've created a list to help you understand your current API strategy a little better.

- Are you able to profit from charging for APIs using any of the models mentioned?
- Is the API seen as an upsell opportunity for the business?
- Are there ways to generate indirect revenue through your API?
- Are there opportunities to create partnerships via API?
- Can an API improve operational efficiency and reduce activity times?

Keep two things clear in mind when adopting an API monetization strategy:

- Map and measure the revenue streams related to your API.
- If possible, compare your revenue with that of other customers who do not use an API.

Like any IT initiative, ensure your monetization strategy is directly aligned with your business objectives. If it's not aligned with your objectives, don't waste your time and focus on other actions.
