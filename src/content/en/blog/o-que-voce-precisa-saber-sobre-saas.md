SaaS ( **Software** **as a Service** ) is part of the daily routine for many companies and is only expected to grow even more – driven by increasing market demand.

We can say that the SaaS model has played an important role in the revolution of user consumption patterns. Increasingly, people are seeking *pay-* per-use solutions, want the autonomy to subscribe to and cancel services at any time, want to use the latest versions of applications, and demand quick responses to problems and fixes from providers.

One proof of this is that companies that traditionally grew using the software sales model are migrating to the SaaS model. In 2011, Microsoft launched Office 365, a SaaS model for the traditional Office suite. In the same year, Adobe, known for its software with anything but "accessible" licenses, such as Photoshop and Illustrator, launched Adobe Creative Cloud.

These two products were received with enthusiasm by end users, and undoubtedly generated a huge impact on companies, which had to spend large sums on software licenses to maintain compliance. SaaS applications are also seen by major software manufacturers as a legitimate alternative against piracy, one of the biggest problems faced by the software industry.

In this sense, the software market is increasingly migrating to the cloud, bringing real benefits to users and companies.

In this content, we will discuss what SaaS is and what real benefits this model has brought, both to the corporate world and to users.

The corporate world is undergoing a period of structural change. This is driven by the emergence of solutions such as the classic cloud computing triad: SaaS, IaaS, and PaaS. This type of format is essential for companies that need to reduce the physical space required to store servers and do not want to compromise on service quality.

In this content, we will focus on the benefits of SaaS, presenting this concept and the advantages that companies gain by adopting this model.

Stay with us until the end and ask your questions.

## What is SaaS?

SaaS stands for Software as a Service, and it refers to software hosted in the cloud that is offered by providers on a rental or subscription basis. In other words, the company no longer needs to buy and install software on its own computer; it simply accesses the service's URL through web browsers. Therefore, all the infrastructure you will need is a good internet provider.
The concept of Software as a Service emerged in the early 2000s as a major innovation. Salesforce was perhaps the most successful of the early SaaS providers.

The CRM market has transitioned from a large, complex on-premise deployment to a cloud-based service at an unexpectedly rapid pace. By the end of the decade, emerging SaaS offerings existed for almost every type of application, with new competitors appearing every day.

Today, the SaaS, PaaS, and IaaS triad is part of the reality of corporate IT and is a vibrant market, still full of opportunities.

## What benefits does SaaS offer?

With SaaS, applications become available through any device, from desktop to smartphone — as long as they are online — and hardware and IT infrastructure costs are significantly reduced. This allows for the construction and delivery of dynamic services (from CRM to enterprise management systems), with faster maintenance and updates, thus ensuring greater end-customer satisfaction with less manual work from developers.

Furthermore, the pricing is different, since the company no longer needs to pay for a license when purchasing a complete package, using only what it actually needs. In this way, by subscribing to the service, it has the freedom to use it for as long as it deems convenient and cancel at any time.

Below are the main benefits that may be causing your company to lose money if you are not yet using Software as a Service:

- **Maintenance and innovation –** Since the entire infrastructure is concentrated in the cloud, upgrades become much simpler. By being able to update the software for all users at the same time, the need to maintain older versions is eliminated. This allows for faster feedback, enabling improvements to be made more quickly and offering a better user experience for the end customer.
- **Simple and scalable –** The infrastructure conditions offered by cloud servers make the business scalable, both for the user and for the company, which can use MVPs to validate ideas and develop new products and services. In addition to not requiring large investments in hardware, SaaS has no installation costs for the user, leaving the development team with more time to develop and upgrade the product.
- **Cost-benefit ratio –** This is perhaps the biggest advantage of SaaS and the reason why it has been used so frequently by startups and IT companies as an alternative for developing new products and services. It is much simpler and cheaper to innovate with software offered as a service because there is no need for very high capital investments for development and maintenance. These lower costs make SaaS a much more competitive model.
- **User relationship –** Because it's a more dynamic service, SaaS needs to develop competitive advantages that attract and retain users for a much longer period. Since companies can invest more time and effort in ensuring a good user experience, the result is a well-delivered service and greater customer loyalty.

## Why use SaaS in your company?

In the current scenario, the cloud is driving digital transformation and compelling companies to modernize, at the risk of becoming obsolete.

It's nothing new in the corporate world that "doing more with less" is at the heart of the equation for a profitable company.
Investing in tools that are aligned with cloud computing can be a solution for companies that want to leave the competition behind and stand out in the market, offering agile and efficient deliveries to their clients.
Obviously, simply subscribing to a software-as-a-service solution isn't enough without considering the current situation of your company.

As we saw in the article about [iPaaS](/en/What-is-IPAAS/) , not everything is rosy when it comes to SaaS. Care must be taken to ensure that the various applications, both in the cloud and in legacy systems, can integrate, thus avoiding the dreaded "information silos".

In this sense, there is nothing better than carefully planning the transformation process that the company will need to undergo, mapping the current situation and training its team to best embrace the changes that digital transformation demands.
