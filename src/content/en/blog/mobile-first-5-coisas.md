**Mobile-first** is becoming the watchword when designing companies' communication channels. Investing in a mobile-first strategy has become an increasingly common practice. And it's no wonder: smartphones are by far the most frequently used devices in Brazil. In this sense, it is essential for companies' marketing and IT teams to understand the best practices for developing an efficient **mobile-first strategy**.

In this article, we'll cover the 5 most important things you need to know to succeed with your mobile-first strategy.

Stay with us until the end.

## **Why consider a mobile-first strategy?**

There was a time when having a mobile-first strategy was limited to having a responsive website. Nowadays, when we talk about responsiveness, we must understand that we are in the omnichannel era, in which the company must be able to communicate with its audience in a consistent and user-friendly way, regardless of the channel they are using.

According to a study published by the statistical research company Statista, Brazil leads the ranking for average time spent per person browsing on mobile phones.

## **1 – The first step is a mobile website.**

The first step towards mobile is ensuring your website is truly responsive. That is, it adapts to users' devices without sacrificing performance or usability. There are two ways to develop a mobile-friendly website: either a device-aware site or a dedicated mobile site.

### **Sites sensitive to access methods**

This type of website adapts to the screen size of the device the user is using. It's a great way to start because it's easier to develop. The same codebase is used for all devices, making it simpler to create.

### **Dedicated websites**

These websites are designed differently for each device family. It's helpful if you have information about which types of mobile phones your content is accessed most frequently. Your customer may feel more valued by noticing the attention to detail.

However, you can start by focusing on websites that are responsive to different access methods. The website is important because search engines always direct users to it, making it easier to find what your project offers.

## **2 – App adoption**

When adopting a mobile strategy, many companies wonder whether or not to invest in an app. The answer to this question is "it depends." It's important to understand the business strategy behind the communication channel to evaluate the cost-benefit of having an app or not.   

Investing in apps brings several benefits that can be very strategic for the company, such as:

- Access user information, such as location, calendar, camera, and more;
- Utilize push notifications as a communication and interaction strategy with the user;
- To offer the user the convenience of having access to information at any time, at their fingertips;
- To offer the user an offline experience, which would not be possible with access from mobile browsers;
- To facilitate the sharing of information on social media, generating greater engagement;
- To offer the user the option to log in via social networks and, with this, obtain permission to access their profile data on the social network.

When planning to create your own app, it's important to know the two types of apps:

### **Hybrid Applications**

Hybrid applications utilize web technology to deliver information to users. They are generally developed using standard programming languages ​​such as HTML, JavaScript, and CSS. This makes it easier to find web developers to create your applications, resulting in reduced costs and a potentially faster development cycle. Furthermore, a single codebase is needed for all systems in the mobile world; you don't need to develop separate applications for each operating system. If the focus is on user experience, this is definitely not the best option. While it's possible to customize the application for each operating system, guaranteeing optimal performance is not always feasible.

Therefore, these apps are perfect when you want to access the device's native features, but user experience isn't crucial to the app's success. This model is also excellent for disposable apps that are only used for a specific event or for a limited period of time, such as conference or sales apps.

### **Native Applications**

Native applications are developed according to the needs of each mobile platform. That is, they require a different codebase for iOS, Android, Windows, BlackBerry, etc. Therefore, these restrictions demand a slower release cycle, a steep learning curve for programming languages ​​and specific features, in addition to maintaining multiple codebases. Despite requiring more time and resources, developing this type of application is strategic when:

Businesses depend on them to conduct their business (for example, companies that exist solely on apps);

The device's native resources are used intensively;

The app needs to be fully accessible offline.

Therefore, when we talk about customer experience, despite the pros and cons, investing in a native app is the option that will deliver the best results.

## **3 – Consider the user experience.**

Developing an application with an intuitive and user-friendly design is essential for its success. But beyond that, meeting user needs is fundamental to ensuring your audience's satisfaction. Therefore, it's imperative to create websites and/or applications that are constantly updated to meet the demands raised by the users themselves.

Mapping the user is necessary to have an ideal starting point for creating your application. However, some details are common to converge into an engaging mobile experience for users. Below, we will detail some important points that should be taken into account:

### **Keep menus short.**

Keeping menus short allows for quick searches and demonstrates organization. It is recommended that categories be kept separate.

### **Reduce promotions and advertising.**

Avoid cluttering the page with advertisements and promotions. Besides causing visual pollution, it distracts the user.

### **Visible search field**

Making the search button easily visible not only makes it easier to use, but also prevents users from abandoning your website or app because they can't find what they're looking for.

### **Navigation without initial registration.**

People like having the freedom to explore the website/app before providing any information.

## **4 – Reconcile App and Website**

Website and app can be complementary, depending on their respective usability. This variety increases your company's interaction with the customer.

The adoption of apps may be driven by a different business objective than a website. While a website should offer a complete user experience, apps, in turn, can serve a specific business objective. For example:

- To be a sales channel, e-commerce;
- Offer discounts;
- To build customer loyalty;
- Create a gamification model;
- Develop a community around your brand;
- To be a channel for lead acquisition;
- Activate the brand;

## **5 - Think about monitoring and quality.**

In general, applications need to be monitored and constantly updated to ensure that the application remains functional over time.

When discussing monitoring, it's important to keep in mind that, in addition to tracking failures, a quick response is needed to resolve the problem. Therefore, the ideal solution for those who use applications and value quality delivery is to invest in an application performance monitoring (APM or Application Performance Management) solution. This type of tool allows for real-time system monitoring, enabling the identification of problems in 94% less time.

Furthermore, having a continuous delivery pipeline, with the adoption of [DevOps](/en/What-is-DevOps/) , ensures greater efficiency in performing updates and maintenance on your application. The world's most innovative companies, such as Google, Facebook, and Netflix, adopt DevOps to perform production deployments constantly and imperceptibly to the user.

In addition to this, it's important to keep in mind that "first impressions don't get second chances." In other words, the company needs to make a good first impression so that its applications continue to be accessed and well-rated by its audience.
