**Systems integration** remains a major problem for many corporate IT departments. Every day, we encounter large companies weakened by the chaotic landscape of integrations. These companies commonly suffer from extended *time-* *to-market* for projects that depend on IT, given the difficulty in performing the necessary integrations for each project.

As we saw in the [article about iPaaS](/en/What-is-IPAAS) , many IT teams have seen their demands for connectivity between applications grow uncontrollably, impacted by the popularization of SaaS solutions in companies, the migration to the cloud, and the accelerated pace of technological change in recent times.

As a consequence of this scenario, we commonly encounter many companies whose integration architecture more closely resembles a spider web.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/problema-de-integracao-de-sistemas-e1548338075889-768x474-1.jpg)

## **Why is API-led connectivity necessary to solve integration problems?**

Having an API strategy is becoming increasingly important, as the technologies that companies are using to interact with their customers, employees, and partners are changing at an unprecedented speed.

The convergence of enterprise technologies such as IoT, SaaS, big data, social networks, apps, and APIs is providing powerful new tools to enable companies to do more, generate new revenue streams, better understand their customers, and innovate faster than ever before.

Furthermore, the increasing adoption of [microservices](/en/difference-between-microservices-and-apis) requires companies to mature in their use of public and private APIs to connect microservices and applications within the company's systems.

[Click here and also check out the article "What is the difference between microservices and APIs?"](/en/difference-between-microservices-and-apis)

**Therefore, it is necessary to integrate these new technologies.**

Whenever a project requires integrations, they are done through point-to-point connections, also known as *ad hoc* connections . This makes the system fragile and complex, prone to failures, which requires significant time and IT resources to maintain.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/spaghetti-code-768x374-1.png)

Furthermore, there has been an increase in the frequency of updates in these new systems. For example, while a banking system's database is typically modified once a year, the requirements of online and mobile applications that connect to these systems can change weekly, daily, or even hourly. The speed of these changes cannot be met by traditional point-to-point integration methods. In this case, another approach is needed: API-led connectivity.

## What is API-led connectivity?

API-led connectivity is a **method of integrating data into applications** through **reusable and specific APIs** . These APIs are developed to perform a specific role – unlocking data from systems, composing data into processes, or providing an experience.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/api-led-connectivity.png)

When the entire organization adopts an API-driven integration strategy, everyone in the company gains the ability to access the best resources for delivering applications and projects. This is achieved through API discovery, self-service , and reuse.

**The goal of API-led connectivity** is not only to create reusable APIs to compose new services and capabilities, but also to decentralize and democratize access to enterprise data. IT produces reusable assets and, in the process, unlocks key systems, including legacy systems, data sources, and SaaS applications.

The IT team and other project teams can reuse these API assets and compose the information at the process level. Then, application developers can discover and "self-serve" all these reusable resources, creating the API experience layer and, ultimately, the applications. This API-driven integration approach increases agility, speed, and productivity in project delivery.

## **How does API-driven integration reduce IT workload?**

API-led connectivity has an important role to play. This is because IT is often tasked with implementing these new technologies, making the necessary adaptations, as well as maintaining legacy systems *(and sustaining their connections with other systems).*

The demands on IT are constantly growing, even when resources remain constant. Eventually, this results in an IT delivery gap.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/IT-delivery-gap-768x439-1.png)

The number of new projects needed to implement current technological needs – measured against IT's capacity to deliver them – is constantly increasing. And, as business technological requirements continue to grow multiplicatively, IT resources grow only linearly, regardless of how many resources a company can throw at the problem.

According to Mulesoft, most IT decision-makers expect their budgets to remain the same or increase very little; therefore, having unlimited resources is not an option. This is why API-led connectivity is a winning integration strategy.

## **How does the API-driven integration model work?**

API-led connectivity provides an approach to connecting and exposing assets. With this approach, instead of making point-to-point connections, each resource becomes a managed API – a modern API that allows discovery through *self-service* , without losing control over the management of each resource.

The APIs used in an API-led approach to connectivity can be classified into three categories:

- **System APIs** – They typically access the company's core systems and provide a means of isolating the user from technological complexity. Once created, many users can access data without needing to learn the underlying systems and can reuse these APIs across multiple projects.
- **Process APIs** These APIs interact with and model data within a single system or across systems. *(breaking down data silos)* and are created here without depending on the source systems of that data, as well as the destination channels through which that data is delivered.
- **experience APIs** Experience APIs are the means by which data can be reconfigured to be more easily consumed by your target audience, all from a common data source, rather than setting up separate point-to-point integrations for each channel. Typically, an experience API is built with API-based design principles, where the API is designed with a focus on user experience.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/2016-02-04_2332-e1548340854583.png)

By creating and organizing your APIs in this way, and then making them discoverable and available to the business in a self-contained manner, API-driven connectivity makes your business more compact. In this way, it allows teams across the enterprise to compose, recompose, and adapt these APIs to the changing needs of the business.

## How could API-led connectivity help my business?

API-led connectivity is a critical element in closing the IT delivery gap. In a traditional point-to-point integration approach, one might want to develop a web application to provide real-time order status and order history. For this example, let's assume you have customer data in SAP and Salesforce, inventory data in SAP, and *order data* in an e-commerce system.

What can be done at this point is that your IT team can aggregate customer data, integrating the database from all systems – including the codes. Then, the concatenated data is combined with the order data in the e-commerce system to produce the order status and order history data – with more codes. Now, these two data sources are connected to a web application API, which can be used by the app.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/point-to-point-integration.png)

This project can be considered a success: it was launched on time, within budget, and has the correct functionality. But then, the sales team, who are frequently on the road, demand that this functionality be available on their mobile phones. So, the IT team now has the task of creating a mobile application. But the developers creating the application cannot use any of the work that was done on previous projects. Therefore, they will have to redo all the work, which is already not a good outcome.

Even though developers know this is likely a short-sighted approach, they justify it given the typically intense time pressures. If consultants are involved *(as is typical)* , the problem is exacerbated, as they have little incentive to think long-term. Over time, changes become too expensive or nearly impossible to make. But because change is constant, agility is now very difficult. As you can see below, the well-known "spaghetti code" pattern begins to take shape.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/point-to-point-integration-2-768x341-1.png)

With an API-led connectivity approach, however, when teams are tasked with creating a new mobile application, there are now **reusable resources** , created from system and process APIs, eliminating all the work required to create them.

Creating the mobile application, therefore, is a matter of connecting the various systems. And now it's much easier to innovate and add new services, in this case, adding shipping status information, etc. This saves time, money, and resources, and ensures that projects can be completed more quickly.

As you can see, API-driven integration uses some of the principles of service-oriented architecture, but this approach is an evolution of SOA towards self-service consumption of reusable assets.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/api-led-architecture-768x342-1.png)

The API-driven connectivity approach allows developers across the enterprise to compose and recompose reusable services to create the projects they deem necessary, rather than the cumbersome top-down processes of traditional SOA approaches.

The **method** by which these assets are used in a company is just as important in the API-driven systems integration approach as the assets themselves.

## **What are the business benefits of API-led connectivity?**

An API-driven connectivity approach to delivering IT projects ensures that you not only stay on schedule and on budget, but also build reusable assets that will save the business time and money, creating an infrastructure designed for change, built with visibility, compliance, and governance. Furthermore, it ensures that the infrastructure meets the business needs for sustainable long-term agility.

API-led connectivity allows you to move quickly on your first project, but actually accelerate even further from your second project onwards, thanks to reusable resources and integrated organizational capabilities. API-driven connectivity frees up resources, allowing you to innovate and move quickly.

On average, MuleSoft customers have found that the increased agility and speed provided by API-led connectivity have resulted in delivering projects 3x to 5x faster and increased team productivity by 300%, compared to legacy or in-house integration solutions.
