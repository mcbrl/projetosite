**Blockchain** is a technology that functions like a large ledger, allowing for the recording, verification, and validation of transactions. It is one of the key drivers of **digital transformation and disruption** in various sectors of the economy.

The translation of the term means "Blockchain". The name was given because the records of the operations are stored together (block of information), digitally signed, and added to the sequence of blocks that make up the ***blockchain*** . These blocks are replicated across various "nodes," which are computers connected to the web with the mission of validating and propagating the transactions.

**Blockchain** is currently the hottest topic in the tech world. You've probably heard of it through one of its applications: **cryptocurrencies** **. Yes, we're talking about the famous Bitcoin and Ethereum. The generation of these coins is the heart of the financial incentive for anyone to add a new node to one of these blockchain networks. The developments that have come from there (altcoins, exchanges** , hardware wallets, etc.) are a very interesting subject, but one we won't discuss here.

This accounting record technology is rapidly spreading across various sectors. This is because ***blockchain*** is quite secure, inexpensive, and offers high potential for improving the security and reliability of systems, reducing costs and creating new revenue streams. The impossibility of breaching a blockchain allows networks to be composed of participants who do not trust each other, decentralizing records and providing complete transparency to their operations.

According to [research by Deloitte ,](https://www2.deloitte.com/br/pt/pages/finance/articles/blockchain-servicos-financeiros.html) ***blockchain*** technology is expected to be a key business focus for various sectors. "The impact that blockchain will have on companies across diverse sectors is not yet fully understood," says David Schatsky, managing director at Deloitte.

Companies in various sectors are already using the technology, including brokerage firms, insurance companies, logistics companies, healthcare companies, data analytics firms, application development firms, companies involved in the collaborative economy, and consulting firms.

The results of the Deloitte study indicate that both understanding and commitment to this technology vary across sectors, but most see it as disruptive. Many companies that have already adopted ***blockchain*** have found a new way to securely transmit monetary and confidential values.

This approach has allowed companies to develop a new business dynamic. This is the case with the Californian startup [ABRA](https://www.abra.com/) , which created a network of people connected by an app that allows anyone, even without access to a bank account, to transfer money quickly, securely, and cheaply to numerous locations around the world.

**Blockchain** provides this by storing digital data in an immutable way, allowing this information to be securely shared between networks and users. It is a **peer-to-peer (P2P) network** combined with a distributed timestamp server, enabling an autonomously managed database.

## **Discover the advantages of Blockchain.**

- The level of transparency helps reduce errors and fraud, as it requires parties to contact each other to view policy and payment data and policy statuses.
- It allows for the tracking and recording of events and payments in each country associated with a document. For example: an insurance policy.
- Neither party may modify or delete any record without all parties involved in the network being present.
- Blockchain-based “smart contracts” can be used to automatically handle payments between organizations.

According to a recent Accenture report, blockchain technology could help reduce infrastructure costs by 30% for 8 of the 10 largest investment banks, representing annual savings of around US$10 billion.

Operations in the financial services sector are overflowing with inefficiencies because organizations in the process must maintain their own data and must communicate status updates with others throughout the process.

This leads to agreements taking days to finalize. This forces institutions to set aside money that could have been invested elsewhere, with intermediaries who add no value. Another issue is the adoption of processes and controls that should be unnecessary.

**Now, thanks to blockchain** technology's ability to share data with the organization, the need for reconciliation, confirmation, and trade-offs is eliminated, resulting in a more effective and efficient settlement process.

### **Tax savings for businesses**

Financial institutions (such as banks) manage most of the available payment systems. Whenever money is transferred between organizations, there is a transaction fee associated with it, especially for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs).

On the other hand, large companies have always enjoyed an advantage in the global market, whether it be the capital to absorb the cost of such fees, obtain lower rates, or better protection of intellectual property, for example.

**Now, Blockchain** technology helps SMEs compete in the global market by cutting these transaction fees. For example, consider a B2B payment service leveraging Blockchain. It allows its customers to transfer money internally without payment. In comparison, larger banks charge around $50 per transaction.

By doing so, he believes it will directly affect the experience and timing of these transactions for SMEs, allowing them to be more competitive in the market.

In the end, we can see that this technology opens up new ways to share data, or to conduct business between organizations.

Blockchain offers a new infrastructure that allows companies to be even more efficient and effective than ever before**.**

## **Blockchain in practice**

Distributed ledgers on blockchain can be used to automatically execute contracts. In fact, the P2P database first captures all the terms and conditions, then uses data – shared between nodes and servers – to determine when the terms and conditions have been met and authorizes the payment.

AIG, IBM, and Standard Chartered Bank recently worked on a project to strengthen one of the most complex types of insurance policy: a multinational policy.

They created a master policy, including three local insurance policies from Singapore, Kenya, and the United States, in a "smart contract" based on blockchain technology. It provided a shared view of the policy documentation and data in real time.

According to IBM, the solution is designed so that all stakeholders (brokers, regulators, and auditors) can collaborate more efficiently and effectively within the network.

Essentially, the solution provides a unified view of policy and payment data and documentation, allowing all stakeholders to make informed decisions based on a common set of reliable data.

The solution was designed to ensure trust and transparency. When policy data is stored on the **blockchain** , all authorized parties on the network have a single view of the data, and no one can make changes without the approval of all parties.
