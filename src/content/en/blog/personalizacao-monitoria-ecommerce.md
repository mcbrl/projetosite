Managing an e-commerce business presents very different challenges than managing a business with face-to-face contact with consumers or visitors. Convincing customers to convert sales requires new actions and therefore new indicators, since the digital arena is new and everything is being created now! The big question is identifying the best way to relate to each of these consumer segments, and there are several tools that help map the buying journey of each consumer INDIVIDUALLY, thus establishing personalized touchpoints. We are in the [omnichannel](https://materiais.vertigo.com.br/omni-channel-varejo) phase of retail.

As with most companies, the main objective is to understand and improve the business's value proposition; technological issues end up acting as a backdrop and, in some cases, don't receive much attention within the company. After all, in a physical or virtual business, any manager can only convince the board that they need to adopt this or that solution by presenting strong arguments, proving that vital errors will be avoided, and even showing a rationale for calculating the ROI, right?

In this post, we'll discuss personalization in e-commerce, but at the same time, we'll present simple and accessible solutions so you can implement them without fear. With these solutions, you'll know what's happening in your applications (websites, mobile apps, etc.) in an integrated and immediate way, allowing you to intervene at any time if you detect an error or want to improve the results. You know how difficult it is to gather this information when it's deployed randomly across various departments of the company.

## We've achieved the experience!

Implementations of personalization have evolved significantly in recent years. Currently, the digital customer must experience a unique journey when making a purchase, as [Gartner](http://www.gartner.com/technology/home.jsp) warns . It is up to the retailer (or wholesaler) to provide this indispensable engagement that this new consumer demands. They want to make the purchase by navigating through various channels, perhaps deciding to complete the transaction online or in person. Keeping up with them is not easy. Not at all.

In the initial stages of personalization, targeted content delivery aimed at static customer groups across websites, email marketing, banners, product recommendations, etc. The premise was that consumers could be grouped based on common criteria, targeting products and offers, resulting in increased response rates, conversions, and sales.

In this initial phase, a customer returning to an online clothing store, for example, would have their demographic information recognized and receive suggestions for a wide range of products based on gender and age, combined with information about the season (summer or winter, for example) and location.

## Evolve to sell more.

In the current wave of personalization, however, that's not enough. Contextual marketing has emerged, and in it, you need to interact with the consumer in real time. Segmentation is now based on the customer's intent and needs at the exact moment they are interacting with the brand. Google calls this micro-moments.

It's necessary to adapt to this new phase that the new consumer is already experiencing and expecting. They have unique shopping experiences with more structured e-commerce sites and end up using that as a reference for any other digital environment. At this crucial moment in the sales funnel, they may even have the intention, but their patience is short. Any failure is reason enough to leave. It's no longer possible to treat them as before, that is, simply as a member of a particular segment.

It's worth moving forward, since e-commerce businesses at the forefront of personalization, using available technologies, have already expanded their average conversion rates. In-house marketing teams report that this percentage has risen to 19%, while digital agencies report an increase of 21%.
