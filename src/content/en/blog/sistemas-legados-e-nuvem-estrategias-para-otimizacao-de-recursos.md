Many companies find themselves trapped in legacy systems, either due to a lack of alternatives that meet their needs or a fear of migrating to new solutions and facing potential instability. However, even older systems can be revitalized and updated to meet current challenges.

Although they weren't born in the cloud, companies should take advantage of the benefits of the "cloud-native" model, a term that has recently gained prominence in the technology world. The pursuit of this model, which offers scalability and flexibility, is becoming a trend.

However, there is a misconception that only cloud-native companies can be considered truly "cloud-native," which should not be a verdict for companies that operate or have started their operations in other structures. The key here is adaptation.

The reality is that your company may not have been born in the cloud, but that doesn't prevent it from reaping the benefits of migrating to it. Managing a hybrid environment, integrating both on-premises and cloud technologies, and combining legacy elements with modern "as-a-service" solutions, is the foundation for building a robust operational infrastructure. This foundation is built on the pillars of computing, storage, networking, and applications.

Within this context, gaining agility to respond to customer demands, optimizing workflows, having the flexibility to expand or reduce resources according to demand, and being ready to implement strategies quickly become the foundations of Digital Transformation.

Mastering the ability to effectively use data to plan and execute actions is what distinguishes market leaders, regardless of where their companies' digital journey began.

## The Impact of the Cloud on Legacy Systems

Maintaining and updating legacy systems is imperative in the pursuit of relevance and competitiveness. As a company grows, its workflow becomes more complex and its technological needs diversify. Integrating legacy systems with contemporary solutions not only simplifies operations but also boosts efficiency and productivity.

Building and implementing robust and scalable automated systems has become crucial for companies as they grow in the market. This is because, as they gain more ground, they also need to handle an ever-increasing amount of data. And, to process this data securely, adequate resources are necessary.

It is in this context that the maintenance of legacy systems becomes fundamentally important. It's not just about making them compatible with the latest solutions, but about making the most of their capabilities to improve work processes.

Furthermore, this practice also plays a crucial role in protecting legacy platforms from threats. After all, when legacy systems are not updated, they can become vulnerable to attacks from unauthorized individuals, representing a risk that no company can ignore today.

Keeping these systems updated not only improves daily operations but also acts as a preventative security measure. This creates an additional layer of protection, helping to keep data and business processes safe from potential threats. Therefore, updating legacy systems is not just a matter of efficiency but is also essential for maintaining cybersecurity.

In reality, there are several reasons to "repair" your legacy systems. The actual cost of operating this software is one of the main factors.

In 2019, the United States federal government allocated 80% of its Information Technology budget to operations and maintenance. This spending was primarily associated with older legacy systems, raising questions about efficiency, cybersecurity, and operational risks. To put this in context, only 20% of IT resources were allocated to Development, Modernization, and Enhancement.

## Cloud Infrastructure Spending Trends

According to IDC's "Worldwide Quarterly Enterprise Infrastructure Tracker: Buyer and Cloud Deployment" report, spending on computing and storage infrastructure for cloud deployments increased 14,9% year-over-year in the first quarter of 2023, totaling $21,5 billion. Cloud infrastructure spending continues to outpace the non-cloud segment, which decreased 0,9% in the same period, to $13,8 billion.

Demand for shared cloud infrastructure grew considerably, reaching $15,7 billion in the first quarter, a 22,5% increase compared to the previous year. IDC forecasts continued growth in demand for this category, which is expected to surpass spending on non-cloud infrastructure in 2023. On the other hand, spending on dedicated cloud infrastructure decreased by 1,5% year-over-year in the quarter, reaching $5,8 billion.

For 2023, IDC estimates a 7,3% growth in cloud infrastructure spending, totaling $96,4 billion. Meanwhile, non-cloud infrastructure is expected to decline by 6,3%, reaching $60,4 billion. Shared cloud infrastructure is projected to grow by 8,4%, reaching $68 billion, while spending on dedicated cloud infrastructure is expected to grow by 4,8%, reaching $28,4 billion in 2023.

IDC highlights that this forecast of moderate growth reflects the expectation of significant macroeconomic challenges and reduced demand, but demand for the cloud continues to be driven by modernization, a focus on operations, and continued growth in consumers' search for digital services.

These figures reinforce the growing trend of cloud adoption as a viable strategy for optimizing resources and improving business efficiency.

## Resource Optimization Through the Use of Both Systems

Resource optimization is a crucial topic for companies seeking efficiency and competitiveness. The interaction between legacy systems and cloud solutions offers a strategic approach to achieving these goals. Let's explore how this integration can bring substantial benefits to organizations in different aspects.

**Maximizing Existing Investments**

Integrating legacy systems with cloud technologies allows companies to make the most of investments already made in their older systems, avoiding excessive costs associated with the complete replacement of existing infrastructure.

**On-Demand Scalability**

The scalability of cloud solutions is unparalleled. By integrating legacy systems with cloud services, companies can easily expand or reduce their resources according to fluctuations in demand, avoiding unnecessary investments in fixed infrastructure.

**Improved Operational Efficiency**

Successful integration between legacy systems and the cloud can simplify workflows and processes, resulting in greater operational efficiency. Automation and data centralization can reduce errors and streamline operations.

**Leveraging New Capabilities**

Cloud solutions offer a range of modern features, such as advanced data analytics and machine learning. Integrating these capabilities with legacy systems allows companies to adopt cutting-edge technologies without disrupting their existing operations.

**Enhanced Security**

Migrating to the cloud often raises security concerns. However, integrating legacy systems with cloud solutions can create a more secure environment by leveraging the cybersecurity features of these modern platforms.

**Business Agility**

The integration between legacy systems and the cloud empowers companies to be more agile in their operations. This is vital in a constantly changing business environment, allowing for rapid adaptation to new demands and opportunities.

In summary, by combining the robustness of legacy systems with the agility and flexibility of the cloud, companies can create a technological infrastructure that optimizes resources, stimulates efficiency, and paves the way for continuous innovation.

The key lies in a well-planned and executed integration strategy, aligned with the specific objectives and needs of each organization. This puts companies in a strong position to face current and future technological challenges, ensuring a lasting competitive advantage.
