It's noteworthy that adopting DevOps helps both companies and IT professionals with their results and a rewarding development experience. In fact, exponentially growing digital companies are "champions" of DevOps.

Adobe has taken the initiative in structuring cloud products. Amazon saved approximately 40% by creating its Amazon Web Services and increasing the scalability of its services. Facebook has used DevOps practices since its inception. It's no coincidence that it performs numerous system updates on the same day.

In this article, we will discuss the context in which DevOps becomes necessary in companies. We will talk about the practice and its benefits.

**Some reflections on DevOps**

Let's begin this article with a famous quote and the title of a book from the 90s:

*"There is nothing so useless as doing efficiently that which should not be done at all" – Peter Drucker*

*"Business at the Speed ​​of Thought: Using a Digital Nervous System" – Bill Gates*

Both of the above phrases seem timeless, and indeed they are. The first refers to the inevitable inertial nature of corporate processes, which must always be reevaluated to – at the very least – determine if they still make sense. The second reminds us that the technology market thrives on empty promises and that there is a vast graveyard of good intentions that haunts us.

"Keeping up-to-date isn't enough: you need to innovate!" – variations on this theme were discussed in the 90s, at the turn of the millennium, and in the current decade. "Now it's going to happen!" could be the slogan of every commercial platform that came, saw, and didn't succeed.

Finding definitive solutions to wasted time was the dream of any entrepreneur who dealt with the digital world, but reality has always been crueler. The 90s and the following decade brought us "integrated" (read: "non-integrable") ERPs, *software suites* for everything imaginable (portals, processes, bus systems), formal software development processes and – bam – at least 10 years went by in which the entire IT industry went in the wrong direction.

This was the serious functionality crisis in the technology sector at that time: in short, the IT industry was looking for methods to deliver more reliable systems, but in practice it only made the problem worse.

Two events were essential in changing this collective disaster: the *Agile Manifesto* (a turning point that resulted in the current hegemony of agile processes) and the advent of public clouds (notably AWS with its IaaS offerings).

Companies born with a digital mindset have been able to create new provisioning models, development and delivery processes, team empowerment, and continuous delivery. In fact, the roles that make up a typical team developing a software product have changed radically.

*We call this new set of agile practices, culture, tools, and processes DevOps.*

### **Looking to the future**

While cloud-born companies already have DevOps culture ingrained in their DNA, established corporations with their traditional IT departments, for the most part, struggle badly in this area.

A typical corporate IT department has its organizational structure designed for the ideal model of the 90s – distinct and isolated operational silos supporting "old school" platforms like classic ERPs. This traditional IT model has very outdated approaches to risk and cost analysis, and hinders software delivery with slow and (currently) meaningless processes. *It's impossible to adopt agile processes when delivery processes are hampered by notoriously incompatible provisioning models and organizational bureaucracy.*

While many corporations invest in the festive and folkloric aspects of innovation (opening "garages," cozy corners with beanbag chairs, press releases, innovation tourism, and other forms of self-help/deception), there are some truths already perceived by those who have done their homework:

- Every company is a software company.*Software is eating the world.*), [as stated by Satya Nadella – CEO of Microsoft](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/tec/2015/09/1687952-toda-empresa-sera-de-tecnologia-no-futuro-diz-presidente-da-microsoft.shtml).
- Traditional IT is an obstacle to innovation – a *the digital and sustainable transformation* It's impossible without a corporate IT transformation \[LINKS\]
- Adopting DevOps practices is an excellent benchmark for transforming corporate IT. It helps define strategies, priorities, and, most importantly, ROI.

Fortunately, many large companies are already adopting DevOps practices to optimize their results and ensure greater productivity quickly and effectively.

## **In summary, the DevOps concept**

The concept of DevOps can be complex, but at its core, it's a practice that involves *agile culture, tools, and processes* . It results in a rearrangement of responsibilities and collaboration between systems development and IT operations departments, which until then worked in isolation.

This practice aims to improve the software production and delivery process in corporate IT, resulting in more frequent and higher-quality deliveries. As a result, we have greater process agility, continuous delivery of high-quality software, collaboration supported by tools, maximized trust between departments, better allocation of responsibility for products, among numerous other aspects.

DevOps starts from the understanding that suppressing change directly implies a greater accumulation of changes with each release. This leads to frequent and recurring errors and problems. Interestingly, the greatest risk lies in the traditional risk management strategies of traditional IT departments. These are the ones that adopt practices that currently make no sense at all.

With this new approach, adopting DevOps means constantly seeking to automate repetitive processes throughout the entire software production chain. This implies self-service provisioning of resources for teams, automated build and testing pipelines, continuous delivery, tools that facilitate communication, collaboration and monitoring, among others.

## **The benefits of DevOps**

DevOps practices transform the activity of developing and delivering technology within a company, generating fast workflows in tasks executed in a predictable manner.

IT productivity gradually increases as practices mature, reducing error rates and providing solutions to address any problems that arise within a system. Some organizational boundaries in IT become blurred and/or reinvented, while ownership *of* deliverables gradually shifts to those who possess genuine expertise.

The "bad" thing about the DevOps methodology is that it's not a magic solution, but something that requires consistent work and determination. If you believe you can achieve all the results overnight, you're in the wrong business.

But the "good" thing about the DevOps methodology is that it can indeed have a gradual adoption that brings measurable results in a very short time, just with some specific practices, *open-source tools, or even through specialized consulting. This is a* *self-funding innovation* model that allows the results of recent efforts to justify continuing with the adoption.

### ***Cooperation and culture***

One of the pillars of DevOps practice is *culture* . If you understand what this term means, you understand part of what DevOps is.

It's not just about forming more collaborative teams, but also about implementing a rearrangement of responsibilities (supported by tools) that empowers project teams, putting an end to team friction.

From consistently achieving results, the business evolves towards a culture of cooperation and trust, always valuing transparency, providing feedback that is consistent with reality, and maintaining a policy of accountability among employees.

### ***Agility, Automation and Productivity***

A large part of the problems that corporate IT departments face stems from the lack of automated cycles for testing and system analysis aimed at optimizing time, avoiding wasting it on war rooms and bug fixes. Formal and bureaucratic delivery processes only guarantee the recurrence of problems, in addition to the unsustainability of corporate IT in a competitive environment.

DevOps practices allow for the automation of the entire project delivery pipeline, all the way to production, enabling the release of new system functionalities multiple times a day with controlled monitoring.

Time is money, and nobody throws money away. Therefore, valuing the time it takes to deliver a particular software is essential to pleasing and retaining customers and generating more financial return.

### ***Planning and organization***

Having an action plan and maintaining an organized approach is paramount in any field, and IT is no exception. However, implementing DevOps practices – combined with essential technologies such as cloud computing, containers, and ***serverless*** models – makes corporate IT much more resilient.

Good choices ensure the ability to anticipate and solve problems, with a great capacity to react and apply appropriate solutions.

While traditional IT pursues extremely expensive resilience models (capacity planning for redundancy, disaster recovery, etc.), modernized IT delivers the same benefits at a much lower cost and risk.

The DevOps methodology is essential for the transformation of companies.
