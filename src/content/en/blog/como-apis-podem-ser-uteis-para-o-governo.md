We do this from anywhere, whether from our computer or mobile devices. **Government APIs** give us access to some interesting data, as we will see examples throughout this content.

## **But what about government APIs?**

Looking at the advancements in information technology within the private sector, we can consider how **APIs** can be used by public institutions, such as **governments** , to optimize various resources. With this in mind, we've highlighted some key areas where **government APIs** , or public APIs, excel and enable the existence of diverse services. See below.

### **Transparency**

There is a consensus that transparency is key to controlling public spending and is essential in the fight against corruption. An **API** can be used, for example, to make data from the government's procurement system publicly available, providing suppliers, contracts, and tenders.

To publicize APIs available for public consumption, websites like the [Brazilian Open Data Portal](http://dados.gov.br/) disclose and document these APIs to facilitate the understanding and consumption of information by ordinary users or information systems, separated by topics or interests. Many applications can use these APIs to access and analyze raw data and make it available in various different views. This has the potential to meet specific societal needs.

### **Sustainability**

**Government APIs** can work to benefit the environment. Are you wondering how this is possible?

An **API** could use, for example, official data from government agencies such as the National Institute for Space Research ( [INPE](http://www.inpe.br/) ), the Environmental Research Institute [(IPAM](http://www.ipam.org.br/) ), and the National Information System on Solid Waste Management ( [SINIR](http://sinir.gov.br/) ), which provide fundamental information for monitoring the national territory.

**Application Programming Interfaces (APIs)** make data more accessible and easier for civil society to understand, benefiting the entire nation. Citizens themselves should also be able to contribute to the creation of this data , made available to society through **public APIs** . Open data can be fundamental to the creation of other services or even new businesses. The analysis of public data is monetized by various companies in the provision of services through other APIs.

### **Employment Opportunities**

Optimizing the process of searching for professionals is desired by both job seekers and companies looking for employees. A system that can integrate the demands of both would be essential to streamline various hiring processes.

A **public API** that allows the use of demographic census data could map the highest employability rates in a given city or region using [IBGE](http://www.ibge.gov.br/) (Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics) indicators . This would help people who are thinking about moving and relocating to another city or state.

The ability to verify if there is a demand for a professional's work could be crucial in this decision. Knowing the detailed demand for services by area could be an option to make the market more dynamic.

### **Education**

This is another area of ​​government that could greatly benefit from the use of APIs. Through them, schools that offer Adult Education (EJA) could be mapped. This would make the information more accessible so that adults who have not completed their studies can be better guided on how to resume them.

**Public APIs** could be used on a digital platform to provide information on the nearest Municipal Early Childhood Education Unit. Additionally, it could gather data related to school statistics, such as enrolled students, average student grades, faculty qualifications, and more.

## The future with APIs

Brazil is moving towards making more data increasingly accessible, defining a [national open data infrastructure](http://www.governoeletronico.gov.br/acoes-e-projetos/Dados-Abertos/inda-infraestrutura-nacional-de-dados-abertos) , INDA. Some of the initiatives mentioned earlier are under construction. This means more information is available to develop broader, more complex, and useful APIs. Government APIs are moving towards a digital legacy. You can learn more about the initiative [at this link](http://www.brasildigital.gov.br/main.jsp?lumChannelId=8A8182A24CFB0BC2014CFC6AE2A33DFA).

The power of APIs can be used to improve services provided to citizens, with countless possibilities and enormous potential for applicability. In the case of Rio de Janeiro, the city government invested in the creation of [Carioca Digital](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mCH9VAWBU5s) , a portal where citizens can access online public services offered by the Rio de Janeiro City Hall.

It also invested in [Data.rio](http://data.rio/) , which is an open data portal that provides relevant information about various public services categorized by areas of interest.

Given the many potential uses of APIs in government spheres, it's impossible not to consider what innovations are yet to come in this field and how many services can benefit from a quality *boost with the help of technology.*
