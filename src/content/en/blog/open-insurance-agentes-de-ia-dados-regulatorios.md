Open Insurance increases the volume and complexity of data that insurers, banks, and cooperatives need to exchange in a standardized and secure way. The more institutions that participate in the ecosystem, the greater the pressure on technology and compliance teams to transform regulatory data into timely business decisions. In this scenario, AI agents applied to data integration cease to be a differentiator and become a structural part of the ability to compete agilely within the regulatory standard.

## **The challenge of interoperability in Open Insurance.**

The promise of Open Insurance depends on a simple premise: data flows between institutions with sufficient standardization and security to support automated decisions. In practice, many insurers still operate with point integrations, built on demand for each partner or regulatory API, which increases maintenance costs and limits the scale of the ecosystem.

### **Fragmented regulatory data and the cost of poor integration.**

Without a consistent integration layer, each new Open Insurance requirement becomes an isolated project. IT teams replicate data mapping efforts, and the organization loses the unified view needed to make underwriting, pricing, and customer relationship decisions at the speed the market demands.

### **Compliance as a bottleneck for product customization.**

The fragmentation of regulatory data also hinders personalization. Without consolidated and reliable access to ecosystem information, insurance products continue to be designed generically, far from the potential that Open Insurance offers for models tailored to the risk profile and customer behavior. This scenario is among the most recurring [**pain points in the insurance sector**](/en/5-pain-points-in-the-insurance-sector%3B-specialized-consulting-reduces-costs./) , alongside fragmented legacy systems and manual processes.

## **AI agents as a layer for integrating regulatory data.**

Given this scenario, AI agents play a specific role: interpreting, validating, and connecting regulatory data from multiple sources, reducing reliance on manual integrations and accelerating the path from data arrival to business decision-making.

### **From API to automation: how AI agents orchestrate Open Insurance data sources.**

A well-structured API Management architecture organizes data traffic between Open Insurance participants. On top of this, AI agents add an orchestration layer that interprets the context of each request, applies business rules, and directs the flow to the correct systems, without requiring manual reconstruction for each new regulatory integration. This layer represents the natural evolution from [**API Gateway to AI Gateway**](/en/From-API-gateway-to-AI-gateway%3A-the-evolution-that-the-financial-sector-cannot-ignore./) in the financial and insurance sectors.

### **Faster decisions: AI agents applied to subscription and pricing.**

With already integrated and validated regulatory data, AI agents support underwriting and pricing decisions based on up-to-date ecosystem information, not just internal historical data. The same principle is already proving effective in other operational areas, such as **[claims settlement](/en/AI-agents-in-claims-settlement%3A-how-to-reduce-analysis-time-and-operational-costs./)** , where AI agents reduce analysis time and operational costs. The result is a risk assessment closer to each client's actual profile, with a response time that meets market expectations.

## **How to structure the onboarding journey without compromising compliance.**

Accelerating decisions with integrated regulatory data is only valuable when compliance keeps pace with automation. Compliance and Data Management need to be involved from the architecture design stage, not just at the final validation stage.

### **Data governance and LGPD as a foundation, not a barrier.**

An integration architecture aligned with the LGPD (Law 13.709/2018) treats data governance as part of the technical design, with traceability of origin, purpose, and consent in each regulatory data flow. This foundation allows AI agents to operate on trusted data, without turning legal requirements into obstacles to business speed. This same traceability logic underpins the **[governance of AI agents](/en/AI-agent-governance-and-team-reduction/)** , especially when automation begins to take over tasks previously performed by entire teams.

### **From tactical interoperability to competitive advantage.**

Institutions that treat Open Insurance as an isolated regulatory requirement tend to maintain piecemeal integrations and standardized products. Those that structure interoperability as an organizational capability, supported by AI agents and a clear maturity journey, transform compliance into a basis for personalization, speed of decision-making, and competitive differentiation in the insurance market.

Integrating regulatory data with AI agents represents a game-changer: it moves away from reactive compliance and towards building more precise insurance products, faster decisions, and an operation prepared for the next stage of Open Insurance. **FAQ**

**What is Open Insurance and why is poor interoperability one of the main pain points in the insurance industry?**

- Open Insurance is the standard that allows for the secure sharing of data between insurance companies, with the customer's consent.
- Poor interoperability fragments integrations and increases the cost of each new regulatory requirement.

**How do AI agents help integrate regulatory data from Open Insurance?**

- They interpret, validate, and connect data from multiple regulatory sources in an automated way.
- They reduce reliance on manual integrations and accelerate subscription and pricing decisions.

**What is the difference between Open Insurance and Open Finance?**

- Open Finance encompasses banking and credit data, regulated by the Central Bank.
- Open Insurance is specific to the insurance sector, with its own rules and participants.

**How can I tell if my insurance company is ready to adopt AI agents in regulatory data?**

- An AI maturity assessment evaluates data architecture, governance, and team capabilities.
