What happens when there's an increase in website or application traffic? We aspire to exponential growth, and if possible, in a short period of time! However, how prepared are you if your application goes viral and your ordering systems, websites, and sales teams receive the workload resulting from this success? It's good to have a high volume of traffic to an application, right? Undoubtedly, but it's also important to be prepared for it.

To attract customers and visitors, many companies run campaigns via email, search engines, social media, and other channels. If planned correctly, these campaigns can generate very positive results for the business. However, when launching the ads, many professionals fail to consider that the traffic to the company's website or app can increase considerably, especially depending on the nature of the promotion, and this can lead to an overload that causes the application to stop working.

Next, we'll show you three problems that this increase in traffic can generate, and what to do to overcome them. Check it out:

## 1\. Slow performance and glitches on the website or app.

When a company's website or application isn't prepared to handle a large volume of traffic in a short period, it will inevitably experience glitches or slowdowns, which is unwelcome to users. It's worth remembering that, depending on the volume, it could even go offline. To avoid this, IT personnel need to scale server capacity to prevent the application from crashing during peak traffic periods. Yes, it's always important to inform IT personnel about planned actions and the expected traffic levels associated with those actions.

Do you know the limit for increasing traffic volume before your application goes offline? Use this website to measure [your website's performance.](https://www.dynatrace.com/pt/products/performance-center.html)

The problem is that, in many companies, the IT and marketing departments don't work together, and neither knows what the other is doing. Because the IT team has no knowledge of the campaigns, they might panic at an unexpected traffic spike, think the website or application is under a bot attack, and make hasty decisions, further exacerbating the problem.

This is just one example of what an email campaign can do for traffic volume.

## 2\. Inefficiency of marketing campaigns

If a user clicks on an ad on a social network or searches for a product/service online and is directed to a website that is malfunctioning or slow, they will certainly abandon it! This is our daily behavior; it's what we do every day. Of course, depending on our needs, we will have more or less patience to wait for that application to respond. The important thing is to know what it means [to be fast enough in the digital world](/en/fast-enough-digital-world) . This is because internet users are more demanding regarding the performance of websites and applications due to their busier routines, as they don't have much time to spare waiting for a slow application to load.

## 3\. Loss of financial resources

If the user fails to complete the intended conversion due to glitches or slowness, the company's investment in attracting them will be wasted. After all, the user didn't learn about the solutions the business offers, nor the benefits they can provide, didn't purchase the product, etc. As a result, the company will not obtain the ROI (Return on Investment) from the campaign investment, and this may be due to a lack of clear communication between two teams within the same company.

Not only will the organization's cash flow be impacted, but also its image in the market. This is because the user became frustrated; the interaction with the brand failed right when it was their turn! It's very possible that this bad experience will be etched in their mind and that it will impact future uses of that service/product.
