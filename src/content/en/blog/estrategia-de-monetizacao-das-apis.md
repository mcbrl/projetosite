## **API**

Behind some of the programs and systems we use, there's a set of routines and standards that allow them to connect to other systems without direct user intervention; this happens via [APIs](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/o-que-e-api-entenda-de-uma-maneira-simples/) . These are developed by companies that want their software to be associated with other types of services. And this is the way many organizations are generating revenue and expanding their market power. Several companies are doing this: [UOL](http://mais.uol.com.br/static/dev.vm) , [Google](https://developers.google.com/apis-explorer/#p/) , [Philips](http://www.developers.meethue.com/philips-hue-api) , [Samsung](http://developer.samsung.com/web-api) , [BMW](http://data.hackthedrive.com/) , [Mailchimp](https://apidocs.mailchimp.com/) , and others of different sizes and sectors.

## **Relationship between API and increased revenue**

Imagine a company that sells airline tickets through online orders. The Marketing department will immediately think about using paid media, SEO, and social media, which is valuable, but it's possible to be even more effective. If that same company could integrate its ordering system, retrieving information directly from airline websites, can you imagine the number of new customers that could be generated through this initiative? That's what [Decolar.com](http://dev.despegar.com/) does; APIs have already been recognized by the market as powerful revenue streams, and if your business hasn't yet paid attention to this opportunity, it's best to rethink your lead generation strategies. This is why Marketing and IT departments need to communicate more. If the IT department offers this type of solution to the Marketing department, it's quite possible that they will emerge as a business model.

We can also give a practical and real-world example of how much [profit an API can generate for a company](/en/services/API-management) and professional growth for visionary managers. Netflix operated in a single dimension (offering subscriptions through a traditional website). Far beyond developing a responsive website (providing a good user experience for smartphones and tablets), Netflix went deeper and integrated its program with other technologies, allowing its customers to access services through a Smart TV, for example. Do you see that there are no limits to the use of this technology?

## APIs as a monetization strategy

### Direct Revenue

This format is one of the best known and most widely used for charging for API access. It's commonly used by companies that offer their services via [SaaS (Software as a Service)](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/o-que-voce-precisa-saber-sobre-saas/) . You create a clear limit stating that a certain number of API calls will cost a specific amount. It's a direct relationship between volume and the amount to be paid, and this amount can be fixed or variable. To encourage API usage, some companies offer a lower price or even free access for a period of time, allowing developers to verify their need for those APIs.

### Packages or plans

The difference lies in the contracting method; it's a plan where you create call ranges, similar to what phone companies do with consumers. The developer can use it in the most convenient way until the end of the plan. [Click here to learn about the](http://datamarket.azure.com/dataset/bing/search) [Bing Search API](http://datamarket.azure.com/dataset/bing/search) billing method.

[Click here to learn about the](https://www.google.com/work/mapsearth/products/mapsapi.html) [Google Maps API](https://www.google.com/work/mapsearth/products/mapsapi.html) billing structure .

### Affiliate program and marketplace

Amazon, Buscapé, Mercado Livre, Cnova: the existence of marketplaces is intimately linked to the power of APIs. It is through them that an e-commerce site can integrate with the base platform where the sale will take place. The marketplace offers an ocean of products from diverse sources without altering the user experience. This means increased revenue for both partners!

## **Example: APIs as a monetization strategy**

### Online payment systems

Anyone who owns an online store probably knows what an API is, since they depend on it for their customers to complete their purchases securely and without redirects (with transparent checkout). Through digital wallets, the customer, without leaving the store's website, only needs to enter their PayPal login and password, and that's enough to make the purchase. There's no need to provide credit card details, as these are already stored on the highly secure servers of these payment intermediaries. PagSeguro and Moip also do a great job with APIs, integrating their services with other websites by making their code available.

### Advantages of API exposure in summary

- Generating revenue by distributing its services directly on other platforms, websites, and programs;
- Diversification of your distribution channels (API allows you to appear in other locations, creating new sources for promoting your services);
- Enabling new partnerships;
- Free advertising (for example, Facebook, Google, and Twitter allow free access to their API);
- Speed ​​in registration (Through the integration between applications, it is possible to obtain user data without the need to fill out forms);
- Strengthening internal management (most APIs provide customized reports, as well as powerful management tools, enabling you to control the variations in your business with greater stability).

## Conclusion

### Checklist for monetizing your API

Okay, we've talked a bit about how to monetize an API, right? We've created a list to help you understand your current API strategy a little better. These questions will help you see if you could quickly monetize your APIs today.

1.  Are you able to profit from charging for APIs using any of the models mentioned?
2.  Is the API seen as an upsell opportunity for the business?
3.  Are there ways to generate indirect revenue through your API?
4.  Are there opportunities to create partnerships via API?
5.  Can an API improve operational efficiency and reduce activity times?

Keep two things clear when adopting an API monetization strategy:

- Map and measure the revenue streams related to your API.
- If possible, compare your revenue with that of other customers who do not use an API.

Like any IT initiative, ensure your monetization strategy is directly aligned with your business objectives. If it's not aligned with your objectives, don't waste your time; focus on other actions.
