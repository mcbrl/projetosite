Redesigning squads and platforms solves the first bottleneck any company at scale faces when investing in AI: coordination between teams no longer consumes time that should be spent on delivery. But clear boundaries and well-defined interaction modes, on their own, still don't answer a question that arises as soon as Team Topologies is applied: who decides what about AI, how often, and based on which indicator? It is this gap that the Vertigo module of the Masterclass addresses, transforming team redesign into an AI operating model that the organization can operate every day.

## **From redesigning squads to AI operating models**

Once the boundaries between squads, platforms, and enabling teams are defined, the next challenge shifts from structural to operational. The question changes from "who does what" to "how do AI decisions flow between these teams without recreating the same coordination bottleneck that **[Team Topologies](/en/Team-topologies%3A-the-forgotten-prerequisite-for-scaling-IA./)** just solved?"

### **Clearly defined team boundaries are not enough without a clear decision-making process regarding AI.**

Squads with clear boundaries can still make AI decisions in isolation, each with its own prioritization criteria, tools, and level of governance. The result is a mosaic of AI initiatives that don't communicate with each other, even within a well-designed organizational structure. The operating model exists to avoid exactly this scenario, defining how an AI decision made in a stream-aligned squad connects to what the platform team provides and what the enabling team recommends as best practice.

### **How the operating model connects platform, squads, and AI governance on a daily basis.**

In practice, the operating model functions as the set of routines, roles, and decision points that link the team structure to the AI ​​lifecycle in production: from prioritizing use cases to validating models, including how squads consume AI capabilities made available by the internal platform. Without this set of routines, the organization has the right structure, but still operates AI in an improvised way.

## **What does the AI ​​operating model solve in practice?**

Once the operating model is up and running, the effects become apparent both in the speed of decision-making and in how the organization measures the return on investment from AI.

### **Less rework and faster AI decisions across squads.**

With defined decision flows, squads no longer have to wait for cross-approvals to move forward with an AI use case, and the enabling team is no longer called upon for decisions that already have established criteria. The direct effect is less rework, both in the development pipeline and in the AI ​​content generation and review cycle.

### **AI indicators that are starting to make sense for technology and for business.**

The operating model also defines which AI indicators matter to each layer of the organization: engineering tracks decision time and rework avoided, while the business tracks cost avoided per person and incident reduction. With this common vocabulary, investment in AI becomes justified by financial results, and not just by adoption metrics, such as the number of active users, which do not show whether the organization is spending less or delivering faster.
