The multicloud environment has made human management even more complex. [Transformation](/en/Traditional-IT-can-be-an-enemy-of-digital-transformation./) The adoption of cloud-native technologies accelerated sharply in the last year due to the COVID-19 pandemic. This movement shows that special attention needs to be paid to the security of the technology.
Ultimately, the level of complexity of DevOps has some disadvantages. Integrations via APIs and web services are creating loopholes in firewalls. Intrusion detection systems are failing, and vulnerability scanners are missing real-time changes in production and pre-production environments.
**These gaps demonstrate how multicloud renders the traditional security approach insufficient.** These models are no longer able to meet the needs of DevSecOps and C-Level teams, leaving the door open for dangerous loopholes.
Companies that adopt DevOps understand that they need to integrate security into their work processes collaboratively. They recognize the need for change to empower their developers to find and fix security flaws earlier.
Many of these organizations make this move by integrating their security scanners into their CI pipeline using APIs and plugins, as well as CI scripts, with the goal of providing scan results to developers.
In this article, we will discuss the reasons why traditional security strategies are no longer sufficient for modern IT. Enjoy your reading!

## Where is technology security failing?

### Risk and complexity

Open-source applications and Kubernetes present a new level of risk and complexity that existing security approaches cannot handle.
This update introduces new layers of complexity that make vulnerability monitoring more difficult across microservices, containers, and different versions.
Furthermore, the lack of visibility raises several problems because conventional methods are incorporated into the continuous delivery pipeline and may be exposed to potential vulnerabilities. For example:

- Exposure to hosts, containers, credentials, and information storage;
- To determine and identify what is running in the environment in real time;
- To differentiate and control the real vulnerabilities;
- Prioritizing actions without knowing what, where, and who is executing and accessing them.

All of this points to the need for automated and intelligent security solutions that expand their reach in multi-cloud applications.

### Lack of confidence in vulnerability protection.

Scanning the pre-production environment is important for detecting vulnerabilities, but it has become insufficient for several reasons:

- Production rollbacks can end up reintroducing vulnerabilities;
- False positives present an additional risk, as their alert distracts developers from their actual work;
- New vulnerabilities may be identified after deployment;
- Cloud-native stacks can build or update containers in real time, creating configurations that differ from those in testing and pre-production.

**What connects all these points is the lack of context. If vulnerability scanners can't capture all the changes in an environment,** [DevSecOps](/en/Discover-the-difference-between-DevOps--DevSecOps--and-NetOps./) teams won't have a complete understanding of their exposure and vulnerability.

### Bottleneck between traditional security tools and DevOps.

Currently, it's difficult to keep up with security requirements, checks, and coverage. It becomes even more complicated as DevSecOps environments and pipelines change at an ever-increasing pace.
Current security tools hinder Agile in DevSecOps. They are time-consuming, unfriendly, and riddled with false positives. This is a problem because scanners lack the necessary context to determine whether vulnerabilities are real or being flagged incorrectly.
Consequently, DevSecOps teams are forced to waste time searching for and analyzing problems that are not relevant – or that may not even exist.

### Fragile integration

**Integrations are very difficult to achieve and maintain.** The most significant security testing challenge inherent in the CI/CD workflow is the lack of integrated and automated security tools. 
Although most of them have a command-line interface to integrate the tool into the CI/CD pipeline, it is necessary to consider the various checkpoints defined in the pipeline where they are executed.
Security professionals should consider what those working with DevOps already know: a single platform with integrated CI/CD security can not only eliminate this maintenance nightmare, but also provide efficiency and reduced risk.

### Need for the application of verification policies

Operations teams are known for working faster than security teams because there will always be more developers than security professionals.
If you have a complex toolchain, it can be challenging to apply the appropriate security settings everywhere they are needed.
Each scanner typically has its own settings, so you must define the CI pipeline to enforce what is acceptable (or not) to pass to the deployment. These settings are frequently replicated.

### Lack of visibility

Implementing an entire security toolset across any existing pipeline can be challenging. Can you see, within your DevOps tools, who changed what, where, when, and why?
If someone raises an exception and validates a pull request in CI, can your dashboard provide you with that information? **Without end-to-end visibility throughout the development lifecycle, teams are challenged when faced with audits and regulatory compliance.**.

### scalability

Let's say you want to apply a manual deployment to projects that use different programming languages. You'll need a new pipeline for that. In other words, it's one more pipeline to maintain. What ensures they stay in sync?
What you really need is a CI model that automatically identifies the correct scanner for the programming language being used. **The larger an organization becomes, the more complex its projects will be.**

### Unpredictability of costs

If you're paying by the number of security scanners, their size, or their application, it can be difficult to predict costs until you receive the bill.
How many scans, applications, or more storage space will be required when DevOps practices are more fully implemented across your organization?
You need a solution that carries a much more stable and predictable variable. **Choose to adopt a more holistic approach to information technology and security.**

## Multicloud demands dynamic technology security.

An update means automatic deployment to production, which requires no manual configuration. It also includes real-time mapping and transaction flow analysis for risk assessment of exposures and vulnerabilities.
**Continuous, automatic updates are necessary to understand application vulnerabilities at runtime and their impact on production environments.**
This also means full stack code, observability, and infrastructure analysis for attack vectors. Intelligent analysis eliminates false positives that were not executed.
Finally, Artificial Intelligence automates risk assessment and prioritizes actions based on their impact on the business. All of this is driven by a basic understanding that the time to act after an exposure is limited and crucial. In other words, every second counts.
In a dynamic world with multiple cloud providers, traditional application security is no longer adequate. Vulnerability detection and risk assessment now require a continuous, targeted automation upgrade that keeps your organization truly secure.
The key to a successful, scalable DevSecOps implementation is... **integrated security automation in your CI**This not only helps eliminate security bottlenecks, but also empowers developers and allows the security team to focus its efforts on bigger problems.
