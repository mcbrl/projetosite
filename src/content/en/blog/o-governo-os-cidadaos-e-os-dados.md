The way the Public Administration handles the data it possesses, and how this data is presented to the public, has a significant impact not only on transparency but also on the quality of services provided to citizens and even to local businesses. This issue points to what we understand as... [Smart Cities](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/o-que-e-smart-city/)We have a post where we talk about [Smart Cities and what Rio de Janeiro is all about.](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/o-que-e-smart-city/) is doing things differently in that regard, [click here](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/o-que-e-smart-city/) to read.
We can increasingly use technology to solve and prevent problems; there are several initiatives happening right now that are moving in this direction.
Around the world, many cities are discovering how to use data – through APIs – to their advantage, stimulating innovation while providing public services. Some cases are particularly noteworthy, as we will see below.

## 1 – Washington, DC and Iowa

With the recent passage of Hurricane Jonas, which battered the northeastern United States in early 2016, some cities used online services to help the population cope with the harsh weather. In Washington, users can consult an address on a “[snow map](http://snowmap.dc.gov/)to check what the [snow removal machines](http://iowadot.maps.arcgis.com/apps/webappviewer/index.html?id=3d5bc4ec8c474870a19c7e8f44b39c9c) They have been doing this in the surrounding areas in recent days. In addition, the system allows for tracking calls for snow removal around the city.
Even before Hurricane Jonas passed, the state of Iowa had been making efforts to help drivers navigate more safely during snowstorms, which are common in the state. In this regard, the state Department of Transportation allows snow removal machines to be tracked in near real-time, and also provides access to photos taken from the machines' dashboards.

## 2 – Los Angeles

The city has made available to its citizens, through an integration with Google Maps, resources that allow them to monitor [weather trends](http://elnino.lacity.org/) associated with the El Niño phenomenon unfolding in the Pacific Ocean. Thus, it is possible to view layers indicating regions affected by storms, power outages, traffic jams, and even find locations with available sandbags to contain flooding.

## 3 – California

The state recently made available a [series of three maps](http://oehha.ca.gov/ej/ces2.html) , in line with new legislation, that allow for the identification of communities threatened by high levels of air pollution. The first map identifies the concentration of pollutants in the air. The second indicates the concentration of people sensitive to these high levels. The third combines the data from the previous two to pinpoint the communities most at risk.

## 4 – Manassas, Virginia

The city recently implemented a service that indicates when citizens should place certain types of materials for collection on the curb. Through a map, the routes and details for each type of collection are made available for a given address, which, in addition to providing a valuable service to residents, has also alleviated a large volume of calls requesting information from the responsible municipal agency.

## 5 – Chicago, Boston and Philadelphia

The city of Chicago recently made available a service called [OpenGrid](http://opengrid.io/)This allows users to access various administrative information, such as sanitary inspections, pothole locations, road closures, and much more. All this with the option to use search filters, such as the postal code of a specific region or even an area drawn on the map.
[The Boston Portal](http://bostonopendata.boston.opendata.arcgis.com/datasets), accepts contributions from users, with their own maps, in addition to integrating a series of other information, such as traffic data extracted from Waze and the location of bus stops.
And [Philadelphia Portal](http://www.phila.gov/map)The information provided includes planned road and bike lane closures, as well as the location of parks and other municipal resources, including even the locations where flu vaccines are offered.

## 6 – Houston

The city has a telephone hotline for reporting situations requiring administrative attention. To enhance the service's intelligence, a feature was introduced that allows for spatial visualization of the concentration of various types of requests, enabling the analysis of trends and identifying the locations where certain problems occur most frequently. All of this information is available through the [Houston Portal.](http://performance.houstontx.gov/311Dashboards#map)

## 7 – Rio de Janeiro

The city of Rio has an [open data portal](http://data.rio//) containing diverse information about what is happening in the city, from an informational point of view. The idea is to encourage the use of this data through applications that can be useful to some segment of society. Through the exposure of this information, decisions can be made to improve the lives of those who use it, allow these people to be more active in society, have great economic importance, enable business decisions, and be of great value to both the citizen and the government itself.

## Guiding the growth of new businesses.

What do all these cases have in common? They use already available data to provide valuable services to the population. And they all consist of excellent examples of how the proper handling and availability of data, particularly with the use of APIs and the integration of systems with other services, can generate value for a society and make a difference in the lives of citizens.
APIs can and should be used by companies in various sectors. New businesses and services can emerge through this concept of making some of their information available for someone to consult at a given time; this is what all these initiatives are doing. Don't be afraid to make some of your information available.
