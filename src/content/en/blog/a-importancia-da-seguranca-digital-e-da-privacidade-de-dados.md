## Large companies are vulnerable to hacker attacks.

A survey conducted by [PSafe](https://www.psafe.com/blog/98-dos-sites-corporativos-possuem-vulnerabilidades-aponta-levantamento-da-psafe/) , a company focused on digital security in Latin America, analyzed approximately 2.400 corporate websites, curating vulnerabilities that could be exploited by hackers and jeopardize the integrity of confidential data.

Within the total number of websites analyzed, 98% presented a medium to high risk and 2% a critical risk of suffering some type of cyberattack.

Even though most of the websites analyzed have a moderate risk of being hacked, it's worth mentioning that cybercriminals are constantly searching for vulnerabilities, no matter how small, because significant damage can be done through these entry points.

If even a single compromised device gains access to sensitive information, it's already possible to compromise data from the entire project through this entry point.

Given this data, along with the constant news of cybercrimes that are common knowledge, the concern for digital security cannot be ignored or treated lightly within large companies and their projects.

## Digital security strategies are becoming essential to avoid financial losses.

The financial sector remains a major target for cyberattacks, with cybercriminals developing strategies to compromise user credentials through phishing.

If an employee uses their account credentials, such as passwords, after falling into an online trap, hackers can instantly violate their privacy, gaining access to personal information and even carrying out scams that divert their money.

There are various types of attacks, but the most widely used scam in recent years, and one that is growing every day, is ransomware. This involves hacking into systems and hijacking data to demand a ransom, almost always in cryptocurrency.

The Brazilian company JBS was the victim of ransomware attacks and [paid a ransom](https://www.infomoney.com.br/mercados/jbs-pagou-resgate-de-us-11-milhoes-em-bitcoin-apos-ciberataque/) of US$11 million in bitcoin to the hackers who even managed to disrupt operations at its factories.

Many of these attacks originate from flaws in companies' security policies, which need to define a clear digital security strategy and often encounter difficulties such as the inability to protect dynamic environments and face obstacles when reviewing software security methods.

Companies seeking Digital Transformation and considering a good [DevSecOps](/en/devsecops-ransomware-protection/) strategy need [to break down monolithic systems](/en/the-advantages-of-microservices-architecture/) to accelerate innovation and eliminate the difficulty of dealing with containers. It's necessary to address the lack of visibility and the sharing of secrets between teams, implementing restrictions and access based on the employee's role.
