## What is a CMS and Portal?

CMS stands for Content Management System. It's a framework that facilitates the rapid publication of a website.

Companies that need to constantly update their website content, whether with dynamic information or various blog articles, often resort to this solution. A good example of a CMS is WordPress.

The user's main concern is creating content, without needing to apply extensive technical programming knowledge, as the interface of a Content Management System is user-friendly and intuitive.

Therefore, a CMS is the ideal solution for anyone who needs a website that isn't 100% static and temporary (designed to be online for a short period of time).

With the advancement of Digital Transformation, new digital channels have emerged, such as mobile devices and different screen sizes. Promoting digital accessibility has become mandatory for companies in the public sector, in addition to sparking interest in the private sector.

With the increasing demand for more high-quality and inclusive content, the CMS is having some difficulty meeting all the requirements, making it necessary to migrate to a DXP, which we will discuss below.

Portals play a key role in fostering solid relationships between companies and their customers after the product or service purchase stage. They are excellent solutions for experiences that require login and password access to private content, but their management can be complex and expensive for teams.

For example, employees of an institution who need to access registration data, exam results, competition results, and other relevant information.

A portal is a platform that aggregates information from different sources into a user interface, providing relevant information according to its context.

With the popularization of the internet, web portals have evolved into platforms that seek to generate personalized content, always improving the customer's digital experience.

## What is a DXP?

To understand the advantages of a DXP, we need to understand that it stands for Digital Experience Platform.

Implementing a DXP means that your customer is the central focus, as business actions are entirely geared towards improving their experience.

Therefore, many companies are restructuring their business practices, implementing digital channels as the main means of interaction between organizations and their target audience, making this practice the key point in their business models. All of this stems from the implementation of a digital experience platform.

DXP is a platform that brings together various tools and features that ensure intuitive, enjoyable, and secure navigation for users, transforming their journey into incredible digital experiences.

Originally derived from CMS, an application that manages shared information, DXPs sometimes have their functionalities confused, but in reality they are much more robust and versatile.

A CMS is typically used for Enterprise Content Management (ECM) and Web Content Management (WCM).

ECM typically supports multiple users in a collaborative environment, with the ultimate goal of enabling the delivery of relevant content to users. Alternatively, WCM is used in conjunction with a web experience manager (WEM) to build web-based experiences.
