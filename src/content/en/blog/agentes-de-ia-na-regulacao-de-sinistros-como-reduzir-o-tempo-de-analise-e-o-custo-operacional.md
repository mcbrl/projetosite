The claims settlement process is one of the biggest drivers of operational costs in the insurance sector. Manual validations, scattered documents, and approval queues make each claim slower and more expensive than it should be. AI agents change this scenario by working in an integrated way with the claims workflow, automating critical steps with full traceability and without sacrificing human control.

## **What are AI agents and why are they different from conventional automated systems?**

**[AI agents](/en/agents-insurers-team-topologies/)** are systems capable of interpreting unstructured content, cross-referencing information from multiple sources, and suggesting or executing actions based on this analysis. They differ from traditional automation because they do not rely on fixed rules: they learn from historical patterns and can handle variations, non-standard documents, and situations that require contextual interpretation.

In practice, an AI agent applied to claims can read a photo of damage, identify the type and extent of the problem, cross-reference it with the policy coverage, and suggest a compensation amount. All this in seconds, without human intervention in the initial screening.

### **What is the difference between traditional automation and AI agents?**

Traditional automation executes predefined tasks based on fixed rules. It works well for repetitive and predictable workflows, but fails when any variation outside the configured pattern arises. AI agents, on the other hand, interpret context, learn from historical data, and make adaptive decisions. The practical difference is that automation follows a script, while the agent reasons about the problem.

## **Why is the claims settlement process so expensive and slow?**

The high cost per claims process is not caused by the complexity of the work itself, but by the way that work is organized. Most claims processing workflows were designed for analog environments and have never been adequately modernized.

The main causes of slowness and cost are: manual collection and verification of documents, cross-checking in disconnected systems, manual comparison between claim data and policy clauses, and approval queues that depend on human availability at each stage. Any document inconsistency restarts part of the cycle, generating rework and delays.

### **How does a slow claims process affect the insurer's financial results?**

The impact is twofold. The direct cost lies in the labor hours involved in each manual step. The indirect cost is the vulnerability to fraud: without automated cross-referencing of data in real time, inconsistencies go unnoticed and fraudulent claims are settled without due scrutiny. According to IRB Brasil, fraud in the insurance sector represents losses of billions of dollars annually for the national market.

## **How do AI agents operate within the claims workflow?**

AI agents intervene on three complementary fronts within the regulatory process. In each of them, the logic is the same: to automate what is repetitive, to flag what requires attention, and to deliver to the human analyst a case that is already structured, not a pile of raw documents.

### **How does the agent validate documents?**

The agent receives the documents sent by the insured, identifies the type of each file, verifies the completeness of the documentation, and automatically flags any missing or illegible items. This process takes place in seconds. When the case reaches the analyst, the screening is already complete and the documentation is organized for merit analysis.

### **How does the agent detect fraud and inconsistencies?**

The agent cross-references the claim data with the insured's history, the coverages contracted, and previous claims records. When an inconsistency is identified, the case is not dismissed: it is flagged as the specific point of divergence, and forwarded for human review with the context already mapped. This makes the analyst's work more objective and reduces investigation time.

### **Does the agent make decisions on its own, or does the analyst still need to validate them?**

The agent suggests, the analyst decides. Based on document analysis and data cross-referencing, the agent proposes a compensation amount compatible with the policy coverage and historical parameters. This proposal is presented to the responsible analyst, who validates, adjusts, or rejects it. Human control over the final decision is maintained at all stages.

## **How to ensure compliance with the LGPD (Brazilian General Data Protection Law) when using AI agents in claims processing?**

The data processed in the claims process includes health information, financial data, and sensitive personal information, all protected by the General Data Protection Law (LGPD), **[Law 13.709/2018](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm)** . To ensure compliance, three requirements must be met by the system architecture: restricted access only to the data necessary for each step, documented consent for the processing of information, and a complete audit log of each action performed by the agent.

When agents are integrated via APIs with configurable access control policies, these requirements are applied automatically and consistently. Compliance with the LGPD (Brazilian General Data Protection Law) no longer depends on periodic manual reviews and becomes part of the process architecture. Each agent decision generates a persistent record that can be consulted by internal and external auditors or by SUSEP (Brazilian Superintendence of Private Insurance).

## **What should be considered before implementing AI agents in claims handling?**

Three points determine whether the implementation will generate results or frustration. The first is the quality of integration with existing systems: without a well-structured API layer, the agent operates in a fragmented way and delivers inconsistent results. The second is the clear definition of the boundaries of its operation: which decisions can the agent suggest autonomously and which require mandatory human validation. The third is the training of the teams that will supervise the agents, because professionals who understand what the agent does and where it can go wrong are the factor that determines whether the technology generates real value.

## **Frequently asked questions about AI agents in claims settlement.**

**What is an AI agent applied to claims?**

It is a system capable of reading documents, cross-referencing data, and suggesting decisions within the claims settlement process, automatically and traceably, without replacing human judgment in critical stages.

**Can AI agents replace claims analysts?**

No. They automate operational steps and structure the case for the analyst, but the final decision on compensation remains a human responsibility. The model is one of collaboration, not replacement.

**How do AI agents protect policyholders' sensitive data?**

Through APIs with granular access control, automatic audit logs, and restricted access only to the data necessary for each stage of the process, in compliance with the LGPD, Law 13.709/2018.

**How long does it take to implement AI agents in a claims process?**

It depends on the maturity of the integration of existing systems. Insurance companies with structured APIs can implement the first automations in weeks. Environments with disconnected legacy systems require a prior integration step.

**Which claims processes benefit most from AI?**

Screening and validating documents, cross-referencing data to detect fraud, and supporting the suggestion of compensation amounts are the areas with the greatest immediate operational return.
