The insurance sector in Brazil is experiencing a paradox that exposes the fragility of its operational structures. In 2024, companies in the segment **[recorded record revenue](https://www.irbre.com/seguradoras-arrecadam-r-2076-bilhoes-em-2024-mas-lucro-liquido-cai/)** of R$ 207,6 billion, a growth of 10,2% compared to the previous year.

But at the same time, net profit fell 4,1%, from R$ 37,4 billion to R$ 35,9 billion. The main reason? A significant increase in operating costs, claims payments and, mainly, structural bottlenecks that hinder the ability to scale efficiently.

Growing revenue without increasing profitability is a symptom of operations that are not keeping pace with the market. While insurtechs and fintechs are advancing with digital agility, many traditional insurance companies still struggle with rigid processes, disconnected systems, and difficulty activating new distribution channels. These obstacles not only increase costs, but they also limit competitiveness and hinder expansion opportunities.

Below, we present the five most critical operational bottlenecks faced by companies in the sector in Brazil and how strategic technology can transform obstacles into a competitive advantage.

## **1\. Legacy systems that hinder innovation and increase costs.**

Systems developed decades ago still underpin the operation of a large portion of Brazilian insurance companies. These monolithic platforms, built without modularity, hinder any attempt at integration with new technologies, making each update a lengthy, expensive, and risky process.

The rigidity of these environments prevents companies from responding quickly to market demands. Launching a new product or adjusting a feature can take months, requiring rework on old and poorly documented code. Furthermore, maintaining these structures consumes increasingly scarce and expensive specialized technical resources, generating hidden operational costs that directly impact financial results.

The risk of failures also increases proportionally to the age of the application. When they occur, these failures are costly not only in financial terms, but also in terms of reputation and customer trust.

## **2 – Slow activation of partners and digital channels**

Distributing insurance through partners is one of the main drivers of growth in the sector. However, many insurers face extremely long cycles for activating new brokers, partner platforms, or digital channels. Manual processes, lack of standardization, and dependence on multiple internal areas mean that integrating a new partner can take weeks or even months.

This slowness leads to lost business opportunities. While the deal is being finalized, the time until sales actually begin consumes resources and reduces the return on investment. In an increasingly dynamic market, where insurtechs can integrate partners in days, this operational lag becomes a critical competitive disadvantage.

The difficulty in scaling partnerships quickly prevents companies in the sector from expanding their presence in new markets or taking advantage of strategic growth opportunities.

## **3 – Lack of scalability in digital operations**

With the growth of digital channels and the volume of online operations, the ability to scale has become mandatory. Systems that were not designed to support rapid growth face bottlenecks at critical times, such as peak demand periods, campaign launches, or peak seasonal dates.

Architectures that don't scale on demand compromise operational stability and jeopardize the customer experience. Slow processing times, system unavailability, and technical failures at crucial moments directly affect customer confidence and lead to lost revenue.

Furthermore, the lack of technical scalability limits insurers' ability to serve multiple partners simultaneously, create customized environments for different channels, or adapt the infrastructure to the needs of each line of business.

## **4 – Difficulty in customizing and controlling branding in partnerships**

Expansion through partnerships requires a delicate balance between partner autonomy and **[insurer governance](/en/APIS-insurance-and-finance-sector-innovation-with-Vertigo-technology/)** . Many companies struggle to offer customized digital environments that maintain the visual identity and brand experience of each partner without relinquishing control over processes, security, and regulatory compliance.

This limitation directly affects the attractiveness of partnerships. Strategic partners seek solutions that allow them to strengthen their own brand in their relationship with the end customer, but without compromising operational agility. When this is not technically feasible, negotiations cool down or the experience delivered to the consumer becomes fragmented.

The lack of tools that allow for visual and functional customization without requiring lengthy development cycles creates excessive dependence on technical teams and increases the time between signing the partnership agreement and the effective start of sales.

## **5 – High operational costs due to lack of standardization**

The lack of technical and process standardization leads to constant rework. Many insurance companies operate multiple environments developed in isolation, without reusing components or sharing infrastructure. Each new project requires development from scratch, increasing costs and overburdening technical teams with repetitive tasks.

This fragmentation also makes maintenance difficult. Updates need to be manually replicated in each environment, increasing the risk of inconsistencies and operational failures. The time spent on these activities could be directed towards innovation and continuous improvement of products and services.

Furthermore, the lack of centralized governance prevents complete visibility into what is being executed, who is accessing which systems, and where the main operational or regulatory risk points are.

## **How CNP Seguradora overcame these bottlenecks with strategic technology.**

CNP Seguradora, part of the French group CNP Assurances with a presence in 19 countries, faced precisely these challenges when seeking to expand its consortium operations through partnerships. The need was clear: to expand product distribution in a decentralized manner, without losing control, consistency, or brand identity.

The solution came through a **[partnership with Vertigo](https://materiais.vertigo.com.br/case-como-a-vertigo-tecnologia-ajudou-a-cnp-a-escalar-sua-opera%C3%A7%C3%A3o-digital)** , a company with over 25 years of experience in IT consulting and an official Liferay partner in Brazil. Together, they built a robust, scalable white-label platform geared towards accelerated business expansion.

The central point of the solution was the creation of a structure that allows CNP's partners to offer consortium products with their own branding elements and a personalized digital experience. Using Liferay DXP's StyleBook feature, each portal maintains its visual identity without requiring custom development from scratch.

The results were significant. CNP drastically reduced the activation time for new partners, cut development and maintenance costs through standardization and reuse of components, increased recurring revenue with more active partners in its own channels, and gained centralized control over governance, security, and regulatory compliance.

Furthermore, the insurance company achieved operational autonomy. With a structured technological base, internal areas gained the ability to replicate and manage environments without depending on large development cycles, allowing for more agile decisions and a focus on the continuous evolution of the business.

## **Operational modernization as a path to sustainable growth.**

The operational bottlenecks hindering the growth of Brazilian insurance companies are not inevitable. They are a direct consequence of outdated infrastructure and processes that were not designed for the scale and speed required by today's market.

Overcoming these obstacles requires more than just investment in technology. It demands strategic vision, partnerships with those who deeply understand the insurance sector, and the implementation of solutions that balance innovation, governance, and operational efficiency. The case of CNP Seguradora demonstrates that it is possible to transform complex structures into modern, flexible platforms prepared to accelerate growth with sustainability.
