Observability has been gaining attention due to its effectiveness in allowing developers to raise the level of [*Customer Experience*](/en/How-to-measure-and-analyze-customer-experience./) in their software, often affected by the increasing complexity of digital ventures and the emergence of new technologies such as *cloud native* , DevOps, microservices, containers, and virtualization, for example.

Monitoring allows development teams to intervene quickly when errors are detected in their systems, as the practice aims at the frequent collection of data that allows for the identification of failures. However, it is a reactive method.

Using [traditional IT](/en/Traditional-IT-can-be-an-enemy-of-digital-transformation./) monitoring methods presents several obstacles that hinder your organization's growth due to a lack of scalability and intelligent analysis in the development environment, as well as inefficient and time-consuming problem detection.

In other words, problems arise in the application development environment that are unknown, and the team doesn't know how to manage them because they lack knowledge of what's happening behind the scenes in their IT department. 
A observability, on the other hand, is the [The practice of instrumenting systems with tools to collect actionable data.](/en/Observability-in-the-process-of-new-business-evolution./) that provide not only warnings at the moment the problem occurs, but – more importantly – explain why. In addition to solving them at their root so they don't recur. There is a **Planning for failure prediction.**
Given this type of situation, it is crucial to have a deep understanding of what is happening in your organization's applications and systems.

Observability helps IT teams to:

- To supply **high quality software** and on a large scale;
- Building a sustainable culture of **innovation;**
- Optimize investments **Cloud native**;
- **Analyze the performance** Real-time information about your business.

Metrics, events, tracking, and logs are essential data for observability. They are analyzed to produce insights aimed at understanding and interpreting errors so that intelligence can be activated more quickly. Once this data is aggregated, it manages and monitors suspicious activities.

## Modern architectures demand a new approach to monitoring.

IT teams face immense pressure to quickly and more frequently produce and release new features and experiences to market. [Cloud Native technology](/en/Discover-the-6-pillars-for-building-cloud-native-applications./) has ignited the competitive landscape by lowering the barrier to entry, enabling faster deliveries and adaptations than ever before.

Consumer expectations have increased, and tolerance for errors has decreased. Slow, error-prone, or poorly designed digital platforms are a major obstacle to customer retention. Remember: if they can't get what they came for, they'll try elsewhere and simply won't return.

Today, organizations have the possibility of working with microservices architectures and distributed systems in various Cloud Native solutions. They are easier to adopt and work together in an increasingly integrated way.
Companies are organizing themselves around autonomous, responsible teams. Sometimes these professionals need to use shared platforms and tools provided by the internal team. Automation reduces repetitive and low-value work and **improves delivery reliability**.

In a Cloud Native architecture, everything within the *stack* is controlled and programmed by the software. And like all automation, it is prone to failure. That's why teams need to monitor their automation tools exactly as they would the applications that directly serve their customers.

Ultimately, collecting data about each component of a system is the essence of observability.

## The three main elements of observability

The ability to understand the "why" behind errors and failures is what allows teams to truly address root causes and ensure system reliability. To achieve observability in their systems, observability has the following key elements:
**Open instrumentation
**It is defined as the collection of open-source or vendor-specific telemetry data from an application, service, infrastructure host, container, cloud-native, serverless function, mobile application, or any other type of data emission. 
With *open instrumentation* There is the provision of visibility across the entire surface of applications and infrastructure that are essential to the business.

**Connected entities
**All telemetry data must be analyzed so that the entities that produce it can be identified and connected; metadata also needs to be incorporated to create correlations between these entities and their data.
These two actions create context and meaning from large volumes of data. From there, the curation can be delivered in the form of visual models of the system without any additional configuration.
Furthermore, intelligence can be applied to add even more meaning. Applied intelligence is the application of learning in data science to search for patterns or anomalies so that teams can make decisions and act correctly.

**Contextualization
**Every company is unique, and no automated curation can meet all its diverse needs. Organizations need to create their own context for their telemetry data, combining critical data and business dimensions.
It's important to be able to clearly show the cost of errors and failures in a business process and provide a path to analyze the data in order to find the real reason.

###
The challenges that drive the need for observability.

- **Greater complexity: Cloud Native technology has changed the way applications are built, delivered, and maintained, thus creating more complexity for the teams responsible for their upkeep.** 

- Greater risks: Frequent deployments and dynamic structures mean introducing more risks to operations. This makes it even more important to have instant problem detection and rollback capabilities for releases.

- Lack of skills: Microservices architecture has brought new challenges, as development teams need to rethink how they design, build, and launch applications; 

- Many tools: Dealing with multiple monitoring tools to find and correlate the most important data and resolve problems consumes precious time that teams don't have when their clients are affected by a production issue.

Because of these challenges, teams need a solution that reduces complexity and risk.

###
What types of data must a service produce to be observable?

- **Functional checks** They assist orchestrators in executing automated actions to maintain the overall functioning of the system;
- **Metrics** They are easy to store and retrieve, which helps in identifying trends. Over a longer period, numerical data can be compressed into less granular aggregates;
- **Log entries** These are essential for debugging, as they include stack traces and other contextual information that can help identify the root cause of observed failures;
- Os ***traces*** They capture the end-to-end flow of a request, essentially showing the relationships between services and the work structure throughout the system.

Cutting-edge Customer Experience

As innovation progresses, the world becomes increasingly technological and complex. It will be necessary to automate more and closely monitor your customer's expectations – which may have been set by other companies, including your competitors.

Therefore, it is necessary to adopt observability to:

**– Collect and combine**In one place, it brings together telemetry data from both open and proprietary sources. This instrumentation reduces the proliferation of unnecessary tools and the time-consuming context switching that occurs when emergencies arise – because it offers internal operability independent of the source;
**– To form connections and relationships** and thus apply them to create context and meaning so that the presented data can be understood. The context is presented in selected visualizations that highlight the most important information;
– Capacity of **create custom applications** that offer interactive and curated experiences. Often, integrated workflows allow the combination of external datasets in real time. Programmability redefines the possibilities of observability.
