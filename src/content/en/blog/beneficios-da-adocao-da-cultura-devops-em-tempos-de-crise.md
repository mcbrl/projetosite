*By: Carlos Aiello*
Amidst a scenario of so much uncertainty due to the current coronavirus (COVID-19) pandemic, it is natural that doubts arise due to the lack of economic predictability, public health problems, and a lack of knowledge about how the world will behave in the short and medium term. It is an unprecedented scenario in contemporary society, and one that, unfortunately, will have an enormous human and financial cost worldwide.

In just three months, it was possible to observe common scenarios that only multiplied, such as mass layoffs, companies with low cash flow that are unable to continue operating, and various other operational difficulties.
However difficult it may be to interpret this scenario, it gradually becomes clear that there is no way to expect things to go back to the way they were. The world will be different and needs will change.  

To use a simple analogy, it's as if it were possible, at this moment, to return to a world without cell phone communication or without the computerization of banks. Current needs don't allow for those scenarios. In the medium term, there will be a new perspective, with other demands and needs.

In just two months, teachers adapted to distance learning, and companies that use it... *delivered* This significantly increased the flow of their business, in addition to the rise in internet searches about how to "adjust to life at home."
Something else that stands out is the adaptation of several companies to remote work, realizing the high cost of bureaucratic processes and how they hindered the company's development.

### What does DevOps have to do with all of this?

[**Defining DevOps**](/en/devops/) culture is not an easy task; however, using a formal concept, **it refers to the set of practices for integrating development and operations teams** , with the adoption of automated processes for faster, less costly, and more secure production of applications and services.

In short, **it's the practice of continuous improvement through process automation, resulting in higher quality, less human dependence, and a drastic reduction in your business's operational costs** , creating an integrated and frictionless environment between developers and infrastructure professionals.

Now – more than ever – is the best time to embrace the culture. [DevOps](/en/devops/) In your company, many (including your competitors) have already realized that adopting DevOps is essential for operating at a lower cost and with greater efficiency.
Going forward, it is vital to abandon bureaucratic processes controlled manually by an entire team, resulting in significant operational slowness, and instead allow efficient automation and simple processes to keep your business competitive. **It's not just a cheaper model, but a guarantee of safety and reduced operational risks.**

[Only by adopting a DevOps](/en/devops/) culture will it be possible to survive – or even grow at scale – in a world where the absence of contact and the digital channel are mandatory to meet a new demand from customers, ranging from consumers of products such as insurance, financial applications and food, to the consumer of simple traditional street retail stores.

The world was already moving rapidly in this direction, opting for the convenience and efficiency of products and services consumed and/or requested through digital means, gradually abandoning traditional commerce. However, in the near future, the traditional will be the consolidation of this trend that began before the pandemic.
