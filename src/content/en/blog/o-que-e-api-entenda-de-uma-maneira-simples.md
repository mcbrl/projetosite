The acronym **API** stands for " ***Application Programming*** **Interface** ". In Portuguese, it's called "Interface de Programação de Aplicações". APIs are a way to **integrate systems,** enabling benefits such as data security, ease of information exchange between different programming languages, and monetization of access. Let's delve deeper below to better understand **what an API is** . We'll discuss benefits, application examples, and a brief history of the birth of this technology.

### **But what does API mean in practice?**

APIs are a type of "bridge" that connects applications, and can be used for a wide variety of businesses, by companies of different market niches or sizes, as we will see in more detail throughout this post.

Most people don't know **what an API is** . This is because they are invisible to the average user, who only sees the interface of software and applications. However, programming professionals are intimately familiar with this technology, which is the result of the evolution of various systems and tools. Applications and software of all types can only be built using the standards and specifications provided by APIs.

APIs enable the integration of systems with completely different languages ​​in an agile and secure way. In other forms of system integration, the professional performing the work often needs to install resources compatible with the system being integrated, generating a great deal of work and consequently delaying business generation and productive processes for a company.

The possibilities offered by the use of APIs provide software and application developers with the ability to connect heterogeneous technologies, such as different databases, for example. Furthermore, it is possible to make specific functionalities and tools of certain applications usable in others, without causing any difficulty, as we will see in the following topic.

## **Advantages of API Integration**

![](/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Vantagens-da-Integracao-por-APIs-e1519935308310-768x727-1.jpg)

By now you should be convinced that **Application Programming Interfaces** are very beneficial and useful, and can be used for a wide range of solutions. But what are the reasons that make integrations through APIs more interesting than other types? See some reasons below:

### **Safety**

The way APIs provide integration allows for a very secure exchange of information. This is because they create a kind of gateway, through which only a specific set of information – defined by the company that owns the application – will be available.

For example: A bank has various pieces of information in its internal systems regarding matters such as the number of accounts opened per month, customers in debt, customers who receive amounts exceeding the average income of the population, etc.

By exposing an API, third parties could create an application specifically designed to query only data from customers in debt, or only those with high balances in their accounts. This data could be very useful for credit companies when conducting risk assessments.

An **API gateway** is responsible for providing security and auditability for APIs.

[Expand your knowledge on this topic with our article on **API Management and Gateways**!](/en/API-gateway-or-API-management/)

### **Monetization through access**

Imagine being able to restrict access to your application's capabilities. Application Programming Interfaces that have some kind of access restriction are called **Private APIs.**

[Understanding Public and Private APIs can be a strategic advantage for your business. Learn more here!](/en/public-or-private-API---how-to-know/)

**API monetization** can be done by charging for access to certain types of information. As an example, we can cite the streaming company Netflix, which only allows its content to be accessed by its subscribers, for a fee.

### **Reduction in data volume**

As mentioned above, APIs are capable of performing integrations in a specific way, linking only one type of information and restricting access to others. From a system operation perspective, this can mean optimized platform performance.

The reason for this is that restricting data manipulation can lead to better use of information. The key is to avoid cluttering the system with unnecessary data.

### **Ability to audit access**

Who accessed it, when, from where, and what was queried? These are questions that an API can answer regarding how it is used. An API Gateway provides the possibility of having a detailed perspective on the data flow operating through it.

This is a significant potential benefit for companies that use APIs in e-commerce or in systems that handle high-value or highly confidential data, such as banking information.

## **APIs in our daily lives**

It would be impossible to list all the APIs that are part of our daily lives. Currently, in an increasingly interconnected world, there are more and more software programs that specifically cater to a particular niche of activity. **Application Programming Interfaces** have become key players in many of the tasks we perform every day. See some examples below.

### **Facebook / Uber**

The popularity of Mark Zuckerberg's social network makes having a Facebook account almost mandatory, doesn't it? For this reason, the Facebook API is one of the most widely used by various services seeking to log users into their business platforms. A well-known example among users of this system integration is the Uber transportation app.

Uber offers the option to register using a mobile phone number during the signup process. However, many people click the button for logging in with Facebook, which is usually already logged in on many people's computers and smartphones.

The ability to log into applications with a single username and password aims to facilitate the user experience on the platform. It functions as a single key for the user to access various services offered online. In return, Facebook obtains more data about its audience, contributing to the social network's business logic.

### **Google Maps / Hotel websites**

Imagine you own a hotel. Think how useful it would be to include a Google map on your website, allowing visitors to see the best way to reach your destination and check if the service meets their needs. Imagine your potential customer completing this entire process without leaving your page.

APIs **provide** this **system integration** with Google Maps, optimizing the user experience on a page or application. Keeping users on your page longer increases the chances of generating business, as it reduces the likelihood of your visitor straying to other content.

### **E-commerce websites / Credit card companies**

Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) can function within an e-commerce website, enabling the use of services involving banks and card payment providers. Companies like Buscapé, Google Shopping, and Decolar only exist because of APIs.

[You need to see our content about APIs and online retail!](/en/the-value-of-APIs-for-retail/)

The application submits the data provided by the user to the appropriate APIs, ensuring that it is processed securely and quickly without the user noticing the complexity of the process, making their experience more fluid and pleasant and facilitating the completion of the transaction.

### **Logging into a website using social media**

You've probably been to service pages where you could log in using social media or Gmail. This integration of personal accounts with a platform has the potential to personalize the user experience in digital environments.

Any company can benefit from this process. When **APIs** enable integration between applications, they help the company that owns the platform being accessed by the user to have access to important information about who is browsing the environment.

Social media, for example, stores information that can be useful for evaluating our consumption habits, such as age, gender, location, places frequented, etc. This information can be fundamental to a [digital marketing strategy](/en/Liferay-DXP-7.1/) and to the sales area of ​​an organization when developing personalized products for its customers, amplifying its results.

## **History of APIs**

We already know **what APIs are** and what they are used for, but where did they come from? The acronym API is very old in computing, but the **integration of systems using APIs** as we know it today, based on open web protocols, began in the United States around the year 2000, spearheaded by companies that, to this day, are global giants in technological businesses with a wide variety of objectives.

Companies like eBay, Salesforce, Amazon, and, a little later, Twitter, saw in this increased demand for the internet the chance to generate online business. The integration of interfaces was the way to enhance their respective business networks, generating opportunities for partners and customers, as we will see below.

### **Salesforce**

![](/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/imagem-API-Salesforce-768x538-1.png)

The first API was from Salesforce, which offered a sales force automation service. It provided field sales teams with a tool to make decisions related to their respective objectives. Salesforce identified customer needs to share data across their different business types.

### **Ebay**

![](/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/API-Ebay-768x307-1.png)

eBay remains one of the largest online marketplaces for buying and selling products. Its API, created in 2000, aimed to facilitate business transactions. The application standardized how other applications integrated with eBay, making it easier for partners and developers to conduct business within the eBay environment.

### **Twitter**

![](/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/API-Twitter-300x242-1.png)

While the previous examples were linked to improvements in buying and selling processes, Twitter focused its API on exploring its ability to promote communication and content sharing. Several websites, in 2006 when the application was created, used Twitter (and still use it today) as a way to share pages within the most popular microblog on the internet.
