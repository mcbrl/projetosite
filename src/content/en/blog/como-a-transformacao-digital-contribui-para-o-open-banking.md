The days when financial institutions viewed the concept of open banking with suspicion are gone. Now, instead of being seen as a threat to business, it is viewed as a fundamental and irreversible process for offering faster, more flexible, and more economical ways to meet the growing demands of increasingly discerning customers.

Consumers are demanding increasingly digital solutions. According to EY, 41% of consumers would have no problem switching financial institutions in search of a better digital experience. 

Thus, open banking is becoming a necessity for traditional banking institutions to gain the agility and focus of fintechs.

## **Much more than a simple banking app.**

The idea of ​​open banking stems from the concept of open innovation coined by Henry Chesbrough, a professor at the Center for Open Innovation at the University of Berkeley, who advocates for a more collaborative and diverse form of innovation. This involves various parties external to a company, such as customers, suppliers, research institutions, government agencies, startups, and other actors, generating value through knowledge sharing. 

In the banking sector, open innovation primarily refers to the use of public APIs that allow third-party developers to create applications and services around a financial institution.

As you can probably imagine, this concept represents a radical shift in the mindset of traditional banks, which until recently kept their customers' financial data centralized and believed that modernizing the sector was simply a matter of creating a mobile banking app. But this began to change with the implementation of various open banking initiatives that have evolved over the last decade and have been launched in recent years around the world.

According to [Central Bank (BC) regulations](https://www.bcb.gov.br/pre/normativos/busca/downloadNormativo.asp?arquivo=/Lists/Normativos/Attachments/51028/Res_Conj_0001_v1_O.pdf) , open banking is the standardized sharing of data and services through the opening and integration of systems between participating institutions. And in turn, this data includes, at a minimum:

- Information about customer service channels; 
- Products and services related to accounts, credit operations, foreign exchange operations, payment arrangement services, investments, insurance, and supplementary pension plans; 
- customer registration;
- customer transactions. 

## **The challenges of open banking**

It's time to rethink the banking sector in relation to new open, intelligent, and platform-based models. Banks and financial institutions are sitting on a goldmine of data that can provide powerful insights to enhance different banking functions and create new revenue opportunities.

Open banking suggests to traditional banks that it's time to innovate so they can absorb the benefits offered by opening their APIs and the data they've always held. It's also time to offer consumers more competitive options and deals within a rapidly growing market with the arrival of new companies. Otherwise, banks as we know them today will cease to exist. 

However, for it to function perfectly and be able to aggregate different functions, the [integration of legacy systems and a good API strategy](/en/systems-integration-and-API-management/) is fundamental. After all, this is what will allow the financial institution to maintain integration with third-party companies and applications that, in turn, offer specific services integrated with the institution.

## **Open banking and API security** 

As you already know, the interaction and communication of databases is only possible through the use of APIs, and therefore, financial institutions or third parties that use this technology should not neglect its security. Problematic, unprotected, or hacked APIs are the cause of major data breaches that can damage the image and finances of any organization.

In recent years, stricter regulations in response to personal data breaches, such as the European [GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/) and the Brazilian [LGPD](http://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/L13709.htm) , have imposed new strategies on companies to enable them to respond quickly to any threat to their systems.

Furthermore, banks and financial institutions have already realized that the evolution of open banking, driven by digital transformation, also involves the development of systems and applications that, in addition to being secure, simplify online and mobile transactions for their clients. Therefore, we can affirm that among the main benefits and contributions of digital transformation to the evolution of open banking are:

- **Security of environments and data:** Standardization and automation, where each service should have the fewest possible privileges to reduce unauthorized connections and access;
- **Security of the CI/CD process:** Integration of security verifiers for containers, as part of the process of including them in the registry.
- **Centralization of user access control and identity resources:** Strict controls using authentication mechanisms are essential factors for the security of microservices in the open banking process.
- **Introducing secure API gateways:** Security increases control and visibility over the authorization and routing process. By reducing the number of exposed APIs, banking organizations can reduce attack surfaces.
- **Automation of service and system configuration management resources.**Maintaining compliance with security policies and eliminating manual errors.
