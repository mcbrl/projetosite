*This article is an adapted translation of the original written by Saul Kaplan (“* [So Many Corporate Innovation Labs, So Little Innovation](https://medium.com/bif-speak/so-many-corporate-innovation-labs-so-little-innovation-133738ad74c2) ” *). The comments at the end are by* [*André Fernandes*](https://www.linkedin.com/in/andr%C3%A9-fernandes-65a196/) *, CTO of Vertigo Tecnologia.*

Everyone has already memorized the mantra: "innovate to survive." In a rapidly transforming world, it is imperative to be able to completely reinvent oneself in an increasingly shorter time. CEOs already understand that the ability to innovate is not a competitive advantage, but a basic condition for the relevance (and existence) of an organization.

It's the innovation frenzy.

It's not working.

The disruptive examples of Uber, Airbnb, and Netflix have already been used exhaustively; those who needed instruction have been served repeatedly. Let's "go on the offensive," "create markets," and "think outside the box."

The latest trend is *corporate* *innovation labs* , which are multiplying exponentially across organizations worldwide. Who wouldn't feel good about having one? No CEO wants to be embarrassed at the next Gartner event!

Given that they are bound to submit to the decision-making process currently in place in these organizations, at best these laboratories will produce timid additions to the current business model or proofs of concept that will be applauded as quickly as they are ignored.

After tons of confetti and countless pats on the back for the CEOs who inaugurated them with the usual ceremony and fanfare, the impeccable rhetoric about how labs bring the virtues of disruption and creativity will only result in this: photos of the CEO and his cronies on the intranet or traditional media.

This should surprise no one: innovation labs are designed, structured, funded, and oriented to produce only incremental improvements to current businesses. *As soon as the managers and leaders of the current business units seize control of the innovation lab's agenda, it will be doomed to produce only more of the same.*

Organizational inertia will cause laboratory projects to be prioritized and funded to create products, services, or marginal improvements to current business processes. This is not necessarily bad: healthy and competitive companies should behave this way. However, innovation initiatives like these will not contribute in any way to preventing these organizations from being "swallowed up" by the next Netflix in their sector.

Conclusion: Corporate innovation labs are not being created in a way that helps these companies cause disruption—nor react to it. And here are the reasons why…

#### **10 Reasons Why Corporate Innovation Labs Will Yield Irrelevant Results**

- The objectives (and independence) of innovation labs are not clear. Incremental or transformative innovation require very different approaches and organizational support;

- CEOs should consider the transformative agenda as their own. What we've noticed, however, is that as soon as the spotlight fades, CEOs cede authority to lower management levels whose performance is measured by the current business model.

- Innovation labs fall into the trap of overvaluing the production of "better mousetraps," as opposed to better business models;

- Innovations with truly relevant potential but which will likely cannibalize the current business are criticized and removed from the corporate agenda, distorting the entire purpose of the laboratory;

- Demanding financial projections for innovation projects before testing them in the real world makes sense (perhaps) only for incremental innovations, never for truly transformative ones.*Translator's note: This is hardly news; it's worth remembering the Six Sigma disaster for once-innovative companies, such as...* [*http://www.zdnet.com/article/six-sigma-killed-innovation-in-3m/*](https://medium.com/r/?url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.zdnet.com%2Farticle%2Fsix-sigma-killed-innovation-in-3m%2F));

- Corporate innovation labs tend to view new technologies through the lens of their existing business models, rather than as catalysts for entirely new ones.

- Corporate innovation labs often perpetuate the organizational myopia that prevents the organization from seeing opportunities from the perspective of customer experience;

- Corporate funds (*corporate venture funds*Innovation hubs are not innovation labs. While they may provide capital and resources to entrepreneurs, they tend to retain the most relevant assets: scalability and access to their markets. *Translator's note: the decision-making processes that guide these funds can even turn the entire initiative into a complete disaster when personal relationships and vanity—as opposed to results—are the goal of these projects;*

- Corporate innovation labs are not usually organized with the necessary freedom to create sandboxes that challenge existing processes and experiment with new ways of creating, delivering, and capturing value;

- Organizations lack talent management that develops new leaders with work experience in both core competencies and the innovation lab.*Translator's note: It is not uncommon for these particular talents, questioning by definition, to be stifled early in their careers.*).

Of course, we should always improve what we do, day after day. But incremental innovations (or *continuous improvement* , if you prefer) are not enough—life would be simpler if they were. There are times—and we live in such times—when transformations are necessary. In the 21st century, the most vital skill—for individuals or organizations—is the ability to reinvent oneself.

Still, transformation, reinvention, and disruption shouldn't scare anyone. It's possible to create conditions to explore and test completely new business models while still "pedaling" within the old business model.

An important role of the corporate innovation lab is, therefore, to focus on research and development of new business models—analogous to what traditional businesses do with R&D of services, products, and technologies for the existing business. *Detachment from the current business model and the power structure and decision-making process that supports it is imperative for the pursuit of new business models.* The corporate innovation strategy must, therefore, have distinct approaches for incremental innovation (which aligns with the current business) and transformative innovation (disruptive to the current business).

The fact that CEOs are taking innovation seriously is a positive thing. Some must be realizing that simply launching a lab as a regular business unit subordinate to current management won't be effective—which is true. As CEOs correctly perceive business model innovation as a recurring need on their strategic agenda, we will likely see more effective—and less propagandistic—approaches to innovation.

For now, however, this is what we have: many corporate innovation labs, almost no innovation produced.

Translator's comments:

What motivated me to translate the original text was my observation that Brazil is at the very beginning of this craze, while the US seems to be reaching a point of maturity. Currently, not a day goes by without private and public institutions announcing their corporate burger joints in the style of Bel Pesce, with enormous media fanfare and a total lack of purpose.

Anyone who knows these institutions from the inside knows how sacrosanct they consider their current processes and silos, and can easily see that these initiatives are driven more by vanity and empty rhetoric than by a genuine desire for transformation.

These private institutions will either remain inert to the advancement of disruptive businesses, or they will continue fighting the old battle of cultivating barriers to entry (which is bad for everyone).

Public institutions, however, will continue to serve society with the same flaws and blind to the role they should truly fulfill. While established private companies struggle to review their processes, public organizations have a deep appreciation for them that fills a bureaucrat's heart with joy. The struggle is far more arduous…

Corporate innovation labs cannot be a fancy, *high-key* rehash of staged entrepreneurship. But what do we see today? Confetti, fanfare, and…nothing more. An innovation lab that lacks the freedom to challenge existing processes, reinvent business models, and step on all corporate toes will be completely useless. *People who know the current business well and are simultaneously capable of reinventing it with complete detachment from current processes and organizational structure are an organization's most precious asset—count on them.*

With regard to corporate IT, it is urgent that it become a catalyst for change. The discourse of *digital transformation,* which unequivocally accompanies innovation labs, advocates the adoption of new processes and technologies that enable—and encourage—the creation, testing, and delivery of all digital products that support (and measure results from) the experimentation of new business models.

It is therefore a huge contradiction to choose a traditional IT *vendor as a partner for digital transformation. The technological debt of these companies is gigantic—it would be like hiring an elephant to give ballet lessons. The discourse of innovation and digital transformation has obviously already been appropriated by the usual traditional names, but it was definitely not the large traditional* *vendors* that scaled cloud computing, open-source software, DevOps, or SaaS, PaaS, IaaS models, etc. All the fundamentally transformative innovations in IT in recent years did not come from traditional IT *vendors* (on the contrary, they were fought tooth and nail by them). It is at least strange (if not quixotic) to talk about digital transformation with a *vendor* that until yesterday was trying to push an obsolete mainframe or middleware.

A change-catalyzing IT team is a fantastic asset for any organization. *After the challenging people I've already mentioned, this is the second most valuable asset an organization can have.* To get there, you need to choose partners who know what they're doing – and stay away from those who have a quota of tin cans or middleware to sell in the quartile.

Digital stage transformation only works well on the Intranet.
