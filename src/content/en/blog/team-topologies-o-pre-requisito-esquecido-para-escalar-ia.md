Investment in technology by Brazilian companies has grown consistently in recent years, driven by the race to adopt AI. Teams gained access to code co-pilots, automation platforms, and content generation tools. The expected result was simple: faster delivery to the end customer.

In practice, many technology leaders observe the opposite. The budget increases, but the delivery lead time remains stable or even worsens. The straightforward question is: if the technology is faster, why isn't the organization? The answer lies in a concept that most companies still treat as an accessory, and that's precisely what Vertigo addresses in the Team Topologies Masterclass.

## **The paradox of investing in technology that doesn't accelerate delivery.**

When a technology team reaches 50, 100, or 500 people, the bottleneck ceases to be the capacity of each tool and becomes how work flows between teams. AI makes this evident: co-pilots accelerate code writing, but delivery to the end customer still involves integration, deployment, testing, and dependencies between squads, and none of these steps accelerate simply because the code arrived faster. 

The effect is a bottleneck that shifts location without disappearing. Before, the code was the slow point. Now, production is. This is the shift that Vertigo has observed in large-scale technology companies, and it becomes more visible each month as the expectation of speed increases along with investment.

## **The three symptoms of structural blockage**

Before any redesign, it's worth identifying how this blockage manifests itself in the day-to-day operation. Vertigo maps three symptoms that tend to appear together, regardless of the sector or company size.

### **Squads that have become dependent on handoffs**

Every delivery involves handoffs between departments, and each handoff consumes coordination time that doesn't generate direct value for the customer. Coordinating these dependencies can consume the equivalent of a full-time professional per month, and the problem tends to grow along with the number of squads.

### **Platform without a clear owner.**

Without an internal platform with a defined operating model, each team ends up reimplementing its own deployment, observability, and security pipeline. The platform, which should accelerate delivery, becomes just another silo within the organization.

### **AI accelerating the wrong part of the chain**

The adoption of AI usually begins at the most visible point: writing code. However, coding faster doesn't solve the problem of getting that code into production. The bottleneck shifts, and the result is the worst of both worlds: fast commits and slow deliveries.

## **Why organizational structure has become a prerequisite for scaling AI.**

Given these three symptoms, it becomes clearer why any AI, modernization, or platform initiative enters a cycle of rework when the team topology is not defined. It is from this diagnosis that Vertigo structured its Team Topologies **[approach](/en/agents-insurers-team-topologies/)** , a practical model for redesigning squads, platforms, and enabling teams. At the heart of the model are clear boundaries between teams, well-defined interaction modes, and the management of each squad's cognitive load, so that the organizational structure follows the flow of value, and not the other way around.

One of the effects of this redesign is a common vocabulary between engineering, product, and business, which makes decisions about architecture, prioritization, and investment in AI meaningful for all areas. Without this structure, each team interprets priority differently, and coordination ends up consuming time that should be dedicated to delivery.

## **What changes when the topology is redesigned?**

Redesigning the organizational structure produces effects that appear both in the operation and in the financial results of AI adoption. These are the same indicators that Vertigo monitors with companies that apply the model.

### **Less coordination, more autonomy for stream-aligned teams.**

Teams organized by value stream (stream-aligned) gain autonomy to deliver domain features without relying on constant cross-approvals, reducing the cognitive load of each squad.

### **Internal platform with growing adoption.**

A well-defined platform, supported by platform teams and enabling teams, acquires its own owner, scope, and indicators, which increases organic adoption among product teams.

### **Less rework, less wasted tokens on AI.**

Less rework means fewer code generation and review cycles with AI, which reduces token consumption and makes it easier to justify the AI ​​investment based on the cost avoided per person.
