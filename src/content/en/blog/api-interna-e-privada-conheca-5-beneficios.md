[API](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/parte-1-a-economia-das-apis-ja-esta-aqui-voce-esta-pronto/) It's the hot topic right now, and many companies are benefiting from it, it's true, but some feel uncomfortable making services and data available via APIs to external partners or third-party developers. One way to start evaluating the benefits of an API-based business strategy is to begin using APIs internally.
Companies like Google, Facebook, Twitter, Netflix, and Decolar, whose APIs were largely responsible for their growth and success, are gaining more and more space in the corporate world (we must remember that these companies have both private and public APIs).
With an eye on the advantages of using APIs, such as customization, optimization, and integration – aiming to leverage business – companies have begun investing in the use of internal APIs or Private APIs. This type of API allows integration between the organization's legacy systems, without the need for public exposure of its services or data.
In this article, we'll explore 5 benefits of having an internal or private API:

## 1 – Collaboration and Communication

Private APIs can offer substantial benefits in terms of efficiency and productivity. A study by McKinsey Global estimates that companies can increase their productivity by 20% to 25% by improving internal collaboration, a strategy that is entirely feasible with the use of APIs. Using APIs across the organization means greater shared awareness of data models, processes, and company resources. Through API management, greater clarity in data interpretation is also possible, allowing for the extraction of consistent data to be used when analyzing business operations, process optimization, and employee behavior.

## 2 – Improvement of internal operations

APIs allow data to be distributed and shared across multiple channels and systems within your organization, generating a significant improvement in internal operations by ensuring agility and making information available in a practical and functional environment, providing a comprehensive overview of the business.
As companies adopt private APIs to support internal processes, they sometimes discover opportunities to restructure and modernize their businesses. The key concept behind this modernization is that by breaking down business functionalities and services into blocks, it's possible to create new business value chains by composing services, data, and functionalities into new configurations – reinventing their businesses and processes. You will build the architecture according to your objective, market moment, or the behavior of your workgroups.

## 3 – Infrastructure rationalization

An organization can be both significantly impacted and benefited by the use of APIs, depending on its business objectives. Regarding the internal IT side, the previously bloated and slow infrastructure, due to the use of monolithic solutions and the maintenance of large implementation and customization teams, can now give way to a lean infrastructure with agile and highly scalable teams. With private cloud solutions, it's possible to allocate infrastructure resources according to demand with minimal effort and interaction. And APIs will be present in this transition.

## 4 - Flexibility

Think of APIs like LEGO, that toy with interlocking blocks that you may have played with before. Similar to the toy, APIs allow you to add or remove functionality from a system in a very flexible way, just like interlocking blocks.
This flexibility allows for the timely addition of new systems or services, or the replacement of older solutions. For example, it's possible to use a service that accesses a financial system to feed a tax solution; cross-reference sales reports to retrieve information from the company's Marketing and Trade departments; and so on.

## 5 – Analytical View

As the market changes, so do business requirements. APIs help support these changing business requirements by supporting and facilitating data migration, making it available for consumption by new applications and services almost instantly.
This ease of exposing data allows the company to identify problems in a business operation and even opportunities. Another advantage of adopting an API is the clear visualization, through graphs – which can expose and process data in real time – (Big Data), showing what is happening in our connected systems. It is easier to manage individual components. We can monitor response times, uptimes, and server load, and share analyses that are important for business level and market repositioning strategies.

## Conclusion

As you've noticed, there are numerous benefits to using an internal and private API. Considering the benefits mentioned earlier, private APIs may very well be the best way for some companies to begin their API strategies. API management tools and specialists can help identify the best path for integration and influence the design of a strategy that encourages commitment from the entire business operation. Several companies already adopt this concept; talk to a specialist to understand the best way to navigate and benefit from implementing an API strategy for your business.
