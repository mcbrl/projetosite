## **Are API and SOA the same thing?**

Lately, forums and social networks have been the stage for numerous discussions about the convergence between SOA and API. All of this is apparently linked to the launch of... [Magic Quadrant for Application Services Governance](https://www.gartner.com/doc/reprints?id=1-2DCDLCL&ct=150409&st=sb)According to a Gartner report, the term "Application Services Governance" is "the union of SOA governance technology with API management." Here at Vertigo, we approach these concepts as distinct but ultimately coherent, each with its own mission and focused on different objectives.
In order to finally settle this issue, we decided to prepare this post that will explore the different aspects of API and SOA. See below:

## **What are the definitions of SOA and API?**

SOA (Service Oriented Architecture) is a methodology that can lead to an architectural style. Contrary to what many believe, SOA is not a product, solution, or technology, but rather a methodology based on patterns for creating a fully integrated enterprise software framework capable of providing a rapid response to business changes.
This approach provides the principles and guidelines needed to transform a complex and inflexible application or set of IT resources into integrated and streamlined resources that can be modified to support business goals or other initiatives.
An API (Application Programming Interface) is a set of programming standards and instructions that provides access to a previously established piece of information. By making its API publicly available, a company allows other developers easier access to its products or information, facilitating the creation of other applications and even new businesses based on existing ones (click here to read about how). [Monetize your API](https://blog.vertigo.com.br/estrategia-de-monetizacao-das-apis/)).

## **How do they differ?**

The main differences between SOA and API are related to governance and scope. SOA is a methodology/framework that will be used by the IT department to provide solutions to other areas of the company. It focuses on which systems will be part of the technology infrastructure, which areas will be able to access which information, programming language, information flow between areas, application integration, and more.
API initiatives are more specific and focused on the development and use of certain applications or activities. APIs can be REST or SOAP, depending on the level of agility and security you need to expose that information. If APIs are gathered in a portal, this allows developers, as well as business partners, to search for the APIs they need, request access to them, and use them as needed to streamline certain activities, automate processes, and provide visibility into a specific stage of the process; the possibilities are almost endless.
Thus, as with any SOA initiative, internal APIs need to be well-planned and designed, have a level of policy and operational control, and be effectively monitored and governed. Only in this way will they be truly successful.

## **Conclusion**

**SOA**
SOA is typically linked to enterprise service management. This management includes everything from the design and deployment of the service to its replacement with another, that is, the entire lifecycle of that service or system.
**API**
If we analyze the world of APIs, we will see that it conceptually contains aspects of SOA, but focused on specific needs.
