Spreadsheets were born as a solution. Decades later, they have become one of the main obstacles to the operational efficiency of banks, fintechs, credit unions, and other institutions in the Brazilian financial sector. But this article is not about the problem. It's about what comes after it: how institutions that have decided to overcome this dependence operate with greater agility, less risk, and greater regulatory responsiveness, and how this path can be followed in a structured, secure way with measurable impact.

Modernizing the financial cycle is, today, one of the most direct levers of operational efficiency available to CFOs and CTOs in the sector. And the starting point, almost always, is exactly where most institutions still are: in the shared filing system that nobody wants to replace, but which no longer supports the volume, complexity, or regulatory requirements of the present.

## **Why the reliance on spreadsheets persists and what it really costs.**

Before moving on to solutions, it's worth defining the scenario precisely. The financial sector doesn't maintain spreadsheets by accident. There are concrete structural reasons: legacy systems that don't cover all operational needs, gaps between ERPs and banking platforms that are filled with scattered files, and processes built around key people whose tacit knowledge has never been documented. Each of these reasons is legitimate. The problem is that, over time, they cease to be justifications and become risks.

### **The cost that doesn't appear in the report.**

Human error in spreadsheets is structural, not occasional. A formula with an incorrect reference, an overwritten cell, consolidated data from an outdated version of the file. In regulated environments, these errors are not limited to operational tasks; they affect reports sent to the Central Bank, audits, and financial statements. Furthermore, the rework that sustains these processes consumes hours of qualified professionals on tasks of low strategic value—hours that could be dedicated to analysis, decision-making, and innovation.

### **Regulatory exposure that grows silently.**

Spreadsheets do not offer automatic audit trails, granular access control, or reliable versioning. This means that institutions that rely on them for critical processes operate with permanent regulatory exposure, in an environment where the requirements of Open Finance, the LGPD (Brazilian General Data Protection Law), and Central Bank regulations are constantly growing. Article **[52 of Law 13.709/2018](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm)** provides for fines of up to 2% of gross revenue, capped at fifty million reais per infraction. More than the fine, however, is the opportunity cost: strategic projects awaiting consolidated data, products reaching the market late, and credit decisions based on information that no longer reflects reality.

## **How modernization transforms this scenario.**

The counterpoint to this situation is not an abrupt replacement of the entire infrastructure. It is a progressive modernization, which begins with a precise diagnosis of the most critical processes and evolves in a structured way, with a visible impact at each stage. Institutions that have followed this path operate with a fundamentally different operational reality.

### **Financial cycle automation with API integration.**

Accounts payable and receivable (AP/AR) automation, when properly implemented, eliminates manual intervention in repetitive steps and connects systems that currently rely on spreadsheets as intermediaries. **[API Management](/en/Kong-Inc.-and-the-insurance-sector%3A-the-role-of-APIs./)** platforms orchestrate integrations between ERPs, banking systems, and internal modules with security, traceability, and centralized control. The result is an operation with automated workflows, reliable data, and scalability without a proportional increase in staff. Reconciliations that used to take days now take minutes. Manual entries cease to be a source of risk.

### **Conformity as a consequence of architecture, not as an additional effort.**

A modern architecture treats compliance as a native design requirement. Auditable logs, granular access control, centrally applied security policies, and consent processes integrated into operational flows. With this foundation, compliance with [**Open Finance**](/en/Accounts-payable-and-receivable%3A-7-bottlenecks-draining-the-Brazilian-financial-sector./) , the LGPD (Brazilian General Data Protection Law), and Central Bank regulations no longer requires parallel compliance projects but becomes a natural consequence of how the operation was designed. Each new regulatory cycle represents a controlled adaptation, not an emergency project.

### **Real-time visibility as a management advantage.**

Integrated dashboards, powered by APIs that connect financial, banking, and operational management systems in real time, replace static reports generated with a delay. With continuously updated cash position data, the CFO gains the clarity needed to make quick and informed decisions. The difference between deciding with today's data and deciding with data from three days ago, in a highly volatile market, is often the difference between anticipating a risk and reacting to damage that has already occurred.
