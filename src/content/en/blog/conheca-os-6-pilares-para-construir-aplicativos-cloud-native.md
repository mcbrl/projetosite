Cloud Native Transformation is a set of digital approaches aimed at helping organizations achieve **greater speed and competitiveness in the modern world** . Essentially, "cloud native" refers to how to design, build, and operate applications on a dynamic infrastructure that leverages all the advantages of cloud computing. Cloud native architectures generally focus on task and provisioning automation, [microservices](/en/What-are-microservices/) , containers and their orchestration, as well as various dynamic infrastructure management models.

A cloud-native corporate IT solution doesn't necessarily operate in the cloud – an organization can bring typical cloud practices to a private data center. Cloud-native applications leverage the qualities of this model (whether in the cloud or not) and drive new open-source tools and architectural concepts – since ease of experimentation is a typical and even non-negotiable factor.

Transitioning your [traditional IT](/en/Traditional-IT-can-be-an-enemy-of-digital-transformation./) to a responsive, observable, and resilient Cloud Native IT solution brings several benefits, such as:

– A culture of shared responsibility for deliverables;
\- High [observability](/en/Observability-in-the-process-of-new-business-evolution./) the behavior of systems;
– Continuous improvement models;
– Relentless pursuit of automating repetitive tasks;
– Autonomous and multidisciplinary teams;
– Radical simplification of configuration management (“death” of CMDB);
Risk management without negatively impacting productivity.

Cloud-native infrastructure operations position themselves as a platform provider. *self-service* For product teams (application developers), this model offers reduced risk associated with changes because it makes it possible – and even preferable – for product teams to experiment with scenarios more quickly while building solutions with the most appropriate level of abstraction. Organizations operating in cloud-native mode have absorbed DevOps practices and culture and deliver reliable software continuously; therefore, change decisions have less impact and don't need to be made with as much bureaucracy.

However, to be successful in transforming corporate IT, it's important to develop and implement the right strategy. With that in mind, we will discuss six pillars that need to be part of the Cloud Native Transformation. Check them out:

### **1- Planned transition
**

For any transformation to be effective, it is essential to have an action plan first. After all, while new technologies are exciting, they can also be daunting. If not well planned, even beneficial technologies can be misused, leading to frustration and abandonment. 
It's necessary to resist the temptation to dive in headfirst before aligning your mission, vision, and teams with your aspirations for Cloud Native. To do this:

It is essential to involve leadership, partners, and customers;
– Present your plans and findings;
– Assemble the right team and work together to break down your cloud migration journey into phases;
Divide these phases into development projects, sprints, and actions;
– Set clear expectations and collect feedback frequently;
– Don't underestimate the importance of working on organizational culture – there is absolutely no chance of success without giving this factor its due importance;
– Plan time for experimentation – although the desired future should be clear, how to achieve it is a rudder that is adjusted with each sprint. Cloud Native transformation is not futurology, and this process explicitly demands a good deal of experimentation, as the biggest challenges are always contextual;

### **2- From Silos to DevOps**

Typically, traditional IT silos have different management structures, tools, communication methods, vocabulary, and, as a rule, many negative incentives, which hinders the emergence of mission-oriented improvement initiatives and the implementation of application development.

Despite a preference for a more agile methodology, application development in traditional IT is still commonly organized into the following silos: 

- Software development;
- Quality assurance and testing;
- Database administration;
- IT Operations;
- Project management;
- System administration;
- Deployment management.

To make matters worse, IT operations are subdivided into teams and silos that also don't understand each other well: security, operating systems, storage, network, and monitoring.

It's no longer news that [DevOps](/en/What-is-DevOps/) is a methodology and organizational structure aimed at implementing a culture, processes, and tools that enable continuous delivery and improvement models. The existence of these silos becomes irrelevant, as product teams begin to deal with self-service platforms and automated pipelines for building, testing, and delivering software.

Delivering value becomes the mission of Corporate IT, and constant friction becomes a thing of the past. This leads to a greater focus on frequently releasing high-quality products and replacing cumbersome procedures and unnecessary bureaucracy.

### **
3- Migrate from Water Scrum Fall to Continuous Delivery**

Many development teams are still immersed in what is called Water Scrum Fall: a hybrid approach to the process management cycle, combining the concepts of Waterfall and Scrum. This is curious because both methodologies are contrasting and can cause problems for a project. The result of each update does not become a production-level deliverable because the organization, as a whole, is not integrated.

To develop a Cloud Native application and obtain its structural benefits, a **complete shift to** [**Continuous Delivery**](/en/benefits-of-having-a-continuous-delivery-conveyor-belt/) is necessary . In this methodology, applications are automatically released multiple times a day, which facilitates the build, testing, and delivery of software releases, encouraging experimentation and continuous improvement – ​​the essence of DevOps culture.

By building product development pipelines that automate code integration and testing, the teams' only concern is whether deploying the application with the new changes makes sense for the organization's business.

### **4- Leaving the Monolithic System Behind**

Monolithic systems rarely function properly when migrated to the cloud. After all, deploying a monolithic system is closely tied to a static and continuous infrastructure, which is incompatible with the instantaneous and elastic structure of a cloud application.

Since a monolithic system is not well-suited for receiving good support in Cloud Native environments, it is necessary to organize and transform it into microservices that will then be well-received and stored securely and functionally.

### **5- Designing a service architecture**

Cloud Native architecture is commonly seen as service-based, and these services are usually implemented as a collection of cloud services or [APIs](/en/What-is-API-Understand-it-in-a-simple-way./) . However, while these concepts are easily understood, many developers still have a strong tendency to create tightly coupled applications with no user interface binding. To effectively leverage the benefits of the cloud, **a Cloud Native application must have supporting functions that can be accessed independently**.

When developing a cloud application, it must be built to interact with complex, disparate, and widely distributed systems capable of supporting various loosely coupled applications not locked into data. These services can be combined into composite applications that remain flexible and scalable.

### **6- Decouple and decompose the data**

It's not enough to simply decompose your monolithic system into microservices; it's also essential to decouple your data model. If a development team has the freedom to be autonomous but still needs to deal with a single database, there's a barrier to innovation. After all, if the data is strongly tied to a single application, it won't be well-suited for the cloud.

It's necessary to decouple the data for the same reasons we know it's better to decompose application functions into services: the effort to separate the data will be amply rewarded with the... **ability to store and process them in any cloud instance**.

### **Conclusion**

Cloud Native transformation requires investment in a new way of thinking about technology with new development paradigms. However, many conventional concepts remain necessary, such as good design and automated testing.

One important lesson is that **microservices should be prioritized** , even if they initially result in more extensive application development. The long-term efficiency gains make this the smartest investment your team can make, even if it means a temporary increase in budget.
