For organizations in the financial sector, especially those that still have a more traditional mindset, the challenges are many and require a cultural shift. Consumers now have so many options that, however solid the institutions may be, they will need to surpass themselves every day to remain relevant. And, in this scenario, those who adopt Digital Transformation will have a head start!

One example of this paradigm shift is the much-discussed Open Banking! According to the president of the Central Bank, Roberto Campos Neto, regulation will begin in November 2020 under the name Open Finance, since the system will encompass all financial products.

[The Open Banking](/en/How-digital-transformation-contributes-to-open-banking./) regulations create an environment conducive to the emergence of new inclusive, competitive, secure, and customized service solutions. It is an important step in the digitalization process of the system, promoting greater and better access for the public and businesses to financial services and products.

With the constantly changing regulatory environment, there is a need to adapt to more modern forms of payment, resulting in fierce competition. In other words, financial services companies need to modernize their technology and adjust their processes to provide the experience their customers are demanding, developing lightweight and flexible solutions with modular integration features that allow for rapid integration.

More and more Fintechs, for example, have emerged exploring digital services and proving themselves as better alternatives for customers: Nubank is one of these companies; Banco Original was born in the digital age and offers all its products without a physical presence; Banco Next is a Bradesco initiative seeking to retain customers and attract digital consumers.

However, technology alone is not enough. It's clear that companies are at different stages, but generally, the lack of leadership that practices [modern IT](/en/Traditional-IT-vs.-Modern-IT%3A-The-main-changes-in-relation-to-continuous-delivery./) is the main problem.

In this article, we will list six steps that financial institutions need to consider to bring Digital Transformation into their daily operations. Happy reading!

### **1\. Build a competitive advantage**

Financial institutions need customer-centric innovation to remain competitive. The architecture of their solutions should:

- To have dynamic scalability;
- Fault resistance;
- Quick adaptation;
- Connectivity with a wide variety of devices, data, and systems.

Traditional IT systems offer little value, are complex, and expensive to operate and maintain. Outdated systems force the team to spend an excessive amount of time on maintenance and basic operations—time that could be spent developing innovative ways to add value to the company.
An interesting finding is that open source solutions have long been cornerstones of innovation in many companies, but their advantages are particularly applicable to financial services.

### **2\. Implement continuous cloud delivery.**

To begin the Digital Transformation journey in financial institutions, adopting the use of technologies and models is essential. *cloud-native* It is one of the main pillars for success in this process. Containers, infrastructure as code, automation, provisioning, orchestrators, and microservices are some of the tools for agile, responsive, observable, and resilient IT.
There is a focus on implementing a culture of shared responsibility for deliverables, greater visibility into system behavior, continuous improvement models, and the relentless pursuit of automation. All these actions aim to reduce complexity by isolating applications from the underlying infrastructure and helping development teams work with minimal configuration and reduced management time.
Software build, testing, and release delivery pipelines are only part of the solution. Furthermore, cloud-native transformation significantly reduces the number of CIs maintained by traditional formal processes.

### **3\. Use APIs as a competitive advantage.**

The use of [API](/en/systems-integration-and-API-management/) It's much more than just ticking off a to-do list for your digital development process. A modern API strategy leverages the benefits of mobile, social, and cloud technologies. In other words, it fosters the creation of new businesses.
Effective API management is a key component in the development of Open Banking, enabling the integration of financial services into different digital customer journeys and reducing the information gap between service providers, thus fostering the emergence of new business models and new forms of relationships between institutions and their customers and partners.
Open Banking is a business model that works differently: the company provides API-based interfaces so that other organizations can create applications that add value to the business's services. This can be particularly challenging for financial institutions that have a long history of mergers and acquisitions or are burdened by legacy systems.

### **4\. Utilize open identity management standards.**

Customers need access to a range of digital solutions provided by various providers within digital channels. This problem can be solved by establishing open identity standards such as OpenID so that APIs can be accessed. Customers can then authenticate their login only once, through an identity provider, without having to repeat the process each time they log in.
Open identity standards are part of a broader open-source framework that provides companies with the freedom to use software of their choice. Furthermore, this feature allows financial services organizations to securely offer their products through new channels and partners.
It is also necessary to consider the adoption of [DevSecOps](/en/Devsecops-and-its-main-performance-indicators./), aiming for application security and security infrastructure involved in the project from its inception, within an agile development approach.
The DevSecOps culture places security practices not only at the end of the software development lifecycle, but at all stages of the process, thus ensuring a secure and smooth workflow from end to end.

### **5\. Adopt DevOps to transform your IT.**

DevOps culture is a combination of development and operations that creates proximity between these two IT systems to facilitate collaboration and communication between teams. Therefore, adopting a DevOps approach is a crucial step in the Digital Transformation process.
Implementation can be challenging, but bringing DevOps to your financial company means:

- Automation agility;
- Modernization of organizational culture;
- Encouragement for experimentation;
- Transparency in decision-making;
- Recognition for greater trust and cooperation between teams.
- Improved application quality and performance.

### **6\. Use microservices to modernize existing applications and develop new projects.**

We know it's difficult to let go of those monolithic systems; after all, they've worked so far, but at what cost? Monolithic structures don't support cloud-native applications, they have slow and inconsistent experiences that can't encompass multiple devices, and they can manifest prolonged general downtime due to failure at a specific point in the application. In other words, this type of architecture delivers inefficient workflows.
Os [microservices](/en/Microservices%3A-10-new-security-challenges/) They reduce the time required for building, maintaining, and updating systems. Therefore, this allows any development team more opportunities to customize unique experiences for each end user. Other advantages worth mentioning:

- Faster deployment with reduced delivery time;
- Faster recovery from failed deployments;
- Reduced risk of downtime and interruptions due to deployment failures.

### **Conclusion**

In the financial sector, Digital Transformation aims to collect and analyze information that will assist in decision-making, strategy development, and the selection of investments that yield good returns, among other things.
Therefore, it is essential that financial companies seek to evolve their technologies in order not to lose market share, meet new customer demands, and achieve their objectives, including profitability and sustainable growth. To do this, you can:

- To lead and execute projects using agile practices;
- Adopt processes and tools that facilitate and encourage the identification of projects and employee collaboration for innovation;
- To propel the entire company towards digital growth.

Ultimately, a true Digital Transformation requires the adoption of new technologies and new working methods, as well as a commitment to experimentation and innovation.
