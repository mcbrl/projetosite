When I first heard about Kong, I confess that my first reaction was to think of the character from the King Kong movies. Like most people, the association was immediate and sparked my curiosity about this powerful protagonist in the world of API Gateways.

During this first contact with Kong, I was puzzled by the fact that my initial understanding conflicted with my previous experiences, where services were deployed and centralized on a SOA service bus. This type of architecture was predominant in the market for many years and is responsible for the integration and communication between services in a distributed environment.

It aims to be an intermediary layer between services, routing requests, transforming data, enforcing security, and managing transactions. In addition to these points, the SOA architecture supports a large number of different protocols and message formats, enabling interoperability between services.

However, Kong had a different proposition: to be a lightweight API Gateway, unlike SOA-based bus products. Its focus was to offer a targeted solution for exposing and managing APIs, acting as an entry point at the edge of an organization's environment. This facilitated the exposure, security, and monitoring of all APIs.

Its capabilities are impressive, as it can handle a large volume of traffic and provide features such as authentication, authorization, caching, and load balancing in a simplified way.

Over time it became clear to me that Kong is a simplified and cheaper solution compared to a traditional SOA service bus.

While SOA buses can be more comprehensive and complex, Kong focuses primarily on API-related needs, offering a lighter and more targeted solution for managing them.

This makes [Kong](https://materiais.vertigo.com.br/infografico-diferenciando-gateway-de-api-e-service-mesh) a more suitable, easy-to-maintain, and cost-effective option for projects with specific API requirements that prioritize transaction performance.

## But what exactly is an API Gateway?

Imagine a vast business park within a gated community, with numerous buildings housing different companies. However, to access these companies, one must pass through a main gate where security teams verify your identity and permissions to access the buildings inside the business park.

In addition, security guards check if you are complying with pre-established rules, such as entering with an appropriate vehicle (for example, a car instead of a truck). If you do not comply with the rules, your entry will be denied.

If you comply with the rules, the gatekeeper will authorize your entry and provide instructions on how to reach the desired building and how to return to the exit gate.

This is exactly what an [API Gateway](https://materiais.vertigo.com.br/ebook-esb-versus-gateway-de-api) does: check permissions and route requests to the corresponding internal services. This function can be compared to your visit to one of the condominium buildings in this example:

![](/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/kong_vertigo_1.jpg)

Now that we understand what an API Gateway is and its function within an organization's environment, let's understand Kong better.

### Kong: A high-performance API Gateway supported by customizable components.

Kong is a high-performance API Gateway, capable of acting as the entry point to a Kubernetes cluster, ensuring security and traffic control. This product also boasts a huge offering of **customizable components** in its online store that can help solve various problems.

Below are some examples of scenarios that demonstrate the benefits offered by these components integrated into Kong:

- Preventing excessive uploads: This prevents anyone from uploading a large file to your API, avoiding excessive resource consumption and ensuring that only permitted uploads are performed.

- Preventing fraudulent votes: It prevents someone from voting multiple times in favor of a preferred participant on a TV show, ensuring the integrity of the voting process and avoiding undue manipulation.

- Protecting against bot attacks: Preventing bots from bombarding your service with requests until it becomes inaccessible, providing a layer of security that keeps your service running smoothly.

- Access control: Ensuring access to services is granted only to those who are entitled to it, through proper authentication and authorization.

- Data collection: Allow the collection of service usability information, providing valuable data that can support continuous improvement and the success of your business.
