In the pursuit of efficiency and productivity, more and more companies are adopting DevOps in their operations. Part of this result is achieved through the use of containers, and for this, Kubernetes is the fundamental tool in orchestrating these environments.

Therefore, in this article we will explore the relationships between DevOps in the corporate world, agile culture, the role of containers in [CI/CD pipelines](/en/What-is-a-CD-ROM-pipeline-Understand-it./) , and the integration of Kubernetes into the DevOps pipeline.

We will take an analytical look at how these solutions are structured and from there understand how they can work together, bringing satisfactory results to your company.

## Implementing DevOps goes beyond cultural change.

Before DevOps achieved its current popularity, development and operations teams worked in silos, meaning they were isolated from the rest of the organization as a whole, with independent processes, goals, and tools.

Implementing DevOps correctly helps resolve bottlenecks and assists different teams in understanding each other's processes. This type of change is cultural, as it forces processes and workflows to overlap and function together.

However, it's worth emphasizing that **cultural changes alone are not enough** to solve all the problems that exist with teams working in isolation, because even if the team adopts the cultural change, issues of tools and infrastructure remain.

To solve these technical problems, DevOps teams use pipelines that incorporate automation and configuration, with version control systems used by the development team. This integration allows for code testing and review.

By adopting this practice, we can ensure that processes align instead of competing with each other, as well as eliminate the need for bottlenecks due to waiting times. Pipelines create visibility throughout the software development lifecycle, and this visibility facilitates the identification and resolution of problems from the initial phase.

But what happens when teams become restricted by their tools and DevOps starts being implemented in the cloud?!

## Kubernetes is ideal for migrating your DevOps infrastructure to the cloud.

Kubernetes is a great ally to help transition infrastructure to public clouds, such as [AWS, and is present in Vertigo solutions](/en/partnerships/aws/).

When a pipeline is implemented, it can streamline your company's processes; however, many of these processes can be patched together using a variety of independent tools. To integrate these tools, containerization is a very efficient solution.

With containerization, DevOps teams have the ability to divide their toolchains or individual functionalities into microservices, which can be separated and run independently of the environment. This allows for easy replacement of tools and modifications without interrupting the rest of the pipeline.

Kubernetes allows you to organize your infrastructure as code. Your applications and tools can be deployed to Kubernetes, as well as access controls and databases. Therefore, it's also possible to manage environment configurations as code.

Instead of running a script every time you need to deploy a new environment, you can provide Kubernetes with a source repository containing configuration files.

Furthermore, the code can be managed using version control systems, just like your applications under development. This makes it easier for teams to define and modify the infrastructure and configurations, allowing changes to be sent to Kubernetes to be handled automatically.

## DevOps and Kubernetes enable zero downtime.

Kubernetes' recurring updates and automated rollback capabilities allow you to update or fix bugs in your application without interrupting its operation. In monolithic systems, you need to interrupt your application's functionality to perform updates, but through a DevOps culture, you can use Kubernetes to shift traffic across available services, updating one cluster at a time.

Like many agile methodologies, DevOps seeks to improve the software development lifecycle. For this type of approach, pipelines rely heavily on collaboration, communication, integration, and automation.

Using containers and microservices accelerates development and allows for small-scale modifications. This ensures that you can update your application without making it inoperable, resulting in zero downtime.
