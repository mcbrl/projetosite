The financial sector began its Digital Transformation very cautiously. Currently, almost all banks use the cloud in some way, but few are exploring the full potential of this technology. To offer the benefits of hybrid cloud solutions, it is necessary to balance immediate business requirements with the provision of a consistent enterprise infrastructure.
Hybrid cloud will be an important solution for many companies, as industry organizations rank security, privacy, and compliance issues as the most concerning when running applications in the public cloud.
Therefore, the main motivations for modernizing your IT infrastructure are to increase control over the use of IT resources and to gain the speed and flexibility needed to meet business requirements.
As the industry continues to focus more on data privacy and compliance issues, organizations are turning to the private cloud. This rapid increase serves as the foundation for this cloud model, which is expected to become the industry standard in the coming years.
A hybrid cloud is the combination of more than one public or private cloud. Using tools and technologies, it's possible to achieve unified management, orchestration, integration, and portability of workloads.
With this infrastructure, financial institutions can deploy unified business applications, coordinating tasks performed in different environments. After all, continuous interoperability, scalability, and flexibility are some of its main characteristics.
Continue reading the article and discover the main benefits of hybrid cloud infrastructure for banks and financial institutions. Happy reading!

## Innovation with autonomy

Hybrid cloud infrastructure promotes the use of a broad set of technologies both on-premise and in public clouds. With this approach, teams deliver an enhanced customer experience, create and sell differentiated offerings, improve operations, and manage talent across the company using the same tools for common tasks.
Companies can also leverage application-specific features such as machine learning, facial recognition, and natural language processing.

## Synchronization and integration

The banking sector, particularly banks and institutions offering diversified solutions, has faced challenges in segmenting its workload. Hybrid cloud provides a common orchestration that connects tasks across the enterprise to create cohesive workflows, regardless of the execution environment.
Thus, they can offer better integration between business units, connecting applications to critical data sources, resulting in unified and well-informed decisions.
A solution like [**DockerEnterprise**](/en/docker/) It provides the container orchestration needed for banks that want to automatically provision or deploy servers, increase storage capacity, create virtual machines, and manage networks across multiple business units and cloud environments.

## Resilient and secure operations

Resilience is one of the main benefits of [cloud-native technology](/en/What-are-the-cloud-security-challenges-for-2021/)It helps companies respond to disruptions or downtime more quickly. However, as applications and data are migrated to the cloud, maintaining compliance and continuity in a uniform and consistent manner has become extremely important to reduce risks to data and business.
Cloud providers may offer advanced security protocols for financial services companies, but monitoring multiple servers, each with its own specifications, can become a problem.
When incorporating policies and governance into a hybrid cloud strategy, it's essential to ensure that systems, software, and employees remain compliant with architectural changes.

## Scalability

Banks have invested heavily in enterprise hardware and software development. The hybrid cloud protects and leverages existing investments while providing the foundation for modernization.
Furthermore, they can rapidly scale, increasing or decreasing computing capacity to respond more quickly to the peaks and troughs of customer demand and market changes.
