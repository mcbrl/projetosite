According to a survey by [Forrester Research](https://go.forrester.com/) , more than eight out of ten companies already run cloud solutions. However, even the leading cloud providers can experience failures and impact thousands of customers. And, to reduce the risks of downtime, nothing is better than being able to rely on multicloud, that is, an approach involving multiple cloud providers.

In this new scenario, a major challenge arises: how to manage different solutions across different clouds? Well, that's the question we'll be answering throughout this article.
Good reading!  

##
What is multicloud? 

As the name suggests, multicloud is an approach comprised of more than one service and cloud provider, whether public or private.
Thus, application and system services are delivered across multiple public and private clouds that contain a combination of diverse vendors, accounts, availability zones, regions, locations, or public data centers.
The needs of applications are what generally lead to the decision to adopt multiple clouds. As you probably already know, no cloud solution is perfect for the entire company, and by adopting multicloud, managers can choose specific services from particular clouds according to the needs of each department.
In this way, an organization can leverage the speed and capacity of a particular provider and its private cloud, as well as the resources of another cloud solution from a different vendor. Adopting multiple clouds adds value to the business, helping different departments within the company perform their work more efficiently, and avoiding excessive dependence on a single cloud provider. 

##
Advantages of a multicloud environment 

In summary, we can cite the following advantages of adopting a multicloud environment:

### Shadow IT

Shadow IT occurs when business areas purchase IT services independently, without authorization or even knowledge from corporate IT. In these cases, hardware or software deployed independently of the central IT team may require more oversight, and it may not be possible to migrate the infrastructure and data to another corporate system. Therefore, this shadow IT structure is simply integrated as part of the company's existing clouds, creating a multicloud.

### Flexibility

As mentioned above, you'll rarely find a perfect cloud solution for the entire company. However, by adopting multiple clouds, it's possible to extract the best from each one according to the needs of each department.

### Business

Many similar services are offered by various cloud providers, such as managed database services, storage, server instances, and many others. So why not take advantage of the best prices or promotions from each of them?

### Proximity

To reduce network latency, which is the response time for a request, for cloud users who are far from the data center, the company can adopt regional cloud providers that operate closer to these users physically. 
This allows the company to maintain high availability and adhere to data sovereignty laws, which are protocols that subject data to the regulations of the country where it is located.

### Failover

With multicloud environments, companies can reduce or avoid disruptions to their systems. With a failover solution, multicloud allows companies to have a backup of highly scalable and available data, workflows, and systems should the primary cloud become unavailable. 
This is highly relevant when we consider that companies must comply with local data sovereignty laws. In some cases, government or industry regulations require backup and recovery processes for all digital content.

##
Facing the challenges of managing multiple clouds

While a multicloud strategy offers significant advantages, it also has some weaknesses, including management complexity. Many companies have adopted a cloud solution but manage them autonomously, meaning they have a separate set of management tools for each cloud. 

Each management system has its own set of APIs, and as a result, management becomes a challenge from several perspectives: tasks, business processes, and security. They waste human resources, are unable to move workloads to the best platform, and sometimes pay more for their services than they should. 
So how can an organization reap the benefits that multicloud offers without being burdened by its limitations? The answer: by adopting a platform with support based on... [microservices](https://konghq.com/ebooks/microservices-multi-cloud) and containers. This is the way to facilitate the deployment of a multicloud environment and take advantage of all its benefits.

Microservices-based software development creates single-function modules with well-defined interfaces and operations. In a [microservices](/en/the-advantages-of-microservices-architecture/) and [container](/en/Truths--myths--modernization-of-containerized-applications/) architecture , each component is continuously developed and maintained separately. In simplified terms, the application becomes the sum of its components.

Due to containers' ability to package and isolate applications with their entire runtime environment, users can migrate containerized applications between clouds while maintaining all functionality intact. This gives companies the freedom to choose cloud providers based on universal standards such as uptime, storage space, and cost, without worrying about whether or not their workload will be supported due to proprietary restrictions, thus avoiding the dreaded "cloud lock-in".

In other words, by implementing a management solution built with microservices, companies will find it easier to obtain the benefits that multicloud offers, without being hampered by its limitations.
