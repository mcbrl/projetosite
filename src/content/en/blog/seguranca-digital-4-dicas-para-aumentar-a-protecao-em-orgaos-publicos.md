## Why is digital security important in public institutions?

When we talk about digital security within public institutions, we're talking about something that goes beyond data protection. In many cases, what's at stake are services that are important to the public, as well as valuable information about people, and any interruption of operations can represent a major disruption for citizens.

Recently, we had the case of the hacker attack on the Rio de Janeiro city hall system, as we saw at the beginning of the text, but in addition to that, we can also mention the attack on the [Ministry of Health's systems,](https://g1.globo.com/tecnologia/noticia/2022/01/10/ataque-aos-sistemas-do-ministerio-da-saude-completa-1-mes-nesta-segunda-o-acesso-a-informacao-ainda-e-restrito.ghtml) in which some of the information was affected.

During the period of instability that lasted just over a month, the ability to measure cases and control the Covid-19 epidemic was compromised.

Another case of a hacker attack with a digital security breach was the one that affected the [STJ's systems](https://www.stj.jus.br/sites/portalp/Paginas/Comunicacao/Noticias/04112020-Em-razao-de-ataque-cibernetico--STJ-funcionara-em-regime-de-plantao-ate-o-dia-9.aspx) , causing delays and extensions of deadlines for several cases, thus affecting the population.

Therefore, although the topic of digital security within the public sector is debated, the need for a stricter data protection policy and new measures to protect systems involving basic services that are so important to citizens is becoming increasingly clear.

## How can digital security be increased within public institutions?

Society has been adopting digital solutions more frequently, and the Covid-19 pandemic accelerated this process.

Within the public sector, many basic services for citizens have undergone a digitization process, consequently leaving some loopholes that end up being exploited by hackers.

Therefore, here are some tips to increase digital security within public institutions.

### 1 – Invest in cloud solutions

Migrating your on-premise infrastructure to the cloud is a trend that large private sector companies are already adopting; the public sector will be no different.

Therefore, cloud computing solutions should not be avoided, but rather investments should be made to make them more secure and provide greater safety and agility. In addition to cost reduction, the public sector will have the opportunity to invest in digital security.

Through solutions with [AWS (Amazon Web Services)](/en/Cloud-computing%3A-5-reasons-to-use-AWS-in-your-company./) , companies can have a more scalable cloud infrastructure to support their demands.

### 2 - Increase security through automatic backups

It's essential to have an automated backup routine within your cloud infrastructure; therefore, prioritize solutions that invest in this type of practice.

By implementing AWS, you will have a platform that supports security standards and compliance certifications that stand out in the market.

In this way, public institutions will be able to have a copy of their files and restore access to information in cases of [ransomware attacks](/en/devsecops-ransomware-protection/) or other actions that compromise the functioning of their operations.

### 3 – Train your employees to increase digital security.

Many data breaches are caused by misuse of infrastructure, with the employees themselves being responsible for compromising the information.

Through [DevSecOps practices](/en/devsecops-5-steps-to-implement/) , it's possible to provide training within teams and promote their development. No matter how good the solutions are, if the teams don't adopt the correct approach, the chances of data leaks increase significantly.

Promote educational approaches, create alerts and lectures that teach each employee to take responsibility and understand the importance of their behavior in the workplace.

### 4 – Implement the data access permissions policy.

To mitigate risks that could compromise database security, access must be very well defined, allowing only those responsible for each sector to access specific areas of the system.

Therefore, through AWS you manage access by creating policies that associate IAM identities (users, user groups, or roles). For example, a policy is an object within [AWS itself](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/pt_br/IAM/latest/UserGuide/access_policies.html) that is associated with an identity or resource to define permissions.
