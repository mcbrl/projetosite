Which of these approaches should we use in the era of digital transformation? It's not a question of which one is the best. In this article, we will compare and contrast the approaches and benefits of each of these methodologies.

With the recent introduction of ITIL 4 and the growing popularity of Site Reliability Engineering *(* SRE), discussions have heated up about how different and similar these best practices are. But which one is the most correct to use when managing services in the digital world?

Yes, this is a common question, but perhaps it's not the right question to ask. IT leaders should stop debating the merits of each of these methodologies and instead focus on the best way to develop high-performing teams that enable and accelerate their company's digital strategy.

The fact is: each of these frameworks or best practices has its place in adding value to your IT organization. Furthermore, these three methodologies share some common goals. These are:

- The introduction of a collaborative and connected culture;
- Greater focus on delivering value quickly and with quality to stakeholders;
- The ability to rely on automation to reduce waste and errors made by humans.

A 2019 survey by the DevOps Institute on *Upskilling DevOps* revealed that 66% of teams are adopting DevOps, 47% are applying ITIL, and 10% are using SRE. However, an important point to note is that many of the teams surveyed were using all three resources simultaneously. Therefore, and to guide your choices, here is some important information that will provide a more complete overview of ITIL 4, SRE, and DevOps. Let's start by detailing each of the key players involved:

## **ITIL-4**

ITIL 4 is the evolution of Axelos' service management framework. It introduces a new Service Value System (SVS), which underpins its practices on core principles now taken to another level through the publication of ITIL Practitioner Guidance. The framework facilitates alignment with DevOps and is agile through an approach that retains many of the activities of previous versions but recognizes DevOps practices such as value streams and continuous delivery.

All members of the IT organization are involved, working together to facilitate value creation through IT-enabled services. The main components of the ITIL 4 framework are involved in the service value chain, which aims to add value on demand or opportunity through these guiding principles, which involve issues such as governance and continuous improvement practices. In this case, stakeholders (customers) are receiving an IT-enabled service or product on demand or opportunity.

![ITIL 4 - SVS](/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/ITIL4_SVS.png)
An example would be: “salespeople spending more time interacting with customers” (stakeholders and opportunity); facilitated by “a remote access service that allows reliable access to corporate sales systems from salespeople’s laptops” (IT-enabled service). In other words, the focus is on service functionalities and non-functional requirements such as availability, performance, security, and maintainability.

ITIL 4 emphasizes service quality and consistency and aims to improve stakeholder satisfaction by ensuring value from the stakeholder perspective. Its guiding principles support organizations in adding value to the demands of their stakeholders, whether internal or external clients. There are 34 practices that are part of the service lifecycle.

## **SRE**

Site Reliability Engineering (SRE) is Google's approach to service management, presented in a book of the same name. It's a set of post-production practices for operating large systems at scale, focusing on operations engineering. The core function is the SRE team, whose members are software engineers performing operational functions, rather than a dedicated operations team.

The reliability of production systems and their users is supported by an engineer who applies SRE principles to manage availability, latency, performance, efficiency, change management, monitoring, emergency response, and capacity planning. They can also function as support engineers, leveraging monitoring, capacity, and automation optimization tools. The focus is centered on the non-functional requirements of availability, performance, security, and maintainability.

SREs emphasize the development of systems and software that increase the reliability and performance of applications and services. SREs also have on-call responsibilities, meaning they need to be available to provide service or support.

## **DevOps**

DevOps integrates various teams and processes in software development and delivery. The goal of DevOps is to achieve better quality while managing the appropriate speed of software and services for the business line. The methodology is aligned with Lean and Agile principles.

## **What similarities do ITIL 4, SRE, and DevOps share?**

One important similarity is that all three address the core topic of change management. But while ITIL 4 is geared towards change management governance, SRE uses the concept of "error budget," which allows changes to be made by the SRE team until the budget is "spent." DevOps teams, on the other hand, are continuously managing changes that are generally gradual in nature.

![Change Management](/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/gerenciamento-de-mudancas.png)

As you can see, all three methodologies encourage collaboration between different IT stakeholders and with business and/or product owners. Furthermore, they are all supported by a vast array of automation tools. Some claim to focus on DevOps, while others automate key processes. The automation tool landscape is complex and constantly changing due to new entrants, technologies (AI, etc.), mergers, and acquisitions. Integrations are possible but can also be challenging. Each methodology also focuses on experimentation and continuous learning.

Therefore, at the beginning of this article, we made it clear that knowing the methodologies wasn't exactly about choosing between them. The skills required for these methodologies may vary, but essentially, if you manage services in the digital world, you'll need a combination of automation and process skills, functional and flexible skills, and technical and business skills.

## **What are the main differences between ITIL 4, SRE, and DevOps?**

The main differences between the methodologies lie in the team structure, metrics and automation tools, and the methodology's fundamental belief in or adherence to a governance model.

ITIL 4 does not require team members to be part of a team. It has many applicable subprocesses and certifications available on its foundation, aimed at managing professional, strategic leader, and master levels. A key metric in ITIL 4 is meeting service level objectives. The framework provides solid governance for optimizing and improving IT service management processes and enterprise services for medium and large organizations.

SRE is a defined function with a defined title. Its main properties, as its name suggests, are the reliability of applications and services, focusing on service level objectives and indicators. Some courses to learn and understand SRE are available on Google and other websites; just be aware of those that provide SRE Foundation certifications.

DevOps team topologies vary, but the most effective DevOps teams are a single team with the same goals and metrics. Key DevOps metrics include deployment frequency and time to restore. DevOps certifications are available at basic and advanced levels, and the governance model is primarily based on self-organization.

## **When should ITIL 4, SRE, and DevOps be used?**

ITIL 4 adoption can occur at any time. Requirements from previous ITIL versions are not necessary. A key point in the process is the overall approach to creating products and services, encompassing the design, development, and construction stages. ITIL 4 also presents and manages best practices and common languages ​​for improving service availability, financial efficiency, and customer satisfaction. Furthermore, it addresses organizations and people, information and technology, partners and suppliers, and values ​​flows and processes.

SRE can be adopted by introducing an SRE engineer as a formal team member within a DevOps or Service Management team. Another avenue is adoption by organizations that lack exposure to ITIL 4 or DevOps. SRE members are also stakeholders in the design of new systems, as their knowledge of current services, products, and/or environment is leveraged when new systems are designed. Its primary use is when reliability is a stated organizational goal and the system is experiencing an increase in users, complexity, and/or the number of configuration items.

One of the main benefits of SRE teams is the creation of self-service tools and automation scripts to address the reliability and performance of applications and services, eliminating manual work. The adoption of DevOps, as well as ITIL 4, can occur at any time. The main triggers are demands for faster delivery and higher quality of software, products, and/or services for stakeholders.

One important benefit is that DevOps brings about cultural transformation, improving the speed and quality of how software is developed and delivered. It is based on Agile software development and service management techniques, and encourages the use of automation to reduce manual work by skilled individuals, thus allowing them to focus on tasks and activities that add more value.

DevOps emphasizes the reliability, maintainability, and operability of software across all team members. Let your needs guide your decision.

As we mentioned earlier, reading this text should not be interpreted as excluding all possibilities. The development and management of software products require agile techniques, focusing on the co-creation of value, in order to reduce waste. All three methodologies can coexist to align teams, meet stakeholder demands, and improve the value delivered.

One important fact is that, regardless of the methodology chosen (or combination thereof), you will be more successful if you focus on:
1) a shared vision and purpose;
2) To foster and manage a culture of care;
3) to make decisions and make them visible;
4) Define metrics and measure them before you begin, while continuing to prove the value of your efforts to stakeholders.

Finally, an important point to consider is that [digital transformation](/en/Digital-transformation%3A-are-you-ready/) is not achieved instantly in an organization. Established companies should start with best practices and methodologies suited to their needs, beginning on a smaller scale, and then learning and developing enough expertise to scale up.
